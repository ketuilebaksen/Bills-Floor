DION DAWKINS’ CRYPTIC MESSAGE SHOCKS BILLS MAFIA BEFORE RAVENS GAME!

Bills Mafia, take a deep breath, because Dion Dawkins just posted something that sent this entire fan base into a panic. “You never know when it will be gone.” That’s what he wrote. And with the trade deadline on November 10th, the Baltimore Ravens coming to town, and a lot of numbers around his contract that people are suddenly paying attention to, Bills fans are losing it.

Here’s the situation. Dion is 32 years old. He carries the second biggest cap hit on the entire roster. And his pass blocking grade has slipped to 38th among tackles this season. On paper, you can see why people’s minds went straight to a trade.

But here’s the thing, Bills Mafia. You can grade a left tackle. You can’t grade a Snowman. And by the end of this video, you’re going to see why that’s the part that matters most.

Let’s get into it.

First things first: take a breath. Because Bills fans have seen this movie before.

Go back to March of 2024. Dion posted that he was ready to move on to the next chapter of his football career. The whole city panicked. People thought he was gone. And then, just minutes later, the news broke: Dion wasn’t leaving. He was signing a three-year extension to stay in Buffalo. Even the Bills got in on the fun. The team’s own post said that Dion Dawkins had gotten them too.

And it didn’t stop there. This past April Fool’s Day, Dion posted what looked like a trade announcement sending him to the Miami Dolphins. Of all teams, Bills Mafia. The Dolphins. Fans spiraled. And of course, it was a joke.

So Dion has a long history of messing with us. He loves this fan base, and he loves to have a little fun with it.

But here’s what makes this one harder to laugh off.

During a livestream, Dion was asked about money and loyalty, and he said Bills fans be trying to cook him, and that he would leave. Then he walked it back. So now, every time Dion jokes about leaving, fans can’t totally tell where the joke ends and the truth begins.

And this time, there’s something different. It isn’t really the post. It’s the numbers.

Let’s start with the money.

Dion signed that three-year, 60-million-dollar extension, and it runs through 2027. This season, his cap hit is about 24.8 million dollars. For anyone who isn’t deep into the contract side of things, the cap hit is how much of the team’s spending limit his contract takes up this year. And that number is second only to Josh Allen on this roster.

And here’s a detail a lot of fans missed this summer: the Bills chose not to restructure his deal.

A restructure is when a team moves money around to push part of a player’s cap hit into future years. Teams do it all the time with players they plan to keep long term. Buffalo didn’t do it with Dion. And Yahoo Sports reported that, given his age and the team’s financial situation, the Bills may consider moving on after this season.

Now think about the timing. Buffalo has only around four million dollars in cap space. We’ve been talking all week about how hard it will be to add help in the secondary or at linebacker before the deadline because of that. And now a 32-year-old left tackle with the second biggest cap hit on the team posts “You never know when it will be gone,” right before November 10th.

You can see why everybody’s mind went straight to a trade.

But is he actually slipping on the field? That’s where things get really interesting.

Let’s look at his Pro Football Focus grades. PFF grades every player on every play on a scale up to 100.

As a rookie in 2017, Dion earned a 77.2 overall grade, with a 75.4 in pass blocking and an 80.5 in run blocking. From day one, this was a guy who could move people. And he’s been remarkably steady ever since. Going into this season, Dion had posted a PFF grade above 70 for seven straight seasons, and in eight of his nine years in the league. Last season, his pass blocking grade ranked seventh among all tackles in the NFL.

That’s elite consistency, Bills Mafia. Year after year, Dion Dawkins has been one of the most reliable blindside protectors in football.

Now look at this year.

His overall grade is 74.1, which ranks 14th out of 72 tackles. That’s still very good. His run blocking grade is 83.2, which ranks fourth in the entire league. That’s outstanding. But his pass blocking grade has dropped to 68.5, which ranks 38th.

So the guy who has been one of the best at protecting Josh Allen is now better at knocking people off the ball in the run game than at keeping rushers away from his quarterback.

And part of that might be the new body. Dion said he lost 45 pounds this offseason. Is a lighter Dion a better run blocker and a worse pass blocker? Maybe. It makes some sense: more athletic, quicker to the second level, more mobile on outside runs, but maybe giving up a little of the anchor against power rushers.

But let’s keep it in perspective. We’re only about 270 snaps into the season. That’s still a small sample. A couple of bad reps can drag a grade down quickly at this point in the year.

And there’s another factor we have to remember. Josh Allen escapes pressure like almost no other quarterback in this league. We just saw it in Las Vegas, when he slipped away from Maxx Crosby on third and eight and hit Keon Coleman for a touchdown. When your quarterback can turn a broken play into a big play, it can cover up some of the line’s mistakes. But it also makes it harder to judge any lineman just by looking at the tape, because some pressures that would be sacks for other quarterbacks become highlights for Josh. That cuts both ways for Dion.

And this matters a lot right now, because the Ravens are next. Baltimore comes into this game desperate. They’re outside the playoff picture, coming off a loss to Cincinnati, and a desperate defense is going to come after Josh Allen. Keeping him clean is going to be a huge part of this game, and Dion is the man on his blind side.

Before we get to the most important part of this story, smash that like button. Let’s get this one in front of as many members of Bills Mafia as possible.

So yes, on paper, there’s a case. He’s 32. He’s expensive. His pass blocking has dipped. If this were just a spreadsheet, you could talk yourself into a trade.

But Buffalo has never been a spreadsheet town.

Here’s something that’s hard for us in Buffalo to admit: not every player wants to live here. These are millionaires. They could play in a dome. They could play in a state with no income tax. They could play somewhere it’s 75 degrees in December.

So when a player chooses Buffalo, and especially when he puts his name on an extension to stay, that matters just as much as any grade. When you build a team, you need guys who want to be here. You need ambassadors.

And nobody has been a bigger ambassador for this city than Dion Dawkins.

Think about it. His nickname is literally the Snowman. He’s been saying “snow” since prep school, and then he ended up in a city famous for snow. You can’t make that up. It’s like Buffalo was always where he was supposed to be.

He’s written letters to this city. In The Players’ Tribune, he wrote one directly to Bills Mafia, and he told the bandwagon fans that the wagon was full. Then he came back and wrote another one. Those weren’t contract statements. They weren’t press releases. They were letters to his people.

And last November, Buffalo gave Dion Dawkins the key to the city, right in Niagara Square. It was in recognition of all the work he’s done with kids and families through his nonprofit, Dion’s Dreamers. The mayor said Dion represents everything that makes Buffalo special.

Go find a PFF grade for that, Bills Mafia.

And in Dion’s own words: he can never leave this community, because Buffalo is him.

That’s not a player looking for the exit. That’s a man who has made Buffalo home.

Now let’s go back to that post, because there was more to it. The message was to appreciate what you have while you have it, because you never know when it will be gone.

Everybody read that as Dion saying goodbye. But maybe that message isn’t about Dion at all.

Maybe it’s for us.

Maybe it’s a reminder to Bills Mafia to enjoy this moment. Five and one. Josh Allen in his prime. Keon Coleman breaking out. A real chance at the number one seed with the Chiefs slipping. A brand new stadium waiting for playoff games. These are special times, and they don’t last forever.

And maybe it’s for the people running this team, too. A reminder that some things matter more than a cap number.

Because you can find another left tackle. You can find a better pass blocking grade. But you can’t grade a Snowman. And you can’t replace one either.

Let’s also be realistic about what a trade would actually mean for this team right now. Buffalo is five and one. They’re a top contender in the AFC. Joe Brady has said again and again that he wants this team to grow together, to stay together, and to have each other’s backs through the good and the bad. He said what they can’t do is pit each other against each other.

Trading your starting left tackle, a team leader, the face of this franchise in the community, in the middle of a season where you’re chasing the number one seed? That would be an earthquake in the locker room. It would mean asking someone new to protect Josh Allen’s blind side during a playoff push. And Dan Orlovsky said it himself just this week: this offense can score 30 points a game, and it starts and ends with the offensive line staying healthy.

You don’t break up a line like that in October. Not when you’re this close.

What happens after the season is a different conversation. The contract runs through 2027. The Bills didn’t restructure it. There will be real decisions to make in the offseason. But right now, in the middle of this run, with Baltimore coming to town? I don’t see it. And honestly, I don’t want to see it.

So here’s my take, Bills Mafia.

I don’t think Dion Dawkins is going anywhere before the deadline. I think this is classic Dion: a little bit of fun, a little bit of drama, and a lot of love for this city. His pass blocking numbers have dipped, but his overall grade is still top 15, his run blocking is top five in the league, and it’s still early. And what he means to this team and this city can’t be measured in a cap number.

Appreciate him while you have him. That’s the real message.
