FINAL INJURY REPORT! DION DAWKINS STATUS IN DOUBT BEFORE VIKINGS GAME!

Bills Mafia, it’s almost time for Monday Night Football in Minnesota, and Joe Brady just released the final injury report. One player has been ruled out. Five more are questionable, including the man who protects Josh Allen’s blind side. And against the most aggressive blitzing defense in the NFL, that is a big deal.

But Brady also explained exactly why he trusts some of these players to go without practicing, what happens if Buffalo has to shuffle its offensive line, and the mindset he wants his team to carry into the biggest test of the season.

Let’s get into it.

Here’s the official injury report.

Joshua Palmer: out.

Dion Dawkins: questionable.
Alec Anderson: questionable.
Greg Gaines: questionable.
Jordan Hancock: questionable.
Tyrell Shavers: questionable.

So Palmer is the only player ruled out. Brady confirmed it twice: Joshua Palmer is the only one who’s out.

Let’s start with Palmer. Earlier this season, Palmer opened strong, scoring touchdowns in each of his first two games. But he struggled to keep that going. Against the Raiders, he had a 37-yard catch but later dropped a pass that hit him in the hands. Still, Dan Orlovsky said a few weeks ago that Palmer seems to show up when it matters. Now Buffalo will have to play without him on Monday night.

But here’s the good news: this receiving group has depth. DJ Moore is back and caught the record-breaking touchdown last week. Keon Coleman has been one of the best stories in the league. Khalil Shakir is still a matchup nightmare. Dalton Kincaid caught a touchdown against the Ravens. And the running backs, James Cook, Ty Johnson, and Ray Davis, have been among the most productive pass catchers out of the backfield in football. So losing Palmer hurts, but this offense has plenty of answers.

A reporter also asked about a veteran who wasn’t seen at practice. Brady said it was just a veteran rest day, part of the plan, and nothing more. He said the players involved were in a good headspace and ready to go. So no reason to panic there.

Now, the big one: Dion Dawkins.

Dawkins hasn’t practiced this week, and he’s questionable. And against Brian Flores’s blitzing defense, the left tackle position might be the most important spot on the field.

A reporter compared this to Christian Benford before the Rams game. Benford didn’t practice that week either, was listed as questionable, and ended up playing. So what goes into a decision like this, when a player doesn’t practice all week and the team still believes he can be ready?

Brady said it’s obviously not ideal. You always want your players out at practice. But he said they had a conversation with Dion on Monday. He explained that every position is a little different. He compared it to DJ Moore during the Chargers week, when Moore might have been limited on the last day of practice but was still ready.

And then he gave the key point. Brady said some of these players have played so much football that he trusts they know their assignments and can handle them. He said he felt the same way about Spencer Brown last week.

So the plan with Dion, Brady said, was simple: do what they have to do to get him to the game, keep evaluating, and play on Monday.

Brady was clear that it’s not the same for everybody. But he said there are certain positions, and certain people, who have played enough football that he believes they can go out and do their job without practicing.

And Bills Mafia, if anyone fits that description, it’s Dion Dawkins. He’s had a PFF grade above 70 in eight of his nine seasons. His run blocking ranks fourth in the entire league this year. He’s been the Snowman on Josh Allen’s blind side for nearly a decade. If anybody knows his assignment without practice reps, it’s Dion.

Then a reporter asked an important follow-up. With both Dawkins and Alec Anderson questionable, it’s two players on the left side of the line. Is it harder to evaluate when it’s two guys who play next to each other?

Brady said there’s a lot that goes into it. But he said if the starters are ready to go, they’re ready to go.

And then he explained something most fans never see. He said that in a normal practice, the five starting offensive linemen don’t take every single rep together. And that’s on purpose, because that’s the reality of games. He pointed out that last week, another lineman had to come in, and against the Rams, the backup swing tackle had to come in too. He said Josh even takes snaps from different centers in practice.

So the team practices with the mindset of mixing combinations. If Buffalo has to play a different group up front on Monday night, it won’t be anything new for them.

That’s smart preparation, Bills Mafia. Injuries are part of the NFL. The teams that win are the ones that are ready for them.

Now, the running backs and pass protection.

Brady was asked how the running backs have helped in pass protection this season. He said it’s been good. But he said this week will test them more than pretty much any other opponent, because of how much Minnesota blitzes. He called it a great challenge and a great opportunity.

And then he talked about the team’s identity. He said Buffalo wants a team that doesn’t care who makes the play. When the Bills are running the ball, everybody else blocks for the running backs. So when they’re passing the ball, the running backs have to do their part in protection. He said it’s important to them, just like running the football is.

But he was honest about what needs to improve. He said they have to clean up some technique, and pointed out they had a penalty last week for a running back going low. So there are still things to work on.

Think about how big this is, Bills Mafia. Josh Allen said earlier this week that the best way to beat a blitz is to make one player miss, because then there’s open space behind it. But before you can make someone miss, you need time. And against a defense that sends pressure from everywhere, the running backs picking up blitzers could be the difference between a sack and a touchdown.

Next, a reporter asked about Buffalo’s history of developing offensive line depth. Last year, backup linemen stepped in when injuries hit. This year, it’s been Tylan Grable. Why does this keep working?

Brady credited the coaches. He said Buffalo has had great offensive line coaches. He said Pat is doing an amazing job, and that assistant line coach Austin Gunn spends so much time with the players. He said they stay after every practice to develop.

He explained why offensive line is such a hard position to develop. You’re training a lot of different techniques. A player can come from another team as a free agent and be told to forget everything he was taught, because the technique here is completely different. But he said these players have the right personality and mindset to grow and adapt.

And he gave credit to Brandon Beane, saying Beane does a great job understanding the type of people and personalities the team needs, and the type of players who fit the scheme.

Then he talked about Grable specifically. He said that, just like with the backup who stepped in last year, seeing Grable come in, develop, and not blink has given the team total confidence. And then he said something that tells you a lot: when these backups go in, he doesn’t feel like he has to call a different football game.

Bills Mafia, that’s huge. If Dion Dawkins can’t go, Brady isn’t going to shrink the playbook. He trusts the next man up.

But a reporter asked about a real challenge. Grable played right tackle last week. If Dawkins can’t play, he might have to move to left tackle this week. What is that switch like?

Brady said yes, it’s a challenge. He explained that it’s harder for players who have been starters for a long time on one side. He shared a story from his time in New Orleans, about former Saints right tackle Zach Strief, who played right tackle all the time and once said his body couldn’t even get into a left tackle stance if he tried.

But Brady said this is the life Grable has lived. As a swing tackle, he prepares for both sides. So if he has to move to left tackle, Brady doesn’t think he would blink. He said Grable did a great job at right tackle.

But he was honest that there are real differences: which hand is your dominant hand, how you punch, how you set. Those things take time.

So if Grable does have to play left tackle against Brian Flores’s defense on Monday night, that will be one of the most important storylines of the game.

Now, the Vikings’ defense. A reporter asked Brady if he’s ever seen a defense that ignores normal personnel matching and tendencies the way Minnesota does.

Brady said maybe not to this degree. But he said it’s a growing trend in the NFL. Over the last couple of years, defenses have tried to one-up offenses with versatility, thanks to the type of players coming into the league.

He explained how it used to work. If an offense came out in 11 personnel, with three receivers, the defense would usually answer with its nickel package. Offenses would plan around that. But now, Brady said, some teams will just stay in their base defense against 11 personnel, because they believe they have players who can match up in the passing game.

He said it takes a certain type of coach to trust a scheme like that, and a lot of players who can handle playing in space. And he said Minnesota clearly understands who they are, what they want to be, and how teams are going to try to attack them.

That ties into what Brady said earlier this week: the Vikings can be in the same personnel grouping on third and 25 as on the first play of the game, run the same look, and still have success. And Josh Allen said the same look can mean completely different things depending on who’s on the field. That’s why this defense is so hard to prepare for.

And finally, the mindset.

A reporter brought up Brady’s phrase “competitive stamina,” and asked whether the offense feels extra motivation going up against the best defense in the NFL, as a kind of measuring stick.

Brady said it’s a huge opportunity and a great challenge. But then he explained something that tells you everything about how he’s building this team.

He said he has tried to establish a mindset in Buffalo that it should not matter who they’re playing. If their standards rise and fall based on the opponent, then they’re not building what they want to build. He said if they look at records, at stats, at anything like that, then they won’t play to their standard. It should always be about Buffalo first and foremost.

He said everyone knows this will be a great challenge. Going on the road is always a challenge, and playing this team is a challenge. But he said he hopes his players aren’t sitting there feeling like they have to make it bigger than it is, or that they have to rise to the occasion. They just have to do their job and execute.

And then he said the key line. He said that when players feel like they have to do more, or be a hero, that’s when they start playing outside the system.

So what does competitive stamina mean to Brady? Not caring, and just playing the next play. And he said that’s what this game is going to be all about.

Bills Mafia, that’s the same message Brady has been preaching all season. After the Raiders game, he said he didn’t want any position group feeling like they had to be superheroes. Josh Allen admitted after the Ravens game that he was pressing in the first half, and that once he took a breath and trusted the call, everything changed. And against the Ravens, Brady said his players didn’t care who was playing quarterback for Baltimore. There was a standard, and they played to it.

That’s the formula. No hero ball. No panic. Just do your job, one play at a time.

So let’s put it all together.

Joshua Palmer is out for Monday night.

Dion Dawkins, Alec Anderson, Greg Gaines, Jordan Hancock, and Tyrell Shavers are all questionable.

Dawkins hasn’t practiced all week, but Brady trusts that he can play without the practice reps, just like Spencer Brown last week and Christian Benford before the Rams game.

The Bills practice with mixed offensive line combinations, so they’re ready if they have to adjust.

The running backs face their biggest pass protection test of the season against Minnesota’s blitz.

Tylan Grable has earned the staff’s trust, and Brady says he doesn’t have to change his play calling when backups come in, though moving from right tackle to left tackle would be a real challenge.

And Brady’s message is clear: don’t make the game bigger than it is. Just play the next play.
