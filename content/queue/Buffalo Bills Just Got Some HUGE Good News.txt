BUFFALO BILLS JUST GOT SOME HUGE GOOD NEWS!

Bills Mafia, for weeks we watched this Buffalo Bills defense get picked apart. Giving up close to 30 points a game. Hanging in there for a while, then falling apart late. And honestly, a lot of us thought that was just going to be the story of this season.

So going into the Ravens game, many people expected more of the same.

That’s not what happened.

The Bills held Baltimore to just 10 points, the fewest they’ve allowed all season. But the numbers aren’t even the best part. What happened on that field tells us something about this defense that almost nobody saw coming. And today, we’re breaking all of it down.

Let’s get into it.

When the Bills came out of the bye, a lot of fans weren’t expecting much from this defense. And then the final score popped up: 36 to 10. The defense gave up just 10 points, its best performance of the season.

And let’s be clear about what kind of win this was. This wasn’t one of those games where the offense outscored everybody and we all pretended the defense was fine. No. This was the most complete performance this defense has put together all year. And Baltimore was held to season lows in multiple offensive categories.

To understand how big that is, you have to understand where this defense was coming from.

Going into Week 8, the Bills were allowing 28.5 points per game. That was the third most in the entire NFL. Third most. And it wasn’t just the points. They were giving up 386.8 total yards per game and 285.8 passing yards per game, and both of those numbers ranked second worst in the league.

Remember what Jim Leonhard said after the Raiders game? He said the defense’s ultimate job is keeping points off the board, and they weren’t doing it. He called it a big problem. And remember the stat from CBS analyst Jordan Dajani: Buffalo ranked first in the NFL in points per offensive drive, and dead last in points allowed per defensive drive.

It felt like everyone was waiting for something to click, and it just wasn’t clicking.

Until Sunday.

Greg Rousseau explained what changed after the game. He said the defense talked all week about being aggressive coming out of the bye. The goal was to start fast and, most importantly, to finish.

And that makes perfect sense. Rousseau pointed out that the defense has had good games this year, but then the other team sneaks in some garbage points late, and boom, it looks like they gave up 30.

Think about the fourth quarters, Bills Mafia. The Rams scored 20 in the fourth quarter on Monday night. The Raiders scored 17 in the fourth quarter in Las Vegas. That was the pattern. Play well for three quarters, then give it back at the end.

This time, Rousseau said, they just kept going. Possession after possession, they closed it out. He called that big time for the defense.

And it wasn’t just the players saying it. Joe Brady said the defense should look at this game and understand that this is the expectation.

But here’s what I love about how he said it. He made it clear he wasn’t talking about the production, the takeaways, or the sacks. He was talking about the style. The way they flew around.

A head coach saying the standard isn’t the stat sheet, it’s how you play. Brady said the tape is going to speak loudly, because of the way the players played for one another, the way they ran around, and the communication. He said everything he challenged them with coming off the bye, they responded to.

Communication, Bills Mafia. That’s been the word all season. Leonhard said communication in the secondary was the number one thing to fix. Brady said he wanted the defense to trust each other and play within the system. And on Sunday, Brady said the communication from Leonhard up in the box to the sideline was elite.

And you can see that communication in the numbers.

Baltimore finished with fewer than 150 yards of total offense, and just 68 passing yards.

Sixty-eight passing yards. Those were exactly the areas where the Bills ranked second worst in the league coming in. So the spots that hurt the most are the spots where this defense took the biggest step forward.

And Davidson Igbinosun put it perfectly. He said it’s getting to the point of the season where the defense knows what it has. They’re not trying to establish their identity anymore. He said they’ve created it.

After this defense struggled to find a groove for the first six weeks, that’s exactly what you want to hear.

Now let’s talk about how they built that identity, because the biggest piece, in my opinion, was Jim Leonhard getting aggressive. And I mean really aggressive.

According to Next Gen Stats, Leonhard blitzed on 64.5 percent of Baltimore’s dropbacks. That was the Bills’ highest blitz rate since 2021. Before this game, Buffalo’s blitz rate hadn’t gone above 39 percent in any game this season.

From never above 39 percent to 64.5 percent. That’s a massive swing.

And it wasn’t blitzing just for the sake of it. When the Bills sent extra rushers, they generated a season-high seven quick pressures, meaning pressures in under 2.5 seconds, and they got three sacks. It worked.

Think back to what Leonhard said after the Raiders game. He said Kirk Cousins was getting the ball out fast, and that Cousins hadn’t been sacked on a five-man rush all season. The rush was getting there, but not quickly enough. So what did Leonhard do after the bye? He sent more people, and he got there faster. Seven quick pressures. That’s how you beat a quick passing game.

Terrel Bernard explained it really well. He said Leonhard called a great game, called it at the right times, and sent guys at the right times. But then he added the part a lot of people forget: the players won their one-on-ones when they needed to.

Joe Brady added another layer. He said blitzing sometimes isn’t even about getting home. It’s about creating matchups and all the little nuances that come with it. He called it an incredible game from Leonhard, said Leonhard had a great pulse on how the game needed to be played, and that the players understood it too.

That buy-in is huge, Bills Mafia. The coordinator makes the call. The players trust it. And everybody does their job.

And remember, this was also the game where Leonhard moved up to the coaching box. Brady said being up there helps you see what you need to see without depending on anyone else. That clearly showed up on Sunday.

Before we go any further, smash that like button if you want to see Jim Leonhard keep this aggressive approach. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

When we talk about players winning their one-on-ones, nobody did it more than Greg Rousseau. He led the team with six pressures and added a sack.

As a team, the Bills finished with four sacks and 12 quarterback hits, and that was one of the biggest differences from the first six games.

We all feel it when the pass rush shows up, because everything behind it gets easier. But Rousseau didn’t take the credit. He shouted out the whole defense and said it’s not just the rush and it’s not just the blitzing. It’s the rush and the coverage working together.

And Joe Brady talked about Rousseau this week too. He said Rousseau is very intentional, wants to be great, and is such a pro. He said Rousseau is feeding off Bradley Chubb, T.J. Parker, Deone Walker, and the return of Ed Oliver. And speaking of Ed Oliver, in his first game back since Week 4, Brady said he flew around, made a ton of plays, and was so much fun to watch.

That rush and coverage working together really showed up in the secondary, which is even more impressive, because multiple starters in the defensive backfield didn’t suit up on Sunday. C.J. Gardner-Johnson was suspended. Others were banged up. And this group was still lights out.

The major point of emphasis was Zay Flowers.

Flowers came into the game ranked second in the NFL in receiving yards per game, and he led the league in yards after the catch, with 331. Joe Brady said earlier in the week that when Flowers gets the ball in his hands, he’s electric, and that he has great chemistry with Lamar Jackson.

So what happened?

Zay Flowers finished with zero catches on five targets.

Zero. The Bills completely neutralized him. And honestly, that might be the most impressive part of the day.

Damar Hamlin, who started at safety in place of the suspended Gardner-Johnson, said Flowers was big on their minds all week, because any time he gets the ball he can take it the distance. So the goal was to eliminate space. And Damar gave credit to Jim Leonhard for the game plan that made it happen.

Bills Mafia, think about Damar’s week. Starting at safety. Brady said he’d earned it, that he deserved it, that he was going to make the most of it. And he helped shut down one of the most dangerous receivers in football. What a performance.

And when a game plan comes together like that, sometimes the reward shows up in a big moment. For Davidson Igbinosun, it came in the second quarter, when he caught the first interception of his career.

Christian Benford had a great moment talking about it. He said Igbinosun has been working his tail off, that it was a blessing for him to get that first career pick, and that the guys are always by his side. He said he’s proud of the player Igbinosun has become, and that the opportunity found him.

Remember, Leonhard said a few weeks ago that Igbinosun is confident and aggressive, that this is what gets him in trouble but also what makes him special. He said Igbinosun had earned the majority of the snaps at corner. Now he has his first career interception. That’s growth you can see.

And that closeness in the locker room goes right back to what Brady said about playing for one another. Because 11 different Bills players generated at least one pressure in this game. Eleven. And eight of them had multiple pressures.

So this wasn’t one player having a monster day while everybody else watched. It was a full group effort.

And I think that’s what makes this believable. If it were just Greg Rousseau doing everything, you’d worry about whether it can last. But when that many players are contributing, it feels a lot more like a real identity than a one-game thing.

When you put it all together, the Bills posted season-best marks in five different areas. They allowed just 10 first downs. They allowed only 68 passing yards. They held Baltimore to its lowest total yardage. They forced three turnovers. And they recorded 12 quarterback hits.

That’s an entire defense stepping up at the same time.

Now, some people are going to say this happened because Baltimore lost Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley to injuries and ended up with its third-string quarterback. And look, that’s part of the story. Nobody wants to see players get hurt, and Josh Allen made a point of saying he hopes they’re okay.

But Joe Brady pushed back on that idea. He said he doesn’t think the result was a product of facing backup quarterbacks. He said that, based on the way the team practiced during the week, he felt this was going to be the result regardless. And he said the players didn’t care who was playing quarterback. There was a standard, and they were going to play to it.

And let’s be honest: Zay Flowers getting shut out, the blitz rate, the quick pressures, the eleven players with pressures. Those things aren’t just about who’s throwing the ball. That’s about how this defense played.

And you can hear in the way these players are talking that they see this as a starting point, not a finish line.

Christian Benford said it was a great game for them. He said they played together, played fundamental football, and played situational football really well. He specifically brought up third down and fourth down, saying they stayed true to their technique, trusted each other, and played well.

Those are the moments where defenses usually break, Bills Mafia. And on Sunday, this one didn’t.

Greg Rousseau said they just have to keep it going.

And Davidson Igbinosun talked about having created an identity.

That doesn’t sound like a group that thinks it’s done. It sounds like a group that thinks it just found something. And going a full 60 minutes with a performance this strong is huge for its confidence.

But here’s the thing: confidence is great, and now it has to travel.

In Week 9, the Bills hit the road to face the Minnesota Vikings on Monday Night Football, November 9th, at 8:15 p.m. Eastern. The whole country is going to get a look at whether this defense can keep it going.

And it’s a big test. The Vikings have been one of the best teams in football this season. They’ve got weapons like Justin Jefferson, Aaron Jones, and T.J. Hockenson, and an elite defense of their own. It’s also the same building where a rookie Josh Allen hurdled Anthony Barr, and where Josh now needs just nine yards to become only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards.

So the offense has its storyline. But for me, the biggest question going into Monday night is on the other side of the ball. Can this defense prove that Sunday wasn’t a one-time thing? Can it play with that same style, that same aggressiveness, that same communication, on the road, in prime time, against a great team?

If it can, Bills Mafia, then this team isn’t just a contender. It’s the favorite.

So let’s sum it all up.

The Bills defense held Baltimore to 10 points, the fewest it has allowed all season.

It held the Ravens to just 68 passing yards and fewer than 150 total yards, after ranking second worst in the league in both categories coming in.

Jim Leonhard blitzed on 64.5 percent of dropbacks, the team’s highest rate since 2021, and it produced a season-high seven quick pressures.

Greg Rousseau led the way with six pressures and a sack, and 11 different players generated pressure.

Zay Flowers, one of the league’s most dangerous receivers, finished with zero catches.

Davidson Igbinosun picked off his first career pass.

And the players say they’ve created their identity.

That’s huge good news, Bills Mafia. Because if this defense can play like this consistently, with Josh Allen and this offense on the other side, there may be no team in the NFL that can stop the Buffalo Bills.
