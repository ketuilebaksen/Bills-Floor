THE QB DECISION THAT CHANGED THE VIKINGS GAME PLAN — AND WHAT JOE BRADY REVEALED!

Bills Mafia, before Monday Night Football in Minnesota, Joe Brady stepped to the podium and got hit with breaking news in real time. A reporter told him that the Vikings had just officially announced their starting quarterback for the game: Carson Wentz.

And how Brady handled that moment, along with everything else he said in that press conference, tells us a lot about how this Buffalo Bills team operates. He explained how the defense prepares for two quarterbacks at once. He broke down what makes Brian Flores one of the best minds in football. He opened up, emotionally, about the release of C.J. Gardner-Johnson. He gave updates on DJ Moore, Spencer Brown, and Jordan Hancock. And with today being trade deadline day, what he said about his working relationship with Brandon Beane matters more than ever.

So let’s go through all of it.

Let’s get into it.

First, the quarterback decision.

A reporter told Brady that the Vikings had just announced Carson Wentz as the starter, and that head coach Kevin O’Connell had made it official. Then the reporter asked about the challenge of spending the middle of a week preparing for multiple possibilities, and then shifting once you know exactly who you’re facing.

Brady’s answer showed how calm and prepared this staff is.

He said the defense had to have the mindset of getting ready for both quarterbacks. He gave Wentz real credit, saying he had already played games this season and had been a really good football player in this league for a long time.

Brady admitted there are always some adjustments when you’re dealing with different skill sets. Every quarterback is different. Some extend plays more, some get the ball out faster, some attack different parts of the field.

But then Brady made a sharp point. This wasn’t like finding out on the second play of a game that the quarterback has changed and having to adjust on the fly. They knew ahead of time. And he said there’s nothing in this situation that Jim Leonhard and his staff aren’t used to handling.

And Bills Mafia, we had just seen proof of that. One week earlier, against Baltimore, the Ravens lost Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley during the game and ended up with undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano under center. Leonhard adjusted in real time, from the coaching box, and Brady said the way he called that game was outstanding. Buffalo held Baltimore to 10 points and 68 passing yards.

So if Leonhard could adjust mid-game to a third-string rookie, adjusting to a veteran like Wentz with a few days of notice was well within his comfort zone.

Next, the Flores challenge.

Brady was asked how you simulate Brian Flores’s blitz packages in practice, especially with the NFL’s limits on contact.

And Brady was refreshingly honest. He said no matter how you practice, it’s not going to look the same as a game. It won’t have the same tempo, the same speed, or the same physicality.

He called Minnesota an elite defense, one that makes you work the entire game, and said that’s why they’re number one in the league.

So how do you prepare for something you can’t fully replicate? Brady’s answer: trust your rules and trust your technique. The communication has to be elite. And on the road, he said, it’s always going to be hard. Minnesota does an outstanding job of making every single play stressful. He said your scout team will never look exactly like the real thing at full speed, but you try to simulate it as much as you possibly can.

Then came a funny moment. Two reporters tried to ask follow-up questions at the same time, and Brady laughed and said he could answer both, joking that this is what life on a headset is like.

Then he got to the real question: what makes Flores’s defense so successful?

Brady said Flores is one of the best minds in the game. He said Flores has evolved, and that it isn’t the same defense every year. He keeps changing the scheme. His players have a great understanding of how they’re attacking protections and coverages. And there’s a clear identity in the way they play.

Brady even said that if you’re a fan of football, you have to enjoy how Minnesota plays. And he said Flores just keeps reinventing himself and his defense.

That’s a head coach showing a ton of respect to an opponent. And it lined up with what Josh Allen said that week, when he described Minnesota as a defense with no weaknesses, where if you turn your eyes the wrong way, you’re already wrong.

Now, the noise.

U.S. Bank Stadium is indoors, and it’s one of the loudest buildings in the NFL. A reporter asked how Buffalo prepares to communicate against a blitzing defense in that environment.

Brady said communication on the road starts with him. He has to get the play calls in on time. He has to be mindful of how long each call is and how quickly he can get it to the huddle, so the offense has time to get to the line of scrimmage and make sure everyone is on the same page, without sloppy footwork in the setup.

Then there’s the communication with Josh, especially in the shotgun, making sure every player knows the protection and the plan. At home, Brady said, you can do a lot of those things more easily. On the road, there’s a real time component to it.

And here’s a detail I love. Brady said the team knew it would be playing a lot of road games this season. So in training camp, whenever they practiced indoors or held walkthroughs, everything was done with crowd noise. Both the offense and the defense practiced communicating through chaos and noise.

But Brady admitted that no matter how loud the speakers are in practice, nothing can truly replicate the real thing. He called it a great environment, and an opportunity to show how good the team’s communication is and how much it matters to them.

That’s the theme of this entire season, Bills Mafia. Communication. Leonhard said it was the number one fix for the defense. Brady said it was elite from upstairs to downstairs against Baltimore. And in Minnesota, it was going to be tested on every single snap.

Now, the most emotional part of the press conference: C.J. Gardner-Johnson.

This was the first time reporters had spoken with Brady since the Bills released Gardner-Johnson on Tuesday, one day after his one-game suspension ended. So they asked him directly: what went into the decision?

Brady said, as he’d told reporters the week before, there was a lot to evaluate. The team got together and decided what they felt was best for the Buffalo Bills.

And then he spoke from the heart.

He said he has so much love for C.J. That he really does. He loved getting to coach him. He loved being around him. He loved his edge and his personality, and letting him be himself as a person. And Brady said that’s why the decision wasn’t easy at all.

But he explained that the team also has to be an organization that makes the right decisions. He said what happened was unfortunate, but it also opened a door and gave other players an opportunity. And seeing how those players performed was part of the evaluation.

And Bills Mafia, we know how they performed. Damar Hamlin and Geno Stone played next to Cole Bishop against Baltimore and helped hold the Ravens to 10 points. Zay Flowers finished with zero catches.

Then Brady cleared up the timing question so many people had. He said the team didn’t walk out of Las Vegas planning to release Gardner-Johnson the following Monday. The plan was for him to be here. They were going to evaluate everything after the Baltimore game. And with the moving parts on the roster, they felt the move was in the best interest of the football team.

That fits with the reporting we’ve seen. Insider Cameron Wolfe reported that the release was more about inconsistent play than about the spit. Pro Football Talk reported that the team felt he was too high-maintenance. And with Gardner-Johnson returning from suspension, the Bills needed a roster spot, and decided to keep a defensive back they had signed while he was out.

A reporter then asked if Brady had spoken to Gardner-Johnson since the release. Brady said yes, he was part of it. He wouldn’t share details. But he talked about how hard these moments are.

He said there’s never anything good about releasing a player. It doesn’t matter if it’s cut-down day, or if a player has been with the team for one day, or if he’s a veteran. He said it’s part of the job, and you have to be honest and have those conversations. But that doesn’t mean he doesn’t care.

And then Brady shared something personal. He said he has been called into an office and told he was being let go too. He knows what that feels like. So he doesn’t take it lightly.

And he finished by saying that, about the person and the player he got to be around, he has nothing but love for C.J.

Bills Mafia, that’s leadership. You make the hard decision. But you never forget the human being on the other side of it.

Next, the offensive line depth.

A reporter asked about the backup swing tackle who had started in place of Spencer Brown against the Ravens. How confident is Brady if this player has to play extended snaps the rest of the season?

Brady said the player showed in the Rams game that he could come in during the middle of a game without hesitating, because he prepares. And he explained what life is like for a backup swing tackle. You have to be ready to play left tackle or right tackle at any moment. One play, you might go in for Spencer Brown. Then Brown comes back, and on the next play, you’re going in for Dion Dawkins.

Brady said this player had a tough year last season. But he has grown a lot working with offensive line coaches Pat and Austin, and has embraced the role. Brady said he understands the player’s skill set and knows what fits him when he’s on the field. He said the player has essentially played two games, and there hasn’t been any hesitation in terms of what the team can and can’t do with him.

He gave him a lot of credit for his development and said he has a lot of confidence in him.

And that confidence matters, Bills Mafia. Because later that week, Dion Dawkins showed up on the injury report as questionable after missing practice. Depth on the offensive line is never a luxury. It’s a necessity.

Then came more injury news.

A reporter noted that Jordan Hancock was listed on the transaction wire as returning to practice. And Brady had a little fun. A couple of weeks earlier, he had mixed up the terminology with Tyrell Shavers, saying he was being activated when really his practice window was being opened. So this time, Brady joked that he’d say it correctly: they were opening Hancock’s practice window, so nobody would have to correct him or fix their tweets.

He said Hancock would practice and the team would keep evaluating him. He said he was excited, because it had been a while since Hancock got to play football. He’d been working with the strength staff, but now he’d finally get to put the pads back on.

And Brady confirmed that DJ Moore and Spencer Brown would both be on the practice field too, and that the team would keep evaluating them throughout the week.

Brady was also asked about one of the most iconic plays in Bills history: Josh Allen’s hurdle over Anthony Barr in Minneapolis, back in 2018.

Brady said he’s been in Buffalo long enough that there are many plays from before his time that he’s seen on film. Sometimes they’ll run a play in practice and he’ll ask Josh if he remembers a certain play from 2019 on the road, and they’ll both know exactly which one he means.

He said everybody has their first “Josh Allen moment,” and for a lot of people, it’s the hurdle over Barr. He said he has heard Barr talk about it. And then he joked that he hoped there would be no hurdles that week. It was cool for fans to watch back then, but no more “hurdle avenue.”

But he also said that play captured the Josh Allen experience: a player who will lay it all on the line for his teammates every week. Brady said Josh has grown to the point where he doesn’t feel like he has to hurdle in every big moment, but he’s still the same person who will do whatever it takes to win. And Brady said he’s fortunate to now see it live.

Now, the trade deadline. And since today is deadline day, this part of the press conference is more relevant than ever.

A reporter asked Brady how much he communicates with Brandon Beane, since this is his first deadline as head coach.

Brady said he and Beane have been working together from the very beginning: free agency, the draft, and now the constant moves between the practice squad and the active roster. He said their office doors are right next to each other and they’re always open.

He described their conversations: what Brady is thinking, which players they might need to call up that week, who would be next in line if something happens to someone else.

And he said he has all the confidence in Beane when it comes to the roster.

And then he joked that, with his offense having to block all of Minnesota’s pressures that week, he had enough on his plate without sitting in his office wondering what was happening around the league. He said he trusts the personnel staff completely. If they feel something needs to be added, they’ll do it. And if not, the team will be good to go.

Bills Mafia, think about what that means for today. Buffalo had only about 3.93 million dollars in cap space before releasing Gardner-Johnson, and that move freed up only about 500,000 dollars while leaving 2.02 million in dead money. So Beane doesn’t have much room to work with. But he does have the full trust of his head coach.

A reporter also asked whether the deadline creates uncertainty in the locker room. Brady said that professional football players understand it’s part of the life. They see moves happening across the league. He said he doesn’t focus on it, because the focus was on Minneapolis and Monday Night Football, and everything else would take care of itself. He said if a move were coming that affected the players, the team would communicate with them directly.

So let’s put it all together.

Brady learned in real time that Carson Wentz would start for Minnesota, and he said the defense had already prepared for both quarterbacks, with nothing Jim Leonhard’s staff wasn’t used to handling.

He said you can never fully simulate Brian Flores’s blitz in practice, and called Flores one of the best minds in the game.

He explained how communication on the road starts with him getting calls in on time, and revealed the team practiced with crowd noise all the way back in training camp.

He spoke with real emotion about C.J. Gardner-Johnson, saying he has nothing but love for him, and confirmed the decision was made after evaluating the Baltimore game.

He expressed strong confidence in his backup swing tackle, opened Jordan Hancock’s practice window, and had DJ Moore and Spencer Brown back on the field.

And he said he and Brandon Beane are in constant communication, with their office doors always open, and that he fully trusts Beane at the deadline.
