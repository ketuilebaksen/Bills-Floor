Josh admitted he made a couple of bad throws, including two interceptions. Joe Brady said the Bills should never be throwing the ball 45 times in a game like that, and said flat out that he didn't do a good enough job. And in between, there was a fake punt pass that had Josh staring at an iPad in disbelief, an onside kick idea that never happened, and a performance from James Cook that Brady said puts him among the best players in the entire NFL.

Now, let's be fair about this game, because it was always going to be tough.

Before the game, ESPN analyst Dan Orlovsky warned that Miami's defense was better than its record. He said Jeff Hafley's scheme slows quarterbacks down, because the coverages are so muddy and players move into different roles. He said Miami's defensive line was very good, with both edge rushers playing at a high level. And he said he didn't believe this would be a walk-in, dominant offensive performance for Buffalo.

And Josh said exactly that after the game. He said it was a division game, and the other team gets paid too. He said Miami made some plays, and that it has some really young talent. He said he thought Buffalo communicated well, and that it's just a couple of things here and there that can help the offense become what it wants to be.

A reporter also pointed out that Josh didn't run much in this game. Was that his choice, or was the running room just not there?

Josh said that when he goes back and watches the tape, Miami did a great job of staying sticky on Buffalo's receivers, and then taking away the space whenever he got out of the pocket. He said Miami had a really good game plan coming in, and called it a really good defense.

And when he was asked whether he was seeing the field well, Josh said he actually felt like he was seeing it really well. He said there were plays where Miami's linebackers were sitting in the zones Buffalo was trying to attack. Again, something to learn from.

He also talked about the deep shots Buffalo took early in the game. He said he was just trying to get the ball to his guys. There were opportunities downfield, and he said that when they take those shots, they have to convert. And he said a couple of those were bad throws by him.

There was also a moment involving an onside kick.

Brady was asked about the logic behind a possible onside kick. He said the Bills wanted to see if there was a certain look from Miami's return team that would give them a chance to try it. But he said Miami was given time to get set up and lined up. And he pointed out that the league has been trying to speed that process up.

He explained that Buffalo was coming off a TV timeout, and he said that, in his mind, he had every right to hold the decision until he needed to, because it's not up to the officials when he tells them. But in the end, Buffalo didn't get the look it wanted. So the Bills took a delay penalty instead, and Brady credited his kicker for handling the kick well afterward.

That's Joe Brady, Bills Mafia. Always looking for an edge, but never forcing it if the look isn't there.

Now, Joe Brady's press conference. And he went even further than Josh.

A reporter pointed out that Buffalo had about 45 or 46 dropbacks, while leading for most of the game. What was the philosophy behind throwing so much?

Brady explained that some of those plays were runs that turned into passes based on the looks Miami's defense showed. Some were quick throws. But then he said this: in a game like that, he doesn't think the Bills should ever be throwing the ball 45 times.

And then he took the blame. He said, plainly, that he obviously didn't do a good enough job.

Bills Mafia, think about how rare that is. A head coach whose team just won, going to the podium and saying the game plan was wrong, and it's on him.

And then Brady was asked about James Cook. A reporter noted that Cook had some issues holding onto the football earlier in the season, but that he's clearly worked hard on it, and even his grip on the ball looks better. What did the team work on?

Brady said nothing different. He said the coaches spend a lot of time on the details with Cook, but that it's important to Cook himself, and he's done a great job with it.

Remember, after the Raiders game earlier this season, Cook had a costly fumble in a four-minute situation. And Brady said then that he was going to keep handing him the football, that he trusted Cook to correct it, and that when Cook holds the ball, he holds the whole organization.

Now Brady says Cook has been extremely intentional about ball security. And he said that, just like the whole team, they can't get lax just because results are going their way. He said they'll go back and watch the tape, and if the ball ever looks loose, just because Cook didn't fumble doesn't make it right.

And then Brady said the line every Bills fan will love. He said he thought Cook ran the ball well in this game, but that the team obviously didn't give it to him enough. And then he said: Jimbo is playing as good as anybody in the NFL right now.
