Gardner-Johnson just one day after his suspension ended. The safety who spit toward Brock Bowers in Las Vegas was gone the moment he became eligible to play again. Six games in Buffalo. Six starts. And then, an unexpected exit.

From the outside, it looks simple: he spit, he got suspended, he got cut. But the people who cover this team every single day are telling a very different story. And when you put all the pieces together, you realize the spit was never really the reason.

Part one: the moment that started it all.

Week 6. October 18th. The Bills are in Las Vegas to face the Raiders. It’s late in the first half, and C.J. Gardner-Johnson and Raiders tight end Brock Bowers are jawing at each other after a play. Then Gardner-Johnson spits in Bowers’s direction.

A flag comes out. He’s ejected. His day is over.

The referee explained afterward that the umpire saw the entire thing, and that the league office in New York backed up the call.

And the cost to Buffalo was immediate. The penalty extended the Raiders’ drive. Las Vegas kicked a field goal to tie the game at halftime. And the Bills had to play the whole second half without one of their starting safeties. Damar Hamlin came in and settled things down. And Buffalo still found a way to win, 33 to 30.

But Joe Brady was not happy. After the game, he said the discipline of this team has to improve, and that what happened absolutely can’t happen. He said the team is bigger than that. Everything is bigger than that.

Gardner-Johnson spoke after the game too. He said he had to control his emotions. But when a reporter asked him directly whether he spit on a player, he wouldn’t say yes. He called it competing in the heat of the moment.

Then things got worse. San Francisco 49ers fullback Kyle Juszczyk posted on social media, claiming Gardner-Johnson had spit on him the year before, when Gardner-Johnson was playing for the Bears. And Juszczyk called it the least surprising thing ever.

Meanwhile, on the other sideline, Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak praised his players for keeping their composure. He said his team knew going into the game that certain players would try to get under their skin, and that anyone who took the bait would be standing next to him on the sideline. He didn’t name names. But it was clear the Raiders had prepared for exactly this.

That night, Gardner-Johnson posted an apology on Instagram to Bowers, to his teammates, and to both organizations. He wrote that there was no excuse for his actions and that he took full accountability.

So the apology came quickly. But the real question was whether his play on the field would back it up.

The next day, the NFL announced its decision: a one-game suspension without pay for violating the league’s sportsmanship rule. That meant Gardner-Johnson would miss the Ravens game on November 1st.

Part two: the timing mystery.

This is where the story gets strange.

On the Monday after the Raiders game, Joe Brady stood up for Gardner-Johnson. Yes, he called the spit inexcusable. But he also said the team would not add any punishment of its own on top of the league’s suspension. And then he said this: C.J. is a member of the Buffalo Bills, and they brought him here for a reason.

So think about the sequence.

The head coach backs him.

The league suspends him for one game.

He serves the suspension.

And one day later, he’s released.

If the spit was the reason, why didn’t the Bills release him right away? Why wait more than two weeks?

Hold onto that question, Bills Mafia. Because the answer is the whole story.

Part three: the numbers don’t lie.

Let’s start with how Gardner-Johnson actually played this season.

Pro Football Focus grades every player in the league. The higher the number, the better. Gardner-Johnson’s overall grade this season was 55.2. Against the run, it was even worse: 50.0. For a player the Bills signed to be a starter, those grades are near the bottom of the league at his position.

Then there’s the tackling. Gardner-Johnson missed nine tackles in just six games. Two of them came in the first half against the Raiders, before he was even ejected.

And for a safety, that’s a huge problem. The safety is the last line of defense between the ball carrier and the end zone. When a safety misses, a five-yard gain can become a 50-yard touchdown. Joe Brady said in recent weeks that the defense needed to clean up its tackling across the board. Jim Leonhard said the defense wasn’t doing its most important job: keeping points off the board.

In coverage, it wasn’t much better. When quarterbacks threw at Gardner-Johnson, they completed 64.7 percent of their passes, with a passer rating of 83.5. In plain terms, throwing his way wasn’t a bad idea for opposing offenses.

To be fair, it wasn’t all bad. He made 26 tackles and had one interception, which he returned 41 yards. He had his moments. But a starting safety on a Super Bowl contender needs to be more than a player who has moments.

Part four: the game that changed everything.

After the bye week, the Bills had to play the Baltimore Ravens without Gardner-Johnson. And they won 36 to 10.

Damar Hamlin and Geno Stone played alongside starter Cole Bishop, and they were steady all afternoon. The defense held Baltimore to just 10 points, the fewest Buffalo had allowed all season. The Ravens finished with only 68 passing yards. Zay Flowers, one of the most dangerous receivers in the league, finished with zero catches on five targets. Damar Hamlin said the goal was to eliminate space, and he gave Jim Leonhard credit for the game plan.

Now, let’s be honest. Baltimore was banged up. The Ravens lost Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley, and undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano played quarterback for most of the day. It wasn’t the hardest test this defense will face.

But it answered the one question the Bills needed answered: can we win without him?

Yes.

ESPN reported that the feeling inside the building was that the players already on the roster had played well enough to earn a longer look, with or without Gardner-Johnson.

And there’s another detail that a lot of fans missed. Through the first six weeks, Gardner-Johnson didn’t play a single special teams snap. Meanwhile, Damar Hamlin, Geno Stone, and rookie Jaylon Kilgore all contribute on special teams.

That matters a lot in Buffalo. Joe Brady recently said one metric site ranks the Bills’ special teams number one in the league. He called those units the ethos of the whole football team. He even showed every single kickoff from the Raiders game to the team in a meeting, because so many different players were making plays.

So if Gardner-Johnson isn’t clearly the best safety on the field, he’s taking a roster spot from a player who can do more for the team.

And remember, before Buffalo, he had already played for six teams in five years. The Bills knew he came with a short leash.

Part five: solving the timing mystery.

So why did the Bills wait?

Here’s how it worked. While Gardner-Johnson was suspended, he didn’t count against the 53-man roster. That gave Buffalo an open spot. And on October 31st, the Bills used it to sign defensive back Te’Cory Couch to the active roster.

Once the suspension ended, Gardner-Johnson needed his roster spot back. That meant someone had to go.

The Bills kept Couch. They let Gardner-Johnson go.

So the suspension didn’t cause the release. It just set the date.

And Joe Brady confirmed this when he finally spoke about it. He said the team didn’t come out of Las Vegas planning to release him the following Monday. The plan was for Gardner-Johnson to stay. They were going to evaluate everything after the Baltimore game. And with the moving parts on the roster, they decided releasing him was in the best interest of the football team.

But that still doesn’t explain why Buffalo chose a depth defensive back over a Super Bowl champion. So here’s the real reason.

Part six: the real reason.

NFL insider Cameron Wolfe reported that the release had more to do with how inconsistent Gardner-Johnson’s play was than with the spitting incident. He reported that the team felt there were too many plays that left people wondering what he was doing.

That’s the key. Not the spit. The plays.

Newsweek reported the same thing: it was the inconsistency that led to the release, not so much the spitting. They also noted that it contrasted with what Brady said back in October, when he publicly backed Gardner-Johnson. The Bills had signed him to a one-year deal worth up to 6 million dollars, hoping he’d bring energy to the back end of the defense.

Pro Football Talk heard a similar story from another angle. A source told them the Bills decided Gardner-Johnson was simply too high-maintenance. In other words, the talent and production weren’t big enough to justify the headaches.

Part seven: a pattern Buffalo knew about.

Honestly, Bills Mafia, Buffalo knew this was a possibility from the start.

Last season, the Houston Texans released Gardner-Johnson after just three games. Reports said there was friction behind the scenes, that he wasn’t happy with his role and wanted to blitz more. Then he went to Baltimore and never played a game for the Ravens. He finished the season with the Bears.

And back in 2024, he was ejected during a game against the Commanders and made an obscene gesture toward the crowd as he left the field.

Gardner-Johnson told his own version of the Houston story this summer. He said the trouble started when someone close to the Texans insulted him during training camp. But Texans head coach DeMeco Ryans said the release was his decision.

Either way, it’s the same pattern. He shows up. He brings energy. And then it ends fast.

Players around the league know his reputation too. In The Athletic’s anonymous player poll, he was voted the biggest trash talker in the NFL in 2023. The next year, he won the poll’s “most annoying player” category.

And here’s the part that stings. Before the season, Pro Football Talk reported that general manager Brandon Beane did his homework on Gardner-Johnson and made sure he understood the ground rules. Gardner-Johnson knew people already expected Buffalo to release him at some point, and he vowed he was going to stay.

He made it six games.

Part eight: what Buffalo was betting on.

So why did the Bills sign him in the first place?

They were betting on the 2024 version of C.J. Gardner-Johnson. That year, with the Super Bowl champion Philadelphia Eagles, PFF graded him at 77.0. He had six interceptions and helped Philadelphia win a title.

But in 2025, bouncing between teams, his grade fell to 51.4.

This season, it was 55.2.

Buffalo was hoping to get the 2024 player back. What they got looked a lot more like the 2025 player, with the drama on top.

Part nine: the cost.

The Bills had about 3.93 million dollars in cap space before this move. Releasing Gardner-Johnson frees up roughly 500,000 dollars in cap space, but it also leaves 2.02 million dollars in dead money. Dead money is salary that still counts against the cap for a player who’s no longer on the roster.

So the Bills are paying him not to play for them.

And that tells you how confident they are in this decision. A team doesn’t eat dead money unless it truly believes it’s better off without the player.

Now, on trade deadline day, that cap situation matters. With so little space, Brandon Beane has very little room to work with if he wants to add a safety before the deadline. Sports Illustrated has already floated names the Bills could target if they want more help at the position. So keep an eye on Buffalo today.

Part ten: the other side.

Not everyone agrees with this move.

Former NFL linebacker Emmanuel Acho said the Bills just lost their enforcer. His argument is that you sometimes need a player like Gardner-Johnson to win, and that Buffalo doesn’t have a tough guy on defense who sets the tone.

And there’s something to that. Edge and physicality matter, especially in January. In 2024, Gardner-Johnson’s six interceptions helped the Eagles win a Super Bowl.

But that wasn’t the player Buffalo was getting this year.

And look at where the Bills stand. Hamlin and Stone just helped hold a team to 10 points. The defense found its identity after the bye. Davidson Igbinosun said it himself: the defense isn’t trying to establish its identity anymore, it has created it. Joe Brady said the style, the way the players flew around and played for one another, is now the expectation.

And Brady made it clear he has no hard feelings. When he finally spoke about the release, he said he has so much love for C.J. He said he loved coaching him, loved being around him, loved his edge and personality, and loved letting him be himself. He said that made the decision anything but easy. And he shared that he has been called into an office and let go himself, so he knows what that feels like.

That’s how you handle a tough decision with class.

Part eleven: what’s next.

For now, Damar Hamlin is in line to start at safety. And Bills Mafia, that’s a powerful moment. Every Bills fan knows Damar’s story: the cardiac arrest on the field in January 2023, the comeback, and now his work with the American Heart Association’s Nation of Lifesavers program. Joe Brady said Damar has a unique skill set, knows what to do, and plays winning football whenever he’s out there. Jim Leonhard called him a true veteran who communicates well and doesn’t get enough credit.

As for Gardner-Johnson, he went on waivers. That means any team could claim him. If nobody does, he becomes a free agent and can sign anywhere. And some people are already calling for a return to Philadelphia, where he won his Super Bowl.

So here’s the bottom line.

The real reason the Bills released C.J. Gardner-Johnson wasn’t the spit. The spit just set the date.

The real reason was what happened on the field. A 55.2 PFF grade. Nine missed tackles in six games. Too many plays that left the coaches wondering what he was doing. No contribution on special teams. And a defense that proved against Baltimore that it could thrive without him.

I think it was the right call. This defense has found something: trust, communication, and an identity built on playing for each other. And the best way to protect that is to keep the players who fit it.
