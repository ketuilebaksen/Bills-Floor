Great news number one: James Cook just made NFL history.

Bills Mafia, that record used to be shared by two Hall of Famers: LaDainian Tomlinson and John Riggins. Now it belongs to James Cook alone.

And one analyst explained why Cook is so dangerous. He said the thing about James is that he can score from any distance. He pointed out that Cook doesn't get as many of the short goal-line touchdowns, because those usually go to Josh Allen. But Cook has scored from 40 yards out, from 60, from 35, and from 75.

That home-run ability, the analyst said, is what gets Buffalo's play-callers out of trouble a lot. He said you don't necessarily have to say that the offense flows through James Cook. But at the end of the day, he's the ace up Buffalo's sleeve. The Bills know they have one of the most dynamic running backs in the NFL, and if they get him the ball in space or between the tackles, he can take it the distance.

And the analyst said that takes pressure off Joe Brady, who doesn't have to build the entire game plan around Josh Allen. If the Bills need to, they can make it an ugly game, run the football, and win that way. And Buffalo is good at winning those kinds of games.

Great news number two: the defense.

The Bills have now gone 15 consecutive quarters without allowing a touchdown. That's nearly four full games.

The analysts gave Jim Leonhard credit. One of them, Pete, said he's seen real progress. He said that when Leonhard moved up to the coaching box, the defense became much more aggressive, which he believes it needed to do.

And he pointed to the Amani Hooker trade, saying getting Hooker at safety is big, because it gives Buffalo a lot of range and the ability to cover a lot of ground. Another analyst pointed to Damar Hamlin stepping up and making plays.

Bills Mafia, remember where this defense was. Before the bye week, Buffalo was allowing 28.5 points per game, the third-most in the NFL. Now? Baltimore scored 10. Minnesota scored six. The Jets scored 12. Miami scored nine. Fifteen straight quarters without a touchdown.
