We’ll start with the injury updates, because Buffalo could be getting some important reinforcements before the Ravens come to town. The Week 7 bye gave several players extra time to recover. But the Bills are also dealing with two significant absences.

First up, the big one: wide receiver DJ Moore.

Moore has been recovering from a re-aggravated AC joint sprain in his shoulder, and he missed Weeks 5 and 6. That means he sat out both the Monday night thriller against the Rams and the 33 to 30 win in Las Vegas. The bye gave him extra time to heal, and his return would be a huge boost for Josh Allen.

Joe Brady talked about Moore this week, and you could tell how much the coach values him. Brady said DJ is as tough as they come, the kind of guy who says a lot without saying a lot. He admitted it was frustrating for Moore to miss those games, but said it was the best thing for him. And Brady said it would be nice to get him back on the field and see how it goes as the week progresses. He talked about the toughness and playmaking ability Moore brings to this offense.

Think about what Moore would add, Bills Mafia. This offense has been scoring around 30 points a game without him. Keon Coleman has stepped up in a big way, with touchdowns in three straight games, and he’s leading the league in EPA per target. Khalil Shakir is still a matchup problem. The running backs have been dominant in the passing game. Now add DJ Moore back into that mix. He gives Allen another dangerous target, he creates space for everyone else, and he gives Josh more answers against Baltimore’s defense.

But let’s be clear: Moore’s return hasn’t been confirmed. His final injury designation will decide whether he’s available on Sunday. So keep your fingers crossed.

Next, defensive tackle Ed Oliver.

Oliver has been out since suffering a knee-area injury against the New England Patriots in Week 4. His recovery has included missed practices and limited participation, but Buffalo is hoping to get him back.

And against Baltimore, Oliver could be extremely valuable. His ability to blow up the running game and create pressure up the middle is exactly what you need against an offense like this. Joe Brady said it himself: with Lamar Jackson and Derrick Henry, the Ravens are two-dimensional in everything they do. Buffalo has to control the line of scrimmage. Oliver’s quickness and disruption inside could be a game-changer.

But just like Moore, Oliver’s return is still uncertain until the team confirms his status.

Now here’s where things get complicated.

Safety C.J. Gardner-Johnson is expected to miss the Baltimore game because of his one-game league suspension. We all know why: the spitting incident in Las Vegas that got him ejected. Joe Brady called it inexcusable, and now the team has to play without him.

Damar Hamlin is expected to start in his place.

That puts a lot of responsibility on Damar, especially when it comes to communication and coverage assignments against Baltimore. One mistake in the secondary could turn into a big play. But if there’s anyone you trust to handle that, it’s Hamlin. Jim Leonhard called him a true veteran who communicates well and doesn’t get enough credit for everything he’s asked to do. And when Gardner-Johnson was ejected in Las Vegas, it was Damar who stepped in and calmed everything down, making some huge tackles in the process.

And this week, Brady made an important point about the safeties. He said he doesn’t want any one group feeling like they have to be superheroes. He wants everybody playing within the system, trusting one another. For Damar, that means doing his job, making the right calls, and letting the defense work around him.

There’s another major absence, this time on offense. Right tackle Spencer Brown has been ruled out with a back injury.

That’s a serious concern for Josh Allen’s protection. Buffalo may need to shuffle the offensive line and give extra blocking help against Baltimore’s pass rush. And remember, a desperate Ravens team is going to come after Josh. They’re outside the playoff picture after a loss to Cincinnati, and they know they can’t let him sit back and pick apart their secondary.

Yes, Allen can escape pressure and create incredible plays. We saw that in Las Vegas, when he slipped away from Maxx Crosby and found Keon Coleman for a touchdown. But Buffalo can’t count on him doing that all game long.

That’s another reason Moore’s potential return matters so much. More receiving options means Allen can get the ball out faster. Quick throws, screens to the backs, timing routes to Coleman and Shakir. That’s how you take pressure off a banged-up offensive line.

And it puts even more on the left side, with Dion Dawkins. Dion has been a hot topic all week after his cryptic post. His pass blocking grade has slipped this season, but his run blocking ranks fourth in the league, and his overall grade is still in the top 15. With Brown out, the Snowman needs to be at his best on Sunday.

Now for some encouraging news. Tight end Dalton Kincaid and edge rusher Bradley Chubb both briefly left the Raiders game with rib injuries. The bye gave them extra recovery time, and both are expected to return to their normal roles, though their availability still needs official confirmation.

Kincaid gives Josh another option in the passing game, especially over the middle. And Chubb can help Buffalo generate pressure from the edge, which you’re going to need against Lamar Jackson.

So overall, there’s reason for optimism. Moore and Oliver could be back. Kincaid and Chubb seem to be heading in the right direction. But Brown’s confirmed absence and Gardner-Johnson’s suspension create real challenges.

For me, the biggest question is whether the Bills can protect Josh Allen and play disciplined defense against Baltimore. Do those two things, and this team moves to six and one.

Now, our second story. And this one could have a major impact on Buffalo’s Super Bowl hopes.

The Bills have been named as a potential landing spot for Las Vegas Raiders defensive end Maxx Crosby.

And given Buffalo’s defensive struggles, it’s easy to see why.

But let’s be very clear: there is no trade agreement. There’s no indication in the reporting that Las Vegas has officially made Crosby available. This is a speculative possibility, not an announced deal.

CBS Sports analyst Jordan Dajani recently suggested Buffalo as a possible destination for Crosby, and his reasoning highlights a big concern about this team.

According to Dajani, the Bills had won four games this season despite allowing at least 30 points in each of those wins. And for comparison, the rest of the NFL had a combined record of eight wins and 42 losses through Week 6 in games where they allowed at least 30 points.

Read that again, Bills Mafia. Eight and 42. When most NFL teams give up 30, they lose. Buffalo keeps winning anyway. That tells you how incredible Josh Allen and this offense have been. But it also tells you how much they’ve been carrying this team.

And another stat makes it even clearer. After Week 6, Buffalo ranked first in the NFL in points scored per offensive drive, at 3.13. But the Bills ranked last in the league in points allowed per defensive drive, giving up 2.76.

First on offense. Last on defense. That’s the whole season in two numbers.

That’s why adding Crosby is such an appealing idea. Picture a defensive front with Maxx Crosby alongside Greg Rousseau and Deone Walker. Crosby would give Buffalo another elite pass rusher while also helping against the run.

According to ESPN metrics cited in the report, through the first six weeks, Crosby ranked eighth among qualified pass rushers with a 27 percent pass rush win rate. He also ranked sixth with a 32 percent run stop win rate. Those numbers show why he’s considered one of the most valuable defensive players in the league. He doesn’t just get after the quarterback. He shuts down the run too.

Of course, one player wouldn’t fix every problem on this defense. But Crosby could help Buffalo generate pressure without constantly sending extra blitzers, and that would make life a lot easier for the secondary. And remember what Jim Leonhard said about the Raiders game: the pass rush was hitting the quarterback, but the ball was coming out fast and the coverage wasn’t tight enough. An elite rusher like Crosby forces quarterbacks to speed up even more, and forces offenses to send extra help his way, which opens up one-on-ones for everyone else.

And here’s an interesting connection. The Bills just faced Crosby when they beat the Raiders 33 to 30 back on October 18th. He was the one bearing down on Josh Allen on that third and eight when Josh escaped and hit Keon Coleman for the touchdown. Now his name is being talked about as a possible addition to Buffalo’s defense.

But here’s the biggest obstacle.

The Raiders started the season three and oh, then lost four straight. That losing streak has fueled trade speculation. But it doesn’t mean Las Vegas wants to move Crosby.

According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, the Raiders may actually prefer to keep him. Schefter stressed how important Crosby is to the organization, and questioned whether Las Vegas could get the kind of compensation it would want. The report says the Raiders previously asked for two first-round picks during offseason discussions involving the Baltimore Ravens.

Two first-round picks, Bills Mafia. That’s an enormous price.

Now, I understand why some fans would be willing to pay it. Josh Allen is playing at an elite level, and Buffalo wants to make the most of this championship window. But general manager Brandon Beane also has to think about the future. Giving up two first-round picks would limit Buffalo’s ability to add affordable young talent for years.

And then there’s the money. Buffalo has only around four million dollars in cap space. Crosby’s contract would require major work to fit under the cap. That’s before even talking about the picks.

So the question isn’t whether Crosby is good enough. He absolutely is. The question is whether the price makes sense, and whether the Raiders would even consider moving him.

Right now, there’s no confirmed sign that Las Vegas is actively shopping Crosby.

Still, I think Brandon Beane should explore it. If the Raiders are willing to listen, Buffalo can evaluate the cost. But if the asking price is too high, the Bills have to be ready to walk away. The priority should be improving the defense without unnecessarily sacrificing the future.

And remember, there are other paths too. We talked earlier this week about cheaper, smarter targets in the middle of the defense, like Zack Baun at linebacker, who played under Jim Leonhard at Wisconsin, or safeties like Jessie Bates, Camryn Bynum, and Amani Hooker. Moves like that could help fix the communication problems in the back seven without costing two first-round picks.

And that’s where today’s two stories connect.

Getting Ed Oliver healthy could strengthen the interior of the defense. Having Bradley Chubb available could boost the pass rush. And adding Crosby, at least in theory, could take Buffalo’s defensive front to another level.

But there’s a big difference. Injured players coming back is something Buffalo controls. A blockbuster trade depends on another franchise agreeing to move one of its biggest stars, and right now, that’s still highly speculative.

With the November 10th trade deadline approaching, this is a situation worth watching closely.
