Let’s start with the criticism, because it’s been the talk of sports media this week.

Nick Wright, a well-known Kansas City Chiefs fan, took a shot at Josh Allen’s passing numbers. He challenged people to compare Josh’s passing stats this season to Patrick Mahomes’s, and see if Josh is better in even a single category. He joked that Josh is the greatest one-yard rushing quarterback in league history. But he also admitted that Josh is a devastating runner.

And then he said the part that bothered him: in his view, one quarterback is being called past his prime, and the other is favored to win MVP, and he found that strange.

Wright also laid out numbers comparing Josh to other MVP candidates. He said Josh ranks 12th in passing yards, 15th in passing touchdowns, and eighth in passer rating. He said Josh has a 99 passer rating, with 12 touchdown passes and six interceptions. And he compared that to Trevor Lawrence, with a 112 rating, 18 touchdowns, and four interceptions; Joe Burrow, with 23 touchdowns, six interceptions, and a 115 rating; and Brock Purdy, with 17 touchdowns, five interceptions, and a 110 rating.

His conclusion: he understands Josh’s rushing value, but he doesn’t think that’s an MVP season.

Now, Bills Mafia, let’s be fair. Those passing numbers are real. Josh isn’t leading the league in passing touchdowns this season. But here’s what that argument leaves out.

First, the rushing. Josh Allen just became only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards, joining Lamar Jackson, Michael Vick, Cam Newton, and Russell Wilson. He holds the record for most rushing touchdowns by a quarterback in NFL history. ESPN looked at goal line carries going back to 1977, and Josh tops the list. You can’t just set that aside. Those touchdowns count on the scoreboard the same as passing touchdowns.

Second, the context. Josh just played Monday night without his starting left tackle and left guard, against a defense that blitzed him on 68.6 percent of his dropbacks, the most he’s ever faced. And when Minnesota rushed four or fewer, he went 9 of 11 for 126 yards and a touchdown. He beat the blitz with patience, not heroics.

Third, the wins. The MVP is about the most valuable player. Buffalo is seven and one, and the number one seed in the AFC. As one analyst said this week, people around the league feel good about their playoff chances, but the thing they can’t account for is Josh Allen.

And here’s the thing about Nick Wright: even while criticizing Josh, he couldn’t deny how good this team is.

Wright called the Bills a sensational, very, very dangerous team. He said he understands why, on prediction markets, Buffalo is nearly co-favorites to win the Super Bowl, and the clear-cut favorites in the AFC.

And then he made a point every Bills fan knows all too well. He said that in the Josh Allen era, Buffalo has never had the number one seed. The Bills have hosted home playoff games, and lost some of them. But they’ve never had the AFC Championship Game in their own building. They’ve never had the first-round bye.

And right now, Wright said, they have a very real shot at it.

Bills Mafia, even Kansas City’s biggest fan expects the Chiefs to get crushed in Buffalo on Thanksgiving night. That tells you everything about where this team is right now.

Now, let’s go to Rich Eisen, who moved the seven and one Bills back to the number one spot in his NFL power rankings.

Eisen called the win in Minnesota one of the most impressive wins of the Josh Allen era. And when someone pushed back, saying “oh, you beat Carson Wentz,” Eisen had an answer.

He pointed to Hall of Fame quarterback Kurt Warner, who studied the film and said Wentz didn’t have receivers open most of the time. Warner said it was tough for Wentz to find open receivers when he didn’t even have time to see anything.

And Eisen said that means the Bills play defense. Because the receivers Wentz was throwing to were Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison, two players who can usually find a way to get open.

Wright pushed back, suggesting the Bills don’t play defense against good quarterbacks. But Eisen kept going. He said what Buffalo did on Monday night was walk into a building that was completely fired up, with the Vikings’ Gjallarhorn ready to blast every time Minnesota did something good, and beat that team. He said Buffalo beat up Carson Wentz to the point where Minnesota went and brought back Kirk Cousins.

Bills Mafia, think about that. The Bills’ defense was so dominant that the Vikings changed quarterbacks.

And the numbers back it up. Jim Leonhard blitzed only 11.5 percent of the time and still generated more pressure than Minnesota did. The Vikings went zero for the night on third down. Wentz finished with a passer rating of 8.9, the lowest by any quarterback with at least 25 attempts since 2019. ESPN had his QBR at 2.3, the lowest of any quarterback this season. And Christian Benford broke up a pass to Justin Jefferson, who spent part of the night frustrated on the bench.

And over the last two weeks, this Bills defense, the same defense critics called a liability, has allowed a combined 16 points.

And even Wright gave the Bills credit. He said Buffalo doesn’t need a great defense, just one that isn’t terrible. And he said that’s real progress. He said he isn’t going to criticize the Bills at all on this, pointing to their wins over the Lions, the Rams, the Texans, and now the Vikings. He acknowledged that even if the Texans and Rams have been a little disappointing, those are quality wins. And he pointed out that Buffalo’s only loss was a three-point loss to its division rival, the Patriots.

His conclusion: the Bills are really, really good, and they absolutely can win the Super Bowl. He said right now, on prediction markets, it’s the Bills and the 49ers at the top.

So even the skeptic says Buffalo is a real Super Bowl favorite.

Now, the newest Buffalo Bill: Amani Hooker.

After the critics said Buffalo only needed an average defense to win the Super Bowl, Brandon Beane went out and got more than average. He traded a second-round pick to Tennessee for Hooker, a player Beane compared to Micah Hyde, and whom Josh Allen called the league leader in interceptions.

And reports say Hooker arrived in Buffalo at 1 a.m.

Hooker spoke to the media for the first time this week, and a reporter pointed out that he was going from a team that was one and seven to a Super Bowl contender. So he must be sad to leave, but happy about where he landed.

Hooker said the reason he was sad to go was the relationships he built over time in Tennessee, and he said he’s very grateful for everyone he connected with there.

But he said the opportunity in Buffalo is tremendous and exciting. And then he said: it’s a dream come true. He said he’s always wanted to be in this position, with a contender.

He was asked how quickly he can be ready, since changing teams in the middle of the season is a challenge. He said pretty quickly. He said he prides himself on being a pro and being ready whenever his name is called, no matter what. He said he’s been meeting with the coaches a little extra this week to get up to speed on the style of play Buffalo wants, and then he just has to deliver.

And he mentioned that it helps having Geno Stone in the building, a familiar face from his past.

Brandon Beane said the same thing after the trade: Hooker is very smart, very football smart, and that should speed up his learning curve. Beane said he can play the deep post and track the ball in the air, which lets Cole Bishop keep playing closer to the line of scrimmage, where Jim Leonhard has been using him so well.

Then Hooker was asked what it was like preparing to face Josh Allen in the past.

He said it was always exciting. He said you always knew it was going to be an exciting game. And then he said something that every Bills fan knows to be true: if Josh ever struggled early, trust me, in the second half, he’s going to come out and take over the game.

Bills Mafia, that’s exactly what happened against Baltimore. Josh had a 39.8 passer rating at halftime and finished above 96, breaking Jim Kelly’s franchise touchdown record in the process.

And now Hooker is in the same locker room as him. Hooker said it’s been awesome. He said Josh was one of the first players he met, in the lunchroom. And he said: I’m a fan of him.

Josh mentioned that same lunch in his own press conference, saying the team’s goal is to make Hooker feel like a Buffalo Bill as quickly as possible, in a locker room he called very, very tight-knit.

Then Hooker was asked about expectations. In his last game before the trade, he had three interceptions. Should Bills fans expect that on Sunday?

He laughed it off a bit. He said it’s hard to duplicate great games like that. He said he was just doing his job, and the ball was thrown his way. But he said that if the ball comes his way enough, he expects himself to make plays on it.

And he said Tennessee’s bye week definitely helped get his body back to where it needs to be.

Bills Mafia, a safety coming off a three-interception game, joining the best team in the AFC, who calls it a dream come true. That’s a great combination.

Now, let’s step back and look at the bigger story, because something has changed in Buffalo.

For years, the narrative around the Bills was the same. If Josh Allen didn’t put on a superhero cape and throw for 350 yards, Buffalo couldn’t survive against elite competition.

That narrative died on Monday Night Football in Minneapolis.

Walking into one of the loudest stadiums in football, against the league’s top-ranked defense, without Pro Bowl left tackle Dion Dawkins and starting left guard Alec Anderson, under pressure from the opening kickoff. An older version of Josh Allen might have forced reckless throws into coverage and handed the game away.

Instead, the league saw the most dangerous evolution a franchise quarterback can make: calm, calculated maturity.

Outside of one interception, on a great play by Vikings defensive back Byron Murphy, Josh recognized early that Minnesota’s offense couldn’t move the ball without a short field. So he took what the defense gave him. He found a tight end deep on the depth chart in the end zone. He used his legs to move the chains. And he let Buffalo’s defense strangle the Vikings.

That’s exactly what Joe Brady has been preaching all season: don’t be a hero, play within the system, and play the next play. And it’s exactly what Josh said after the Ravens game, when he admitted he had been pressing, and that he needed to take a breath, settle his feet, and go through his progressions.

This version of Josh Allen, with this defense, and this running game, is a nightmare for the rest of the AFC.
