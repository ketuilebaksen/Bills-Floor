Let’s start with good news number one: the Vikings’ quarterback situation.

A reporter told Brady that the Vikings had just announced that Carson Wentz will start on Monday night. Head coach Kevin O’Connell made it official.

Bills Mafia, that’s a big deal. Earlier this season, the Vikings had Kyler Murray at quarterback. Now, for the biggest game on their schedule, against the six and one Buffalo Bills, on Monday Night Football, it’s going to be Carson Wentz.

Brady was asked about the challenge of preparing midweek without knowing who would play, and then shifting once the decision was made. He said the defense had the mindset of getting ready for both quarterbacks. He gave Wentz credit, noting that he has already played games this season and has been a good football player in this league for a long time.

Brady acknowledged that there are always some adjustments with different skill sets. But he made a great point: this isn’t like finding out on the second play of the game that the quarterback has changed and having to adjust on the fly. And he said there’s nothing here that Jim Leonhard and his staff aren’t used to handling.

And think about what just happened against Baltimore. Lamar Jackson went down, Tyler Huntley went down, and the Ravens ended up with undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano at quarterback. Leonhard adjusted in real time, and Brady said the way he called that game was outstanding. Buffalo held Baltimore to 10 points and just 68 passing yards. Now Leonhard has a full week to prepare for Carson Wentz. That’s a big advantage.

Now, good news number two: DJ Moore and Spencer Brown are back on the practice field.

A reporter asked Brady whether he expected DJ Moore or Spencer Brown to do anything in practice. Brady confirmed that both of them would be out there, and that the team would keep evaluating how things look heading into the game.

That’s huge, Bills Mafia. DJ Moore spent all of last week in a red non-contact jersey and still caught Josh Allen’s record-breaking 259th touchdown pass against the Ravens. Josh called him an absolute stud and said the more they get him the ball, the better this offense will be.

And Spencer Brown, Buffalo’s starting right tackle, has been dealing with a back injury. Having him on the field against the Vikings, one of the most aggressive blitzing defenses in football, would be a massive boost for Josh Allen’s protection.

And speaking of protection, Brady was asked about the backup swing tackle who started in Brown’s place against the Ravens. How confident is he if that player has to play extended snaps for the rest of the season?

Brady said the player showed in the Rams game that he could come into the middle of a game without hesitation, because he prepares. He said that’s the life of any backup, especially a swing tackle. You have to be ready to play left tackle or right tackle at any moment. One play you’re in for Spencer Brown, and the next play you might be in for Dion Dawkins.

Brady said this player had a tough year last season, but he has really grown working with the offensive line coaches, Pat and Austin, and has embraced this role. Brady said he understands his skill set and knows what fits him when he’s in the game. He said the player has essentially played two games now, and there hasn’t been any hesitation in terms of what the team can and can’t do with him. Brady gave him a lot of credit for his development and said he has a lot of confidence in him.

That’s great depth news, Bills Mafia. If Spencer Brown can’t go, Buffalo has a backup who has already proven he can handle it.

And now good news number three: Jordan Hancock.

A reporter mentioned that Hancock was designated to return from injured reserve. And Brady had a little fun with it. Remember, a couple of weeks ago, he mixed up the terminology with Tyrell Shavers, saying he was being activated when he was actually having his practice window opened. This time, Brady joked that he could say it correctly: they’re opening Hancock’s practice window, so nobody has to correct him and reporters don’t have to go back and fix their tweets.

Brady said Hancock would be out at practice, and the team would keep evaluating how he looks. He said he’s excited, because it’s been a while since Hancock has been able to play football. He’s been working with the strength staff, but now he gets to put pads on and get back out there.

With Gardner-Johnson gone and the secondary already banged up all season, getting Hancock back would add depth at exactly the right time.

So there’s your triple dose of great news: Carson Wentz starting for Minnesota, DJ Moore and Spencer Brown back at practice, and Jordan Hancock beginning his return.

Before we get to the C.J. Gardner-Johnson news, smash that like button. Let’s keep this video rolling, Bills Mafia.

Now, for the first time since Tuesday, Brady was asked directly about the decision to release C.J. Gardner-Johnson.

He said that, as he told reporters the week before, there was a lot to evaluate. The team got together and decided what was best for the Buffalo Bills.

And then Brady spoke from the heart. He said he has so much love for C.J. He said he really does. He loved getting to coach him. He loved being around him. He loved the edge, the personality, and allowing him to be himself as a person. And he said that didn’t make the decision easy at all.

But he explained that the team also has to be an organization that makes the right decisions. He said what happened was unfortunate, but it also opened a door and gave other players an opportunity. And seeing how those players performed was part of the evaluation.

And then Brady cleared up the timing question that so many fans had. He said the team didn’t walk out of Las Vegas thinking they would release him the following Monday. The plan was for Gardner-Johnson to be here. They were going to evaluate everything after the Baltimore game. And with some of the moving parts on the roster, they felt the move was in the best interest of the football team.

That lines up with what we heard earlier this week. Reports said the release had more to do with inconsistency on the field than with the spit itself. While Gardner-Johnson was suspended, the Bills signed another defensive back to the active roster, and when Gardner-Johnson’s suspension ended, someone had to go. Damar Hamlin and Geno Stone had just helped hold Baltimore to 10 points. So the evaluation after the Ravens game made the decision.

A reporter asked whether Brady had spoken to Gardner-Johnson since the release. Brady said yes, he was part of it. He wouldn’t share details, but he talked about the human side of moments like this.

He said there’s never anything good about it. It doesn’t matter whether it’s cut-down day, whether the player has been with the team for one day, or whether he’s a veteran. He said it’s part of the job, and you have to be honest and have those conversations. But he said that doesn’t mean he doesn’t care.

And then he shared something personal. He said he has been called into an office and told he was being let go too. So he knows what that feels like. He said he doesn’t take it lightly.

And he finished by saying that, about the person and the player he got to be around, he has nothing but love for C.J.

Bills Mafia, that’s the kind of head coach you want. Tough enough to make the hard call. Human enough to understand how much it hurts.

Now let’s talk about the challenge on Monday night: Brian Flores and the Vikings’ defense.

Brady was asked how you simulate Flores’s blitz packages in practice, given the limits on contact. He was honest. He said that no matter how you practice, it’s not going to look the same. It’s not going to have the tempo, the speed, or the physicality of a real game.

He called Minnesota an elite defense that makes you work the entire game, and said that’s why they’re number one in the league right now. So how do you prepare? Brady said you have to trust your rules and trust your technique. The communication has to be elite. On the road, it’s always going to be hard. And this defense makes every single play stressful. He said your scout team will never look exactly like the real thing at full speed, but you try to simulate it as much as possible.

Then Brady was asked what makes Flores so successful. He said Flores is one of the best in the game. He said Flores has evolved, and that it’s not the same defense every year. The scheme keeps changing. The players have a great understanding of how they’re attacking protections and coverages. There’s a clear identity in the way they play. And Brady said that if you’re a fan of football, you have to enjoy the way they play. Flores, he said, keeps reinventing himself and his defense.

That’s the same thing Josh Allen said this week. He said this defense looks completely different from what Flores ran in Miami, and even different from last year. And Josh said it’s a defense with no weaknesses, where if you turn your eyes the wrong way, you’re wrong.

Next, the crowd noise. U.S. Bank Stadium is indoors, which makes it even louder. How do you handle communication against a blitzing defense in that environment?

Brady said communication on the road starts with him. He has to get the play calls in on time, and be mindful of how long the calls are and how quickly he can get them in, so the offense can get to the line of scrimmage and make sure everyone is on the same page without sloppy footwork in the setup. Then there’s the communication with Josh, especially in the shotgun, making sure everybody is aligned. At home, Brady said, you can do a lot of those things more easily. On the road, time becomes a factor.

And here’s an interesting detail. Brady said the team knew it was going to play a lot of road games this season. So in training camp, whenever they practiced indoors or held walkthroughs, they used crowd noise, for both the offense and the defense, to learn how to communicate through the chaos.

But he admitted that no matter how loud the speakers are, nothing can truly replicate what Monday night will be like. He called it a great environment and an opportunity to show how good their communication is and how important it is to them.

Then came a fun question about Josh Allen’s famous hurdle over Anthony Barr in Minnesota in 2018. Does Brady remember seeing it, and what was his reaction?

Brady said he’s been in Buffalo long enough that there are so many plays from before his time that he’s seen on cut-ups and film. Sometimes they’ll run a play in practice, and he’ll ask Josh if he remembers a specific play from 2019 on the road, and they’ll both know exactly which one he means.

He said that, depending on who you ask, everybody has their first “Josh Allen moment,” and for a lot of people, it’s the hurdle over Anthony Barr. Brady said he has heard Barr talk about that moment. And then he joked: he hopes there are no hurdles this week. It was probably cool for fans to watch back then, but no more “hurdle avenue.”

But then he said something that really captures Josh. He said that was the moment that showed the Josh Allen experience: a player who will lay it all on the line for his teammates every single week. Brady said Josh has grown to the point where he doesn’t feel like he has to hurdle in every big moment. But he’s still the same person who will do whatever it takes to win the football game. And Brady said he remembers watching it, and that he’s fortunate he gets to see it live now.

And remember, Josh needs just nine rushing yards to become only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards, in the same building where that hurdle happened. Maybe no hurdles, Joe. But we’ll take those nine yards.

Next, the trade deadline. This is Brady’s first deadline as head coach working with Brandon Beane. How much do they communicate?

Brady said he and Beane have been working together from the very beginning: free agency, the draft, and now moves involving the practice squad and active roster. He said their office doors are right next to each other and are always open. He described really good dialogue about the team’s direction: what Brady is thinking, which players they might need to call up that week, and who would be next if something happens to someone else.

He said he has all the confidence in Beane when it comes to the roster. And then he joked that, with his offense having to block all of Minnesota’s pressures this week, he has enough to worry about without sitting in his office wondering what’s happening around the league. He said he trusts the personnel staff completely. If they feel something needs to be added to the team, they’ll do it. And if they don’t, the team will be good to go anyway.

Brady was also asked whether the deadline creates uncertainty in the locker room. He said that as professional football players, the guys understand that’s part of the life. They see the moves happening across the league. But he said the focus right now is simple: go to Minneapolis and win on Monday Night Football, and everything else will take care of itself. He said if a move were coming that affected the players, the team would be on top of communicating with them.

And now, the biggest challenge for the Bills defense on Monday night: Justin Jefferson.

Brady has a special connection here. He coached Jefferson at LSU.

He said there isn’t a route Jefferson can’t run. And then he shared something you might not expect. He said he has always told people that Jefferson was the best blocking receiver he’s ever been around. At LSU, they ran the duo run play nonstop, and Jefferson was right at the point of attack, always willing to do the dirty work. And of course, they knew how great he was as a receiver too. Brady said that’s just who Jefferson was, as a player and as a person: nonstop.

Brady gave Kevin O’Connell credit for doing an outstanding job with Jefferson. He said there’s a reason Jefferson is the best receiver in football. He said Buffalo will have to be aware of him on every single snap, because there isn’t a play in the game where he can’t take it to the house or make a big play. And he called it a great opportunity for the defensive backs.

Think about how this defense just handled Zay Flowers, Bills Mafia. Flowers came in second in the league in receiving yards per game and led the NFL in yards after the catch. He finished with zero catches on five targets. Damar Hamlin said the goal was to eliminate space. Now Leonhard has to do it again against the best receiver in football.

Brady was also asked how the offense stays disciplined and avoids penalties against such an aggressive blitzing defense. He said it comes down to technique and fundamentals, and not letting the scheme or the moment become bigger than it needs to be. Every player has to focus on his own job on each play. If you’re supposed to block the man next to you, and a different player blitzes, you have to know your technique against that blitz. He said this is an opportunity to show the team keeps growing in that area and won’t keep putting penalties on tape.

Remember, Brady said this week that he has no patience for pre-snap penalties. Against a crowd like Minnesota’s, false starts could be a real danger. So discipline will be huge.

And finally, Brady was asked about the value of having strong blockers in both the tight end room and the running back room against a defense like this. He said that versatility is part of what makes Buffalo’s offense what it is, with different personnel groups and skill sets. And he said that’s also what makes Minnesota’s defense so tough. The Vikings can be in the same personnel grouping on third and 25 as they were on the first play of the game, run the same look, and still have success, because their players understand how offenses are trying to beat them. Having smart players who can play many roles, Brady said, makes life a lot easier.

So let’s put it all together.

The triple dose of great news: Carson Wentz will start for the Vikings on Monday night. DJ Moore and Spencer Brown were back on the practice field. And Jordan Hancock’s practice window is open as he works back from injured reserve.

Brady explained the Gardner-Johnson release: the plan was always to evaluate after the Ravens game, other players stepped up, and it was best for the team. And he said he has nothing but love for C.J.

The Bills have confidence in their backup swing tackle, who has already stepped up twice this season.

Brady and Brandon Beane are in constant communication before the trade deadline, but the focus is all on Minnesota.

And the challenge is clear: Brian Flores’s elite blitzing defense, an extremely loud indoor crowd, and Justin Jefferson, the best receiver in football, whom Brady once coached at LSU.

Bills Mafia, this is the biggest test of the season so far. The number one offense against the number one defense. Prime time. On the road. But this team is six and one, the defense just played its best game of the year, Josh Allen is playing at an elite level, and the injury news keeps getting better.

My prediction? Josh Allen makes a few blitzers miss, finds the open zones behind them, and gets his nine yards to reach 5,000. The defense contains Justin Jefferson just enough. And the Bills move to seven and one on Monday night.
