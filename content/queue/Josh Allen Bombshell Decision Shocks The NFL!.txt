Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills are six and one! Buffalo put 36 points on the Baltimore Ravens and won by 26. And yet, when Josh Allen stepped up to the podium after the game, he didn’t sound like a quarterback celebrating a blowout. He sounded like a quarterback who knows this team can be even better. He was brutally honest about the first half. He talked about DJ Moore’s big return. He gave the defense its flowers. He showed real class toward the Ravens’ quarterbacks. And he opened up about a milestone, about his faith, and about the very first game of his NFL career.

This was one of the most revealing press conferences Josh has given all season, so let’s break it all down.

Welcome back to Bills Floor, your daily home for everything Buffalo Bills. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on notifications, because we cover every win, every press conference, and every storyline. Smash that like button too, it really helps the channel. And stick around until the end, because I want to know your player of the game.

Let’s get into it.

First, a fun moment to start things off. Josh was asked if he went trick-or-treating, since the game came right after Halloween. He laughed and said no trick-or-treating, he stayed at the hotel. Even on Halloween, Josh Allen was locked in.

Then came the real questions.

A reporter pointed out that despite scoring 36 points, which is a lot, the offense wasn’t at its sharpest, especially in the first half. And Josh didn’t dodge it for a second.

He said the first half was really bad by their standards. He said they didn’t play up to their standard. They weren’t throwing and catching the football the way they should. And then he put it on himself. He said he didn’t feel like he was doing a good enough job.

He pointed to the two turnovers in the first half and said that’s not winning football. And he revealed what the message was at halftime: the players talked to each other and said, we’re way better than this.

So they went back out and focused on the details. Josh said he couldn’t tell you the second-half numbers off the top of his head, but that second half is who they want to be.

And the numbers tell the story. A reporter pointed out that Josh had a 39.8 passer rating at halftime, and finished the game above 96.

Bills Mafia, think about that turnaround. That’s not a small adjustment. That’s a completely different quarterback in the second half. And that’s what makes Josh Allen so special. Even when things are going wrong, he doesn’t panic. He resets and he takes over.

The reporter asked what it took to get to that second half. Josh said it was just about talking to themselves, saying we’re better than this, trusting each other, trusting the guys on the field, and trusting the play call.

And then he admitted something that tells you a lot about him. He said that early in the game, for whatever reason, he felt like he was pressing. And he said they can’t do that.

He was asked what that means, when he says he was pressing. And he explained what he had to do to fix it: take a breath, settle down, settle his feet, and just go through his progressions.

That’s honest, Bills Mafia. The best quarterback in the league, after a 26-point win, admitting he was trying to do too much early. And it lines up perfectly with what Joe Brady has been preaching all season: play within the system, trust each other, don’t try to be a superhero. Josh took his own coach’s advice at halftime, and the result was a dominant second half.

And of course, he also made it clear that a win is a win. He said they’ll take wins whenever they can get them, whether it’s by one point or by whatever the margin was here. And then he joked that the quick math told him it was 26.

Now, the story so many of us were waiting on: DJ Moore’s return.

Moore spent the whole week in a red non-contact jersey, limited in practice, listed as questionable with that shoulder injury. And he not only played, he made an impact.

Josh was asked what Moore’s return means, and he pointed right to the big play: the deep post. He called it an unbelievable catch.

He said that’s the kind of spark Moore gives this offense. With DJ out there, they don’t always have to go on long, sustained drives. Moore gives them that downfield presence, plus the yards after the catch.

Remember, Bills Mafia, this offense was already first in the NFL in points per drive without Moore. Now add a player who can flip the field on one throw? That’s what Dan Orlovsky was talking about when he said this offense could become about as close to impossible to defend as it gets.

But Josh also mentioned ball security. He said they have to clean it up, and that Moore knows it. And then, rather than put it on DJ alone, Josh immediately pointed at himself: he said he threw an interception in this game too. So everybody has to find better ways to hold onto the football.

That’s leadership. When a teammate makes a mistake, you don’t call him out by himself. You put your own mistake right next to his.

And then Josh finished with this about DJ Moore: he’s an absolute stud, and the more they can get him the ball, the better this offense is going to be.

Before we go further, smash that like button if you were fired up to see DJ Moore back on the field. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

Next, a reporter brought up a pattern. As great as Buffalo’s offensive numbers have been, there have been stretches like this first half, and the team has eventually figured it out in almost every game. Is there a common thread when they go through those struggles?

Josh gave credit to the opponent first. He said the other team gets paid too, and that this was a really good defense with a lot of great talent on it. Remember, Joe Brady said earlier this week that Baltimore’s defense under Jesse Minter and its defensive coordinator has a lot of new wrinkles and nuances. So the Ravens came in with a plan.

Josh said that sometimes it just takes a little longer to figure out what a defense is trying to do. But he said Buffalo is really good at making adjustments: the coaching staff, the individual players, everyone. And once they feel like they understand what’s happening in the flow of the game, that’s when they start playing better.

But he was honest about the goal: they want to start faster. He said that’s no secret.

And he mentioned the next game, a Monday night game, which means an extra day to digest the film and keep getting better.

Bills Mafia, that’s a big takeaway. This team adjusts as well as anyone in football. Brady said it himself after the Raiders game: when the Raiders showed a defense nothing like what Buffalo prepared for, Josh stayed calm and figured it out. Now it’s happened again. The next step is getting those adjustments to happen faster, so the offense doesn’t need a half to find its rhythm.

Josh was also asked about one teammate who wasn’t sure all week whether he’d be able to play. Josh said he was so proud of him. He said that throughout the week, the player didn’t know if he was going, but he decided to prepare as if he was. And Josh said he played fantastic.

That’s the kind of commitment that wins in this league. Prepare like you’re playing, and when your number is called, be ready.

Now let’s talk about the defense, because Josh made sure to give them the credit they deserved.

He said, our defense balled out tonight.

Bills Mafia, after weeks of hearing about this defense giving up 30 points, giving up big fourth quarters, and ranking last in points allowed per drive, this was the response. A dominant defensive performance against the Baltimore Ravens.

Remember everything that happened during the bye week. Jim Leonhard said there were changes they couldn’t install until after the bye. He said communication was the number one priority. Joe Brady said he wanted the defense to play confidently, trust one another, and fly around. And with C.J. Gardner-Johnson suspended, Damar Hamlin started at safety. Ed Oliver came back for the first time since Week 4.

And the result? The defense balled out.

But Josh also showed real class. He said, as he had in his on-field interview, that he hopes the Ravens’ quarterbacks are okay, specifically mentioning Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley. He said you hate to see guys go down in this game. It’s an extremely violent game, and everyone understands that, but you want to play against the best.

And then he talked about the Ravens’ number 12, who came into the game. Josh said you never really expect to find yourself in that position or situation, but he made some really good throws.

That’s the respect Josh Allen has for this game and for the people who play it. He just beat you by 26 points, and his first thought is about the health of the other team’s quarterbacks.

And honestly, Bills Mafia, let’s also hope Lamar and Tyler Huntley are okay. Nobody wants to see injuries like that.

But Josh’s final word on the defense was clear: they played lights out.

Now, a fun moment from the goal line.

Josh was asked about a touchdown where things got messy and he reached the ball over the goal line. He said it was fourth down, and you just have to find a way to get the ball over the line.

He gave Baltimore credit, saying they had some really big guys in the middle and a really good plan for Buffalo’s quarterback sneak plays. Anyone who watched knows how tough it was to get through that wall.

Then he brought up another play, a throw to James Cook, whom he called “Jimbo.” Josh admitted he kind of ad-libbed on that one. And he was honest: Cook got the first down, but Josh said that was him doing too much. He said it doesn’t put the team in a great position, and he has to trust the guys up front, trust the call, and just play football.

You see the theme, Bills Mafia? Over and over, Josh is holding himself accountable for trying to do too much. That’s a quarterback who’s constantly coaching himself.

He was also asked whether coming out of the bye, he just had to get back into rhythm. Josh said he didn’t think they were playing bad football in the first half, they just weren’t executing the way they’d like. And again he came back to those two first-half turnovers: they can’t have them. So at halftime, he said, they calmed everyone down and said let’s move on and play the second half one play at a time, focusing on the details.

Now, the more personal part of the press conference.

A reporter brought up Josh’s place in Bills history, and the record he just broke as a passer, and asked whether he ever imagined this when Buffalo drafted him.

Josh said that’s the goal, right? To go out there and be the best quarterback you can be, and play good football. He said they’ve done that for quite a while now, and they have to keep doing it and keep finding ways to get better.

He said breaking records is cool. But then he made sure to share the credit. He said it’s not just him. A lot of people have put a lot of work into this and helped him get to this spot. He said he doesn’t take it for granted to be the quarterback of the Buffalo Bills.

And then he spoke about his faith. He said, as he had in his on-field interview, that God placed him here for a reason, and he’s going to keep trusting God’s plan.

Bills Mafia, that’s who Josh Allen is. Record-breaking talent, and humility right alongside it.

Then came a great trip down memory lane. A reporter brought up one of the very first games of Josh’s career: a rainstorm in Baltimore, when he came in at quarterback for the Bills against the Ravens. How long ago does that feel?

Josh said honestly, it feels like yesterday. He said he’s pretty sure he’s in year nine now, but he can still remember that game. He remembers the feel of the football, how tacky it was. He remembers the downpour going into halftime. He even remembers the first play they ran: a play called “Superman.”

He said it doesn’t feel like that long ago, but he guesses time flies when you’re having fun. And he said he loves this game, loves playing it with his teammates, and he’s going to keep having as much fun as he can.

Think about that journey, Bills Mafia. From a rookie walking into a rainstorm in Baltimore, to a record-breaking veteran beating Baltimore by 26 points at home. Year nine. And still having fun.

And to finish, there was a fun moment about where it all started: the University of Wyoming.

Josh said he saw Pistol Pete, Wyoming’s mascot, on the scoreboard. He joked that he told Kyle Allen he flew Pistol Pete in because he needed some good luck. He said the Cowboys are playing good football right now, and he gave a shoutout to a former Bills offensive coordinator now coaching in Laramie, saying he’s doing some really good work. He said Wyoming is playing really well in their conference, at five and three, coming off a bye.

Even after a huge win, Josh is still a Wyoming guy at heart. Gotta love it.

So let’s put it all together.

The Bills beat the Ravens by 26 points and move to six and one.

The first half wasn’t up to their standard. Two turnovers, a 39.8 passer rating at halftime, and Josh admitting he was pressing.

But at halftime, the message was simple: we’re way better than this. And the second half is who they want to be. Josh finished with a passer rating above 96.

DJ Moore came back and made an unbelievable catch on a deep post, giving the offense the spark it’s been missing.

The defense, after weeks of criticism, balled out and played lights out.

Josh showed class toward Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley, held himself accountable for doing too much, and shared credit for his record with everyone around him.

And now the Bills get an extra day of rest before a Monday night game.

Bills Mafia, this is what a contender looks like. Win by 26 on a day when you weren’t at your best. Have your quarterback walk off the field saying you can be better. And have a defense that finally showed what it can do after the bye.

The road to the number one seed keeps getting clearer. And if this team starts faster, the rest of the league is in trouble.
