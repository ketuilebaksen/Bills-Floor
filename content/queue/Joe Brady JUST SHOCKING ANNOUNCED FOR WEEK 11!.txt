WEEK 11 WARNING! WHY THE DOLPHINS COULD BE A TRAP GAME FOR THE BILLS!

Bills Mafia, on paper, this week looks easy. The Buffalo Bills are eight and one, the number one seed in the AFC, and they’re heading to face a Miami Dolphins team that’s in full teardown-and-rebuild mode. Most fans are already looking ahead to Thanksgiving night against the Chiefs.

But ESPN analyst Dan Orlovsky just broke down this Dolphins team, and he delivered a warning every Bills fan needs to hear. He said that if he were a contender, there are teams in the AFC playoff picture he would much rather play than Miami. He said this Dolphins defense is a real challenge. And he said the Bills secondary got beaten more than people realize against the Jets, and that Miami has a young receiver who could make Buffalo pay.

So today, we’re breaking down everything you need to know about Week 11: what makes Miami’s defense so tricky, the players to watch, the injuries that could open the door for Buffalo’s run game, and the matchup that worries the experts most.

Let’s get into it.

First, let’s talk about Miami’s defense, because it’s better than its record.

The hosts admitted this isn’t exactly the sexiest matchup of the week. The Dolphins are rebuilding. But they pointed out that Jeff Hafley’s defense is very active, with a lot of players running around and making plays on the ball. They said they were big fans of rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez coming out of college, and that he looks like the real deal.

So what makes Hafley’s scheme so difficult?

Orlovsky said there are a lot of moving parts. Players aren’t locked into specific roles. You’ll see defensive linemen drop into coverage. You’ll see defensive linemen line up as edge rushers. You’ll see interior players move outside the line of scrimmage. Nobody is where you expect them to be.

And then he said the coverages are very muddy. As a quarterback, you don’t get a clear picture before the snap. Are they rolling two safeties to one side? Are they trapping one side? Are they playing quarters? Are they reading the play? It’s just a cloudy picture.

And Orlovsky explained why that matters so much. He said defenses can either speed a quarterback up or slow him down. Minnesota, he said, does both. But Miami slows you down. You spend time just trying to figure out what coverage they’re playing.

Bills Mafia, remember how Josh Allen handled Minnesota? He held the ball for 6.07 seconds on one play and found a tight end deep on the depth chart wide open in the end zone. When the Vikings rushed four or fewer, he went 9 of 11 for 126 yards and a touchdown. Analysts said he beat the blitz with patience, not heroics. That same patience will be the key against Miami’s disguised coverages.

But here’s the part of Orlovsky’s breakdown that really stood out. He said the big thing is that Miami’s defensive line is very good right now.

He said that if he were a contender, there are teams currently in the AFC playoff picture he’d much rather play than Miami, because Miami plays so hard on defense. He said the defensive line is extremely active, both edge rushers are playing at a high level, and it’s a good front. He called it a really good challenge for Buffalo.

And one host pointed out that Miami ranks in the top 10 in a lot of defensive categories, including interceptions.

So who are the names to know?

Orlovsky said the first player who would jump out to him is Chop Robinson. He said Robinson is playing at a very high level as a speed rusher, and that Miami is creating good matchups for him and he’s winning a lot on the edge. He said Robinson’s speed is there, but his pass rush moves have also really evolved. Out of everyone on that defense, Orlovsky said, Robinson is the one who would pop off the screen the most.

He also mentioned Jacob Rodriguez again, saying he’s playing at a very high level. And he said the whole defense is full of young players flying around and making plays.

Orlovsky also pointed to veteran linebacker Jordyn Brooks, who Bills fans have seen for years. He said rookie Rodriguez is a really good linebacker. And he mentioned young safety Michael Taaffe from the University of Texas, who has stepped up and brings a lot of versatility, sometimes playing down in the box.

So yes, Miami is rebuilding. But its defense has talent.

Now, here’s the good news.

Miami’s defense has been hit hard by injuries in the past week. One host pointed out that defensive lineman Zach Sieler is on injured reserve. And then Jordan Phillips and Zeke Biggers also went on injured reserve.

He said that leaves Miami really decimated on the defensive interior. And he said he expects the Bills to run the ball and really test it.

Bills Mafia, that sounds like James Cook’s kind of game.

Think about what Cook has done the last two weeks. Against Minnesota, he carried the ball 31 times, the most by any player in any game this season, as Buffalo held the ball for more than 42 minutes. Against the Jets, he ran for 157 yards on just 18 carries, including a 74-yard touchdown that Josh Allen said was the fastest he’s ever seen Cook run. And with that touchdown, Cook scored in his eighth straight game.

Josh has said it again and again: the more touches Buffalo gets James Cook, the better the offense is. And Josh summed up the run-game plan after the Jets game in four words: we’ve got James Cook.

But Orlovsky added a word of caution. He agreed that Buffalo should be capable of running the ball against a lot of teams. But he said Miami still has a good, active front. He pointed out that just two weeks ago, Miami’s defensive line beat up the Detroit Lions’ offensive line more than anyone had all season.

So what’s his advice for Buffalo? Get into 12 personnel and 13 personnel, the formations with two or three tight ends, which Buffalo relies on a lot. He expects Buffalo to be able to run the ball. But he said he doesn’t believe this will be a walk-in, dominant offensive performance. He said this is a good defense.

Another host backed that up with the numbers. He pointed out that the 49ers scored 35 points on Miami, and the Bengals scored 31. But he said that’s about what those teams score on average anyway. In other words, nobody has gone in there and blown this defense out. He said it’s a rough defense to score points on.

His expectation for Buffalo: maybe 24 to 30 points, without a big play or a catastrophic mistake from Miami.

And remember, Buffalo’s offensive line has been dealing with injuries all season. Right tackle Spencer Brown has been dealing with an ankle injury. A rookie offensive guard was just placed on injured reserve. And just a couple of weeks ago, the Bills were without Dion Dawkins and Alec Anderson in Minnesota. But this line keeps holding up. Josh credited offensive line coach Pat and assistant Austin Gunn for the run game and the protection plan against the Jets. And Joe Brady has said that when backups go in, he doesn’t feel like he has to call a different game.

Against a Miami defense missing three interior linemen, that depth could really pay off.

Now, here’s the part of Orlovsky’s breakdown that should get Buffalo’s attention.

He said, honestly, that if the Jets had gotten better quarterback play, there was a chance of an upset last weekend.

He said he doesn’t believe Buffalo played well on the perimeter, in the secondary. He said the Bills got beaten by receivers multiple times. They just didn’t get hurt by it.

Bills Mafia, that’s an important point. Yes, the Bills defense has now gone 33 straight drives without allowing a touchdown. Yes, it held the Jets to 12 points. But Orlovsky is saying the tape shows some cracks that a better quarterback might have exploited. And remember, Chris Broussard made a similar point this week, saying the defense’s streak came against Joe Fagnano, Carson Wentz, Geno Smith, and Cade Klubnik.

And Orlovsky said Miami’s passing game could test those cracks.

He said that even though Kyle McCord is a young, relatively unproven quarterback, Miami’s young receivers are good. And he named the player Bills fans need to watch: Caleb Douglas.

Orlovsky said Douglas is a third-round pick for Miami, and a very, very good player. He described him as a long, lanky receiver who’s strong at the catch point and a really good route runner. He said Douglas is the focal point of Miami’s passing game, the player Buffalo has to stop.

He also mentioned Jalen Tolbert as another receiver to watch. And he said Miami’s offensive line is okay, and that the Dolphins do a nice job of sequencing their plays.

And then he gave the warning directly. He said that if he were Miami, he would be testing Buffalo’s perimeter players. He mentioned Davidson Igbinosun, the young corner Jim Leonhard has praised for his confidence and aggressiveness. And he said even Christian Benford got beaten a couple of times last week.

That matters even more right now, because Benford has been dealing with injuries, including a wrist issue from the Minnesota game. Joe Brady said he’d have a better answer about Benford’s status by the end of the week. And reports have suggested that if Benford can’t play, Tre’Davious White could be elevated from the practice squad.

So the secondary is the matchup to watch this week, Bills Mafia.

But there’s reason for confidence too. Amani Hooker just played 70 snaps in his first game as a Bill, the most of any Buffalo defender, after just one week of practice. And Hooker said that when Buffalo builds a lead and opponents have to throw, it’s a DB’s dream, with greedy opportunities to pick the ball off. If Miami tests Buffalo’s perimeter, it might be throwing right into the hands of the league’s co-leader in interceptions.

Now, Miami’s run game.

The Dolphins have been without De’Von Achane since Week 3. He’s out for the year. And that’s a huge loss for any offense.

So what does Miami’s run game look like now?

Orlovsky said that when Miami runs the ball in a traditional way, it’s mostly Ollie Gordon. He said Gordon is a good player and a big back who’s best in Miami’s zone scheme, when he can press the hole and cut back. And when he gets going, he’s tough.

Orlovsky made it clear he wasn’t putting Gordon in the same conversation as Derrick Henry. But he said there’s a similar idea: when Gordon gets his shoulders squared and his feet aren’t stopped in the backfield, he’s a big back who can get downhill on you.

Bills Mafia, that’s where Buffalo’s run defense comes in. Remember, the Bills rank third in the NFL in allowing only about half a yard before contact, and in the top five in stuff rate. Ed Oliver and Deone Walker have made it extremely hard for teams to run inside. And Greg Gaines, who ranks 10th among NFL defensive linemen in double-team rate on pass rush snaps, is doing the dirty work up front. If Buffalo can stop Gordon in the backfield before he gets his shoulders squared, Miami’s offense will become one-dimensional.

When Miami goes to its perimeter run game, its screens, run-pass options, and spread looks, Orlovsky said it’s mostly Jaylen Wright, who brings more athleticism. And Miami will also sprinkle in Malik Washington when it wants more speed in space.

And finally, Miami’s quarterback situation.

The Dolphins started the season with Malik Willis at quarterback, and now Kyle McCord is under center. So what changes?

Orlovsky said not much. He said Willis was playing at a high level too, and that Willis is more of a playmaker. He said Willis had a better understanding of the timing in Miami’s passing game, especially its play-action game, which Miami loves to use. But he said McCord operated the offense relatively cleanly, with a bit more of the quarterback keeper game. Overall, Orlovsky said, the offense isn’t going to change much.

And he said the play-caller to know is Bobby Slowik, who was in Houston a couple of years ago. Slowik comes from the Shanahan coaching tree, where the goal is to marry the run game and run action, the play game and play action, and hit big crossing routes in the middle of the field, using three-level passing concepts.

Joe Brady mentioned McCord this week too. He said Buffalo liked him a lot coming out of college, called him a really good football player, and said he did what he had to do in Miami’s last game, giving the Dolphins a chance to win. Brady said it will be a huge test for the Bills.

And remember Brady’s message about this game. He said the Dolphins are an extremely well-coached team, that they had an incredible draft, and that Jeff Hafley has been incredible at every stop. He said that when you watch Miami’s tape, you see seven-point games in the fourth quarter and the Dolphins holding leads. And he said it drives him crazy when people call any game “winnable,” because there’s never a week when he works any less, and every opponent plays its best against Josh Allen.

Now we know why Brady was being so careful.

So let’s put it all together.

Miami may be rebuilding, but Jeff Hafley’s defense is active, disguised, and hard to read, and it slows quarterbacks down.

Dan Orlovsky said Miami’s defensive line is very good, that both edges are playing at a high level, and that Chop Robinson is the player who pops off the screen.

But Miami’s defensive interior has been decimated by injuries, with Zach Sieler, Jordan Phillips, and Zeke Biggers all on injured reserve, so expect Buffalo to test it with James Cook.

Orlovsky expects Buffalo to run the ball, but said this won’t be a walk-in, dominant performance. One host expects Buffalo to score somewhere between 24 and 30 points.

Orlovsky warned that Buffalo’s secondary got beaten on the perimeter against the Jets, and that Miami should test it, especially with rookie receiver Caleb Douglas.

And Miami’s run game, without De’Von Achane, will lean on Ollie Gordon, with Jaylen Wright and Malik Washington on the perimeter.

My prediction: James Cook takes advantage of Miami’s injuries on the interior. Josh Allen stays patient against the disguised coverages. Amani Hooker makes a big play in the secondary. And the Bills win, move to nine and one, and head into Thanksgiving night against the Chiefs with all the momentum in the world.
