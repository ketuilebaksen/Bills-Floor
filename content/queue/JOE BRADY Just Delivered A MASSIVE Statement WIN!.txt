Before we break down the plan, let's remember the conditions, because they shaped everything.

It was pouring rain on Thanksgiving night in Buffalo. The temperature was 33 degrees. The wind was blowing at 19 miles per hour. The wind chill was 22.

Throwing the football was a mess. Catching it was a mess. Holding onto it was a mess.

And a lot of coaches would have stuck with their normal game plan anyway, trying to force the passing game because that's what they do every week. Joe Brady didn't.

Number one: Joe Brady read the weather and leaned on James Cook.

Brady saw the conditions and made a decision: this game was going to be won on the ground.

Buffalo ran the ball 43 times. James Cook carried it 32 times for 117 yards and a touchdown. Thirty-two carries ties his career high. And 117 yards is the most any player has rushed for against Kansas City all season.

Buffalo held the ball for almost 37 minutes.

Think about what that does to an opponent. Every minute the Bills had the ball was a minute Patrick Mahomes spent on the sidelines. Every carry wore down Kansas City's defense in the cold and the rain. And it gave Buffalo's own defense time to rest.

Dan Orlovsky posted after the game that it was incredible game management by Joe Brady. And he was right. Brady saw the weather, leaned on the run, and still let Josh Allen pick his spots.

And remember, this wasn't a one-time thing. Just a few weeks ago, in Minnesota, against the most aggressive blitz Next Gen Stats has ever tracked, Brady handed Cook the ball 31 times, the most by any player in any game this season, and Buffalo held the ball for more than 42 minutes. Brady has shown over and over that he'll do whatever the game requires.

Cook's touchdown on Thanksgiving night showed this team's resilience too. Early in the second quarter, Cook looked like he had scored from the three-yard line. Replay ruled him short at the one. Then Dion Dawkins was flagged for a false start, pushing Buffalo back to the five. Bills beat writer Matt Parrino called it a phantom false start, and it really didn't look like Dawkins moved early.

But on the very next snap, Cook ran it in from five yards out anyway. Bills 7, Chiefs 0.

Something goes wrong, and this team just keeps coming.

Number two: Joe Brady stayed patient, and trusted his team when things went wrong.

And things did go wrong.

Kansas City came in with the best red zone defense in the NFL. Teams scored touchdowns only 36 percent of the time once they got inside the Chiefs' 20.

On the first interception, the Bills were at the Kansas City nine in the second quarter. Josh's pass was tipped at the line of scrimmage, and Alohi Gilman caught it in the end zone. Then Harrison Butker kicked a 26-yard field goal with two seconds left in the half. So instead of Buffalo pulling away, it was just 7 to 3 at halftime.

On the second interception, Buffalo was at the Kansas City 14 in the third quarter. A screen pass slipped right through Khalil Shakir's hands, and Nohl Williams took it away.

Two trips deep into Chiefs territory. Zero points. Take away those two plays, and the game isn't close.

Plenty of coaches would have panicked. They would have abandoned the plan, started forcing the ball downfield, or tried to make up for the mistakes all at once.

Joe Brady didn't. He stuck with the plan. He kept running the football. He kept trusting his defense. And he kept believing his team would find a way.

And after the game, Brady said the line that should scare the rest of the league: winning a game when Josh Allen had 80 passing yards says a lot about this football team.

Number three: Joe Brady used Josh Allen's legs at exactly the right moments.

Remember the buildup to this game. Against Miami, Josh didn't have a single rushing attempt, the first time that had happened in a full game in his career. And Chiefs defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo called it a setup. He said he expected Buffalo to run a good number of designed quarterback runs on Thanksgiving night.

He was right.

Josh ran seven times for 43 yards. And when he took off, it mattered. In the third quarter, he spun out of trouble and picked up a first down with his legs. That's the Josh Allen who can still help you win when the passes aren't falling.

And it helped Buffalo on third down, where the Bills went nine of 16.

Not everyone loved every decision. A to Z Sports argued that Brady drifted away from the run at times. That's fair. But when you hold the ball for almost 37 minutes against Patrick Mahomes and win, you're doing something right.

Number four: Joe Brady and Jim Leonhard found the defensive recipe.

When Brady hired Jim Leonhard, a lot of people wondered how long it would take for the defense to come together. Early in the season, Buffalo ranked near the bottom of the league. Now, it's one of the scariest units in football.

On Thanksgiving night, Kansas City went just two of 11 on third down.

I think Brady and Leonhard have found the recipe: run the ball, win on third down, and make the other team beat you with nothing easy.

Just look at one play. Bradley Chubb blew up Emmett Johnson in the backfield and forced a punt. That's the kind of play that kept Kansas City stuck all night.

And here's something remarkable. Patrick Mahomes didn't throw a single interception. Kansas City had zero turnovers. Mahomes went 16 of 29 for 174 yards and zero touchdowns. He took care of the football, and Buffalo beat him anyway.

And this wasn't a pass rush win. Last November, when Buffalo beat Kansas City 28 to 21, the Bills had three sacks and 15 quarterback hits. On Thanksgiving night, they had just one sack and six quarterback hits. Greg Rousseau got the sack, on third and 16, and he had three of the six hits.

So this was a coverage win. Buffalo made Mahomes work for every yard.

Travis Kelce caught four passes for 71 yards and led the Chiefs in receiving. But he never found the end zone, and neither did any Chiefs receiver. Kansas City had zero passing touchdowns.

Without Kenneth Walker III, Emmett Johnson started at running back and ran 17 times for 72 yards. Kansas City ran for just 74 yards as a team. Johnson did score from the one-yard line in the third quarter, and that touchdown ended an incredible Bills streak: 17 straight quarters without allowing a touchdown.

Seventeen quarters, Bills Mafia. And when your defense can win without piling up sacks, it doesn't need a perfect night to beat anybody.

And then there's Amani Hooker, the safety Brandon Beane traded a second-round pick to acquire.

With 1 minute and 51 seconds left in a 10 to 10 game, Kansas City had third and 10 at its own nine. Mahomes hit Rashee Rice for 11 yards and a first down. Then Hooker laid him out.

On the field, it was ruled a catch. But the league office in New York stepped in, and replay overturned it to incomplete, ruling that the tip of the ball hit the ground when Hooker made contact.

Instead of a fresh set of downs, with all three timeouts, Kansas City had to punt from its own nine. Andy Reid wasn't happy. He said the call was not clear and obvious.

And that hit is exactly why Micah Hyde expected Hooker to be a ball hawk in Buffalo. Offenses have to know where he is on every snap. If they don't, he makes them pay.

Number five: Joe Brady's team won the discipline battle.

The referees didn't decide this game. Discipline did.

Kansas City was flagged eight times for 68 yards. Buffalo was flagged just three times for 24 yards.

And Kansas City's penalties came at the worst possible moments. On a late drive in the fourth quarter, a Chiefs holding penalty wiped out a 10-yard run by Emmett Johnson. Then came a false start. And then Harrison Butker missed a 46-yard field goal wide right with 6 minutes and 1 second left.

Bills Mafia, remember where this team was earlier in the season. Jim Leonhard said back in October that Buffalo was 31st in the league in penalties. Joe Brady said he had no patience for pre-snap penalties, and that the team had to clean them up. He talked about it after almost every game.

On Thanksgiving night, against the Chiefs, Buffalo was the disciplined team.

That's coaching.

And then came the ending, where Joe Brady's special teams philosophy paid off.

Brady has said all season that special teams represents the ethos of this football team. He said one metric site ranks Buffalo's special teams number one in the league. He said that if you want to see what kind of team you want to be, you watch your special teams.

On Thanksgiving night, special teams won the game.

Early in the fourth quarter, Tyler Bass tied the game at 10 with a 54-yard field goal, in the rain.

After the Rice punt, Buffalo's drive stalled. Dalton Kincaid was called for offensive pass interference, and Josh was sacked. So Brady used his timeouts to stop the clock, trusting his defense to get one more stop.

And it did.

In the final 20 seconds, Kansas City punted again. And "Buffalo Joe" became Super Joe. Joe Andreessen got a piece of Matt Araiza's punt.

Then Greg Dortch caught it and ran it back 17 yards, to the Chiefs' 39-yard line. Do the math: without those 17 yards, Bass isn't kicking from 57. He's kicking from more than 70. Every single yard of that return mattered.

And then Tyler Bass lined up from 57 yards, in the rain, as time expired.

Money. Bills 13, Chiefs 10.

With that kick, Bass became the first kicker in NFL history to make both a game-tying and a game-winning field goal of 54 yards or longer in the fourth quarter of the same game. And the 57-yarder was the longest field goal in franchise history.

So let's put it all together.

Joe Brady delivered a massive statement win, beating Andy Reid and the Chiefs 13 to 10 on Thanksgiving night.

He read the weather and leaned on James Cook, who carried 32 times for 117 yards and a touchdown, as Buffalo held the ball for almost 37 minutes.

He stayed patient after two red zone interceptions, and said that winning with Josh throwing for 80 yards says a lot about this team.

He used Josh's legs at the right moments, with seven carries for 43 yards and a 9-of-16 night on third down.

With Jim Leonhard, he built a defense that held Kansas City to two of 11 on third down, 74 rushing yards, and zero passing touchdowns.

His team won the discipline battle, with just three penalties to Kansas City's eight.

And his special teams delivered the ending: a blocked-punt tip from Joe Andreessen, a 17-yard return from Greg Dortch, and a franchise-record 57-yard field goal from Tyler Bass.

The Bills are 10 and one for the first time since 1991, with seven straight wins. And Joe Brady, in his very first season as a head coach, has his team in control of the AFC.

Here's my take, Bills Mafia: the Buffalo Bills are playing the best all-around football in the NFL right now. They have speed. They have grit. They hit people, like Amani Hooker on Rashee Rice. They grind, like James Cook with 32 carries in the rain. And they find ways to win.

And the scariest part? They can still get better. Josh Allen isn't going to throw two red zone interceptions every week.
