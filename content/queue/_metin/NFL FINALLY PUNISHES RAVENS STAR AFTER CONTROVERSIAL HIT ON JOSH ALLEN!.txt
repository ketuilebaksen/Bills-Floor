But the punishment wasn’t only about what happened to Buffalo’s quarterback. Smith was caught up in another incident in that same game, against another Bills player. And the total amount he now has to pay is turning a lot of heads.

There’s also a detail about Smith’s history this season that makes this even more serious. And a lot of Bills fans are saying the punishment doesn’t go nearly far enough.

First, let’s go back to the play.

Buffalo’s dominant 36 to 10 win over the Baltimore Ravens. It’s fourth down at the goal line. Josh Allen keeps it on a quarterback sneak, pushing through the pile and reaching the ball over the goal line for a touchdown.

But in the middle of that pile, Roquan Smith grabbed Josh Allen’s face mask and violently twisted his head.

And somehow, no flag was thrown.

Bills fans were furious, and you can understand why. A face mask penalty is supposed to be one of the easiest calls in football, because it’s about player safety. Twisting a player’s head by the face mask can cause serious neck injuries. And this was the franchise quarterback, the man who would break Jim Kelly’s franchise touchdown record later in that same game.

And look at how Josh responded. He got back up, pushed a Ravens defender, and kept playing. He didn’t lose his composure. He didn’t let it take him out of the game. He finished with two touchdown passes and a rushing touchdown, including the record-breaking throw to DJ Moore and another to Dalton Kincaid.

After the game, Josh talked about how tough that goal line was. He said it was fourth down and you just had to find a way to get the ball over the line. He gave Baltimore credit, saying they had some really big guys in the middle and a really good plan against Buffalo’s quarterback sneak plays.

But the cameras told another part of the story. Joe Brady said he saw Josh get up with marks on his face.

Now, the NFL’s decision.

The league eventually stepped in. Roquan Smith received a fine of 17,911 dollars for unnecessary roughness involving Josh Allen.

But that wasn’t all.

Smith was hit with a second fine, another 17,911 dollars, for striking Bills wide receiver DJ Moore while Moore was defenseless over the middle of the field.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. DJ Moore spent the entire week before that game in a red non-contact jersey, limited in practice with a shoulder injury. He was questionable right up until game day. He came back, made an unbelievable catch on a deep post, and caught Josh Allen’s record-breaking 259th touchdown pass. And during that same game, he took a hit while defenseless that the league decided was worth a fine.

Add it all up, and Roquan Smith is paying a total of 35,822 dollars for his actions against Buffalo in a single game.

And here’s what makes this even more concerning.

This was not Smith’s first face mask incident this season.

Back in Week 2, Smith committed a similar infraction against New Orleans Saints quarterback Tyler Shough. The NFL fined him 11,941 dollars for that play.

That makes the Josh Allen incident Smith’s second face mask-related offense this season. Against two quarterbacks.

When you add in the Week 2 fine, Smith has now been fined well over 47,000 dollars this season for incidents involving opposing players.

Now let’s look at how the Bills reacted.

Joe Brady was asked about the play the day after the game, specifically from a player safety perspective. And his answer was measured, but it said a lot.

Brady said he didn’t know anything about it during the game. And then he revealed something important: if he had known about it in the moment, it probably would have affected some of the quarterback sneak calls later in the game, and how those ended up playing out.

That’s a big statement, Bills Mafia. Brady is saying that if he’d seen his quarterback getting his head twisted in that pile, he might have thought twice before calling more sneaks and putting Josh back into that situation.

Brady said that, whether it was intentional or not, it’s obviously not something you want to see in football. He made a point of calling Smith a great football player. But he said he saw Josh getting up with marks on his face, and at the time he thought it was just part of a quarterback sneak. And then he said he was surprised nothing got called. But, he added, that’s not his place.

That’s exactly how Brady handles things. He doesn’t go after the officials. He doesn’t attack the opposing player. But he makes his point clearly: that’s not acceptable, and it should have been flagged.

And Josh? When he was asked about it a few days later, he kept it very simple. He said he doesn’t think that kind of thing happens very often at the goal line. But he said it’s football, it’s a physical game, and stuff like that is going to happen.

No complaints. No drama. No calling for a suspension. That’s Josh Allen. He lets his play do the talking. And in that game, his play said plenty: two touchdown passes, a rushing touchdown, a franchise record, and a 26-point win.

But not everyone in Buffalo is taking it as calmly as Josh.

A lot of Bills fans believe the punishment doesn’t go far enough. Dave Meyers of the “Built in Buffalo” podcast didn’t hold back. He said Smith should be suspended for trying to pull Josh Allen’s head off.

And that’s exactly where the controversy continues.

Some fans believe Smith deserved a suspension, especially given that this was his second face mask incident of the season, and that he was fined for a hit on a defenseless receiver in the same game.

So let’s talk about that debate, because there are two sides to it.

On one side, the case for a suspension.

This was a repeat offense. Smith was already fined for a face mask on a quarterback in Week 2. Then, in Week 8, he did it again, this time to Josh Allen, twisting his head during a goal line sneak. In that same game, he was fined for hitting a defenseless receiver.

That’s three fined plays in one season, two of them involving the face masks of quarterbacks. If the purpose of discipline is to change behavior, and the first fine didn’t stop it, then the argument is that the league has to escalate.

And Bills Mafia, think about the stakes. Josh Allen isn’t just Buffalo’s quarterback. He’s widely considered the best player in the league. National analysts have said this team can win the Super Bowl because it has the best player in football. One wrong twist of the neck in that pile, and Buffalo’s entire season could change in a second.

Player safety has been one of the NFL’s biggest talking points for years. If the league is serious about it, critics say, repeat face mask violations against quarterbacks should come with more than a fine.

Now, the other side.

The NFL usually reserves suspensions for the most flagrant or dangerous plays, or for players with long histories of repeated violations. A fine for unnecessary roughness is a standard response, even for a repeat offense. And there’s no flag on the play, which some people will argue makes it harder for the league to justify a suspension, even though fines can be issued for plays that weren’t penalized.

Joe Brady himself said, “whether it was intentional or not.” Some people will argue that in a goal line pile, with bodies everywhere, it’s not always clear what’s intentional. And Josh Allen’s own reaction, saying it’s part of a physical game, shows that even the player who got hurt isn’t calling for more.

And honestly, Bills Mafia, the fines themselves are not small. When you add up the Week 2 fine and the two from this game, Roquan Smith has been hit with tens of thousands of dollars in fines this season. The league is clearly sending a message, even if it stopped short of a suspension.

So where do I land?

I think the fines were the minimum. A second face mask offense against a quarterback in the same season, plus a hit on a defenseless receiver in the same game, should get a player’s attention. If it happens a third time, I don’t see how the league avoids a suspension.

But I also think the bigger issue is that no flag was thrown. That play happened in front of officials at the goal line, during one of the most-watched moments of the game. That should have been a penalty on the field. And if it had been, maybe we wouldn’t be having this conversation.

Now, let’s put this in the context of the bigger picture, because the Bills have bigger things ahead.

DJ Moore came back from injury and caught the record-breaking touchdown. Ed Oliver returned for the first time since Week 4 and flew around the field.

So in a game full of great moments, the Roquan Smith incident is the one ugly mark. And the good news is that Josh Allen came out of it fine, kept playing, and kept dominating.
