JOE BRADY DROPS BIG UPDATES BEFORE RAVENS GAME! BUFFALO BILLS NEWS TODAY!

Bills Mafia, the bye week is over, the Baltimore Ravens are coming to town, and Joe Brady just stepped to the podium with a ton of news. A major update on DJ Moore. Roster moves in the secondary. Lamar Jackson’s status. Tre’Davious White’s role. A strong defense of one of his players in a controversy around the league. And a look back at that unforgettable 41 to 40 game against Baltimore. There’s a lot to unpack here, and every bit of it matters heading into Sunday.

Let’s get into it.

Brady opened by talking about the week ahead. He said the team had a good week of practice to clean some things up, and he’s looking forward to seeing how they execute on Sunday.

But then he said something I think is really important. He said there are obviously a lot of ways for this team to get better, but he doesn’t want that to make anyone lose sight of all the good they’ve done.

He talked about the things that separate this team from others: the effort, the running to the football, helping teammates up off the ground, all the little things. And he warned that sometimes, when you focus too much on what you have to fix, you end up sacrificing those things. So the challenge is to keep improving technique and details, clean things up, and still hold onto what he believes separates Buffalo from a lot of teams.

Bills Mafia, that’s a smart message coming out of a bye week. Yes, the defense needs to be better. Yes, the penalties need to come down. But this team is five and one. It’s doing a lot of things right. You don’t tear that down. You build on it.

Now, the news everybody has been waiting for. DJ Moore.

Brady was asked about Moore, who sat out the Monday night game against the Rams and the Raiders game. How has that time set him up to get back out there this week?

Brady said getting to know DJ and his personality has been great, and that he’s as tough as they come. He said DJ is the kind of guy who says a lot without saying a lot. Brady admitted it was tough for Moore not to play in those games, and that it was frustrating for him. But he said it was the best thing for him.

And then came the line Bills Mafia wanted to hear. Brady said it would be nice to get Moore back on the football field that day and see how it goes as they progress. He talked about the importance Moore brings to the team and the offense: the toughness, the playmaking ability.

Think about what that could mean on Sunday. Buffalo has been scoring around 30 points a game without its number one receiver. Keon Coleman has scored in three straight games and is leading the league in EPA per target. And now you might add DJ Moore back to that group, against a Baltimore defense that CBS analysts said has been struggling in coverage at every level.

Dan Orlovsky said it this week: with Coleman and Moore both on the field, this offense becomes about as close to impossible to defend as it gets. We might be about to find out.

Next, Brady was asked how he, Jim Leonhard, and the defensive staff handled the self-scout during the bye and found ways to improve the defense.

Brady said that offense and defense approached everything the same way. He went into each room, turned on the tape, watched it with the coaches, and talked through everything, with one goal: be about solutions.

And he made a point of saying that, while he knows the questions are mostly about the defense, the process was no different on offense. He said there’s so much good on tape. But there’s also too much of a gap between how the team performs on schedule versus off schedule, and on third and long versus third and medium. So they looked at how to clean that up from a scheme standpoint, and at what they need to do better with fundamentals and technique.

He said there’s a clear vision of what’s expected and where they need to go. He called Leonhard and the defensive staff outstanding throughout the process. And he said he’s excited to see the players keep buying into the communication and collaboration, and to see what it looks like on Sunday.

Communication and collaboration. Bills Mafia, those are exactly the words we’ve been hearing from Leonhard all season. He said the secondary looked confused too often. He said communication was the number one thing to fix. Now Brady is saying the staff has a clear plan, and Sunday is when we see it.

Then came some roster updates.

Brady was asked about Jordan Hancock, who becomes eligible to come off injured reserve this week. Brady said they’re going to keep evaluating him, and that he wouldn’t be out there for practice that day.

He also said Tyrell Shavers would be out at practice. At first, Brady said they were activating Shavers, but a reporter asked him to clarify: was Shavers being added to the 53-man roster, or was his practice window being opened? Brady corrected himself. They’re opening his window. And he joked that he’s still learning the terminology.

I love that moment, Bills Mafia. A first-year head coach laughing at himself in front of the media. That’s a guy who’s comfortable in his own skin.

Now, the secondary. A reporter pointed out that with C.J. Gardner-Johnson suspended for this game, Buffalo needs better play from its safeties, whether that’s Cole Bishop, Damar Hamlin, or whoever is back there. The last line of defense has to be better.

Brady’s answer was really thoughtful. He said everybody has to have the mindset that they have to play better. But he explained why he doesn’t like singling out one position group.

He said that when you focus on one group, it can create a mindset where players stop trusting each other. If you’re a cornerback and you hear that the safeties have to play better, you might stop trusting the safety behind you. And then, before you know it, you feel like you have to be a superhero. You stop playing within the system.

To Brady, it’s collective. Safeties, outside linebackers, corners, it doesn’t matter. Everybody has to play within the system, trust one another, and not feel like they have to elevate their game on their own.

He did say the defense wants to make more plays on the football in general, and that naturally shows up most in the defensive backfield, where interceptions happen. And he said they have to clean up their tackling at every position. But he doesn’t want any one room to feel like they’re letting the team down and have to carry everyone on their backs, because that’s when players start playing outside the system.

Bills Mafia, that’s a coach who understands people. Think about how many times you’ve seen a defensive back try to make the hero play, jump a route he wasn’t supposed to jump, and give up a touchdown. Brady wants the opposite. Trust the call. Trust the guy next to you. Play your job.

Now, Lamar Jackson. A reporter said Lamar looked like he was practicing, and asked about the challenges that come with facing a team led by Lamar Jackson.

Brady first gave credit to Tyler Huntley, who did an incredible job filling in for Baltimore. But then he said there’s a reason Lamar is an MVP and one of the best players in the world. Pair him with Derrick Henry, whom Brady called another Hall of Famer, and an offense that’s been well coached, and Baltimore is two-dimensional in everything it can do.

He said it’s going to be a huge task for the defense, but also a great opportunity.

And that’s the right way to look at it. If Lamar plays, this is exactly the kind of test that shows what this revamped defense is really made of. Lamar’s legs. Henry’s power. A desperate team outside the playoff picture. If Buffalo’s defense can handle that, it can handle anybody.

Next up: Tre’Davious White. Brady was asked why the team brought him back.

He said Tre played really good football last season. The team has been battling injuries in the secondary, so this is an opportunity, just like with Shaq Thompson, to have a veteran presence who will keep working his way back. And depending on how things shake out, there might be a chance for him to be called up.

But Brady was clear: nothing is guaranteed. There are no special expectations. He said fans would see White out there on special teams and on the scout team. White understands what he’s coming here to be. He can be a voice in that meeting room and help the young players. But he also has to get himself ready, because it’s a long season. And if the opportunity comes up, Brady said, everyone knows the faith and confidence they have in White.

That’s exactly what we thought, Bills Mafia. White isn’t walking in as a starter. He’s walking in as a mentor, a depth piece, and an insurance policy. But with Gardner-Johnson suspended and injuries in the secondary, that opportunity might come sooner than people think.

Then a reporter brought up something interesting. Buffalo has a long history of playing well coming out of bye weeks. What has Brady learned about why?

Brady said he tries not to compare things to the past. But he explained his philosophy for the bye. You don’t want to get too crazy and feel like you have to change everything. He said sometimes more time isn’t necessarily a good thing. Instead, it’s about being proactive in your development, looking at things with an open mind, but not acting like everything is broken.

His approach is simple. Here’s what we’re doing well. Here’s what needs to improve. Here are the solutions. And then the expectation is that it shows up on tape.

Bills Mafia, that’s a balanced, steady approach. No panic, no overreaction. Just honest evaluation and clear solutions.

And then came a question that brought back some incredible memories. That 41 to 40 game against Baltimore.

Brady was the play-caller in that game, and he was asked what sticks out when he looks back on it.

He said that when you have Josh Allen, you’re never out of a football game. And he said it speaks to the resilience of this team. He said there’s never a look in the players’ eyes that says they’re out of it. Even if it doesn’t look pretty, they keep playing.

Brady said there was never a doubt in his mind, even when people were leaving the stands. Just keep playing. Keep putting the ball down and keep going. He said it’s so hard to win in the NFL, but as long as there’s time on the clock, you have a chance, and this locker room believes that until the clock hits zero.

Bills Mafia, if you were watching that night, you’ll never forget it. Fans heading for the exits. The game looking over. And then Josh Allen and this offense refusing to quit and pulling off one of the wildest comebacks you’ll ever see. That’s the identity Brady is talking about. Until it hits zero.

And on Sunday, Baltimore comes back to Buffalo. You’d better believe the Ravens remember that game too.

Now, one of the more serious parts of the press conference. Brady was asked about accusations against Sam Franklin.

And Brady came to his player’s defense.

He said it was unfortunate. He said his job is to protect his players, but when there’s fault, you take ownership. And he pointed to the C.J. Gardner-Johnson incident as an example. He called that inexcusable, said it’s not what this team is about, and said Gardner-Johnson was suspended for it, and that’s part of it.

But Brady drew a clear line between that and what happened with Franklin. He said making an accusation against a player without evidence, based on hearsay, sets a bad precedent. He said it’s his job to back his players, and if it had happened and there was proof, he’d be saying the same thing he said about Gardner-Johnson.

Brady said it’s unfortunate that Franklin’s name got thrown out there, and that he has to deal with it as if he were a dirty football player, without anyone actually knowing what happened. He said everybody needs to check their systems, and he pointed to Bradley Chubb getting fined for getting punched as another example of things that need a closer look.

Brady also noted that Franklin still got fined. He said a lot goes on during plays across the league, and making it about hearsay sets a bad precedent. He said he didn’t know if two incidents had been tied together, but he wished the process had played out differently.

But the good news, Brady said, is that Franklin will be out there this week and will get the chance to play. And as always, toe the line, but don’t cross it.

Bills Mafia, that’s a head coach standing up for his guy. When Gardner-Johnson crossed the line, Brady didn’t defend it. When he believes a player was accused unfairly, he says so. That consistency is how you earn a locker room’s trust.

And Franklin’s availability matters a lot, because he’s a big part of what’s become one of Buffalo’s biggest strengths: special teams.

A reporter pointed out that one metric site ranks Buffalo’s special teams number one in the league. Why have they been so good?

Brady gave a lot of credit to Coach Rogers. He said going back to OTAs, his message has been: if you want to see the kind of football team you want to be, watch your special teams. Watch how they fly around. Watch how they celebrate with one another. Watch how much it matters to them.

He said they were outstanding in Las Vegas. And in a team meeting last week, Brady showed every single kickoff from that game, because so many different people were making plays, and you could see the impact it had on the sideline and how happy everyone was.

He pointed to Ray Davis’s first kick return, when he took it out near Buffalo’s sideline, and how many players ran over to celebrate with him. Brady said the special teams are playing like they’re the ethos of the entire football team, and they have to keep that up and can’t take steps backward.

He credited Coach Rogers and Turner, and the leadership on those units, including Sam and Reed, who are setting the tone for who this team is going to be. And he gave a shoutout to Buffalo Joe and the rest of the group.

Bills Mafia, don’t overlook this. Tyler Bass is perfect on field goals this season. The coverage units have been flying around. Special teams can swing close games, and against Baltimore, Sunday could be one of them.

Finally, Brady was asked what qualities he wants to see more of from his defense after the first six weeks.

He said he could throw out a lot of adjectives and buzzwords, but at the end of the day, he just wants his players to go out there, play confidently, and play within the system. And then he said something I loved: if we’re going to be wrong, let’s all be wrong together.

He doesn’t want one player thinking one thing while another player is thinking something else. He wants them to trust each other and just go play football. He said they don’t need to make it harder than it is. They need to fly around, have fun, and celebrate each other.

Brady admitted there have been a lot of moving parts, especially with injuries in the back end, and that’s affected some of the communication. But he doesn’t want players overthinking it. If the call is post-high, play post-high. If it’s wrong, clean it up on the sideline. If it’s split safety, know who’s making the call and roll from there.

And he finished by saying he’s way more focused on the players playing with each other, understanding what’s expected of them, and having confidence in one another than on buzzwords.

Bills Mafia, that’s the whole plan in a nutshell. Simplify. Trust. Communicate. Play fast. Have fun.

So let’s put everything together heading into Sunday.

DJ Moore could be back, adding a huge weapon to an offense that’s already putting up around 30 a game.

The bye-week self-scout focused on solutions, not panic, on both offense and defense.

The defense is built around trust and communication, with no one trying to be a superhero.

Lamar Jackson looks like he’s practicing, which means a huge challenge with Derrick Henry alongside him.

Tre’Davious White is back as a veteran voice and depth piece, ready if his opportunity comes.

Sam Franklin will play, with his head coach firmly in his corner.

And special teams, rated number one by one metric site, could be a difference-maker.

Add in Buffalo’s history coming out of the bye, and the memory of that 41 to 40 comeback, and this is shaping up to be one of the biggest games of the season.
