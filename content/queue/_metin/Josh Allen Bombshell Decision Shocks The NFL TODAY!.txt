JOSH ALLEN SENDS A BOLD MESSAGE BEFORE MONDAY NIGHT VS VIKINGS!

Bills Mafia, it’s Vikings week. Monday Night Football. The number one offense in the NFL against the number one defense. And Josh Allen just stepped to the podium and said some things that every Bills fan needs to hear. He was honest about how dangerous this Minnesota defense is. He revealed how Buffalo plans to beat it. He talked about returning to the stadium where he hurdled Anthony Barr as a rookie, and opened up about the moment he knew he belonged in the NFL. And he made a big announcement about a project of his own that’s coming very soon.

There’s a lot to unpack, so let’s get into it.

Welcome back to Bills Floor, your daily home for everything Buffalo Bills. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on notifications, because Monday night is going to be massive and we’re covering every angle. Smash that like button too, it really helps the channel. And stick around until the end, because I want your prediction for Bills versus Vikings.

Let’s start with the biggest topic of the week: Minnesota’s blitz.

The Vikings have been blitzing at an insane rate this season. Josh was asked whether he’s ever seen anything quite like it. And his answer tells you just how seriously Buffalo is taking this defense.

Josh said it isn’t even about how much they blitz. It’s about how productive they are when they do. He said they bring pressure from both sides. They’ll blitz up the middle. And they’ll drop players into coverage who you think are coming after you.

He called it a very in-tune defense, full of very smart players who are playing really well together. He said it’s hard to game plan for a team like this, because, in his words, there aren’t any weaknesses.

And then he gave the best description of what it’s like to face this defense. He said you turn your eyes, and you’re wrong.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. Josh Allen, the best quarterback in the league according to national analysts, saying that if you look the wrong way for a split second against this defense, you’ve already made a mistake.

So what’s the answer? Josh said he has to be on top of it. He has to make sure the protection rules are exactly where they need to be. Buffalo needs a plan going into the game, and if that plan isn’t working, they have to adjust during the game.

He admitted this defense presents a lot of challenges for any offensive scheme. And he said something I loved: from a football perspective, it’s fun to watch. But from the perspective of an opposing quarterback, it’s hard to watch. He said they’re trying to figure it out, but it’s a really good system.

And remember, Bills Mafia, this offense knows how to adjust. After the Ravens game, Josh said Buffalo is really good at making adjustments, from the coaching staff to the individual players. Joe Brady said the call sheet shrinks as the game goes on, which gives Josh the chance to anticipate what’s coming. That’s going to matter a lot on Monday.

Josh was then asked if this week is even more difficult to prepare for than usual. He said yes, he thinks so. He said Minnesota shows so many different looks. They use a few different personnel packages where the same look can mean different things. So you have to know exactly who’s on the field and where they are on the field. He said that’s up to the players and the coaching staff to decipher, and the goal is to have the best possible plan going in and adjust accordingly.

That’s what makes this defense so tough. It’s not just the blitz. It’s the disguise. The same picture before the snap can turn into two completely different things after it.

Now, the matchup everybody is talking about. Josh was asked about the number one offense going against the number one defense, and whether there’s something about that kind of matchup that intrigues him.

He said yes and no. He said Minnesota is the number one defense for a reason. They’ve given a lot of good offenses fits. He said it’s going to be a hard week, and the team understands that. But he said they’re excited for the opportunity.

And then he gave a great perspective on the timing. He said that around midway through the season, teams are kind of who they are. And he called this an awesome test for Buffalo, to fight through the adversity of this game and figure it out as they go.

And then the competitor came out. Josh said that as a competitor, your reaction is: heck yeah, I want to play the best. He said he wants to go out there and prove why Buffalo is the number one offense. He acknowledged that’s statistically speaking, but he said it’s still a chip on their shoulder. They want to go out and be the best offense they can be, and they have a great opportunity against a fantastic defense.

Bills Mafia, that’s the mentality of a champion. You don’t hope to avoid the best. You want to play the best and beat them.

And think about the numbers. Through Week 6, Buffalo ranked first in the NFL in points scored per offensive drive. Dan Orlovsky said this offense can score 30 points a game. And now DJ Moore is back. He caught the record-breaking touchdown against Baltimore. Dalton Kincaid caught the next one. Keon Coleman has been on fire. The running backs have been tearing it up in the passing game. This offense has more weapons right now than it’s had all season.

But Minnesota’s defense is the real deal. Earlier this season, Stephen A. Smith pointed out that the Vikings ranked first in points allowed and second in yards allowed. And analysts have been saying all year that Minnesota’s defense is carrying that team.

So something has to give on Monday night.

Before we go any further, smash that like button if you think the number one offense wins this matchup. Let’s hear it, Bills Mafia.

Next, a reporter followed up on the idea of turning your eyes and being wrong. How do you even simulate something like that in practice?

Josh said there have been a lot of protection meetings and a lot of protection walkthroughs. And he said the Monday night game helps, because it gives the team an extra day to game plan. He said he’s sure they’ll have a few walkthroughs before the game in a conference room somewhere in the team hotel.

He said the key is getting as many reps as possible seeing what Minnesota tries to do. But he was realistic about it: Buffalo could game plan all week, and the Vikings could come out and do something completely different. So understanding the team’s base rules and attacking the defense that way is probably what they’re going to have to do.

That’s smart, Bills Mafia. You can’t memorize every blitz a defense like this might throw at you. But if every player knows his rules, knows who he’s responsible for and where the ball needs to go when pressure comes, you can handle anything.

Then Josh was asked about Vikings defensive coordinator Brian Flores. Josh faced Flores’s defenses a lot when Flores was coaching in Miami. Are there similarities?

Josh said not really. He said Flores has done such a great job of adapting. He said even compared to last year, the defense looks a lot different. And he repeated that it’s a tough scheme to figure out, and that it’s going to take all hands on deck.

That tells you something. Even a quarterback who has faced Flores many times is saying this is a new challenge. Flores keeps evolving, and Buffalo is going to have to evolve with him in real time.

Now, the stadium. Josh is returning to U.S. Bank Stadium in Minnesota, the place where he had one of the first big moments of his career. A reporter asked about the impact of going back there.

Josh said honestly, not really much. He joked that they like playing indoors, with no wind and no weather to worry about, which is always a plus.

But then he talked about the crowd. He said Minnesota is going to have a loud fan base, given the team’s record, how well they’re rolling, and how outstanding their defense has been. He said Buffalo is expecting an extremely loud crowd, and that over the next few days of practice, they’ll have speakers blasting to simulate it.

And he said that’s what he remembers most about being there early in his career: how loud it was. He also gave a shoutout to the Vikings’ famous Skol chant, calling it an awesome tradition, and said it will be a fun experience.

That’s class from Josh. He respects the environment, and he’s ready for it.

And that brings us to the famous play. The hurdle.

September 2018. Rookie Josh Allen and the Bills go to Minnesota as 16 and a half point underdogs. Josh runs for two touchdowns, throws for another, and hurdles right over Vikings linebacker Anthony Barr. The Bills win 27 to 6. Even Jim Kelly posted afterward that he could never have hurdled like that.

A reporter asked about the reaction to that play, the T-shirts, all the craziness around it. Did that surprise him?

Josh said that as a football fan, you love to see plays like that. But he admitted he had never really thought about it that much.

Then a reporter dug deeper. That was only Josh’s third game and his second start. Was that when he started to feel more comfortable at the position, or more familiar with what he could do, since it was such an instinctive play?

And Josh gave a really honest answer. He said that play honestly surprised him too. He said he was a good athlete in college, but in high school he was not. He said that as he got older and started lifting more weights, he became more athletic. And he said it was surprising to him what he was able to do, especially early in his career with the running.

He joked that he still runs a little bit now.

And then he said the line that every Bills fan will love. He said that was the moment when he thought: you know what, I do belong here.

Bills Mafia, think about that. The kid from Firebaugh, California, who had zero college offers out of high school. The kid who sent around a thousand emails to college coaches. The kid who went to junior college. The kid who almost had no place to play. And in that building in Minnesota, in his third NFL game, he finally felt it: I belong here.

And now he’s going back to that same building as the greatest quarterback in Buffalo Bills history, needing just nine rushing yards to become only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards.

Remember what he said about the running: he still runs a little bit now. A little bit, Bills Mafia. He’s the all-time leader in rushing touchdowns by a quarterback. That’s a little bit?

Next, a lighter moment. Josh was asked about the University of Wyoming, after a Wyoming representative was in the building for the Ravens game and Josh took a picture with him.

Josh said Wyoming’s season didn’t start the way they wanted, but they’ve picked up steam. He said they’re sitting at five and three and doing really well in the conference. He said they have to keep it rolling, that he loves watching them every week he can, and that he cheers them on as hard as he can.

Once a Cowboy, always a Cowboy.

Then came a big-picture question. What has Josh done throughout his career that has made him so successful?

And as always, Josh pointed to everyone else first. He said it takes the other 10 players on the field understanding and being in tune. He said that, protection-wise, Buffalo has had some great offensive line coaches who have really helped develop a protection plan each and every week.

And he said that this year, with the way they’re doing things, talking and communicating, that helps so much within the offensive scheme. It’s about understanding where the problems are, and if those problems happen, having answers ready.

And then he explained how you beat a blitzing defense, and Bills Mafia, this might be the key to Monday night. He said it helps when you can make one person miss. If you can do that, the defense is usually bringing pressure, which means there’s one less person, maybe two less people, in the back end. Now your receivers are running into some pretty healthy zone areas where there aren’t many defenders around.

That’s the whole game plan in one sentence. If Minnesota blitzes and Josh makes one player miss, there’s open grass behind it. And with DJ Moore back to stretch the field, Keon Coleman winning one-on-ones, and Khalil Shakir finding soft spots, those healthy zone areas could turn into huge plays.

Josh was also asked about the controversial goal line play from the Ravens game, when a Baltimore defender grabbed his helmet and Josh came away with marks on his face. Joe Brady said earlier this week that he was surprised nothing was called.

Josh kept it simple. He said he doesn’t think that kind of thing happens very often at the goal line. But he said it’s football, a physical game, and stuff like that is going to happen.

No complaints. No drama. That’s Josh.

And finally, the announcement. Josh was asked about the trailer that just came out for a project called “The Buffalo Miracle,” and his role in it.

Josh said it’s been a really exciting project to be part of. He said the story itself is fantastic, and he’s excited for the premiere coming up on the 17th. And he revealed that it’s the first real project that his production company has taken on.

He said it turned out really great, and he’s excited for people to see it.

Bills Mafia, that’s a new chapter for Josh Allen. Not just the greatest quarterback in franchise history, but now a producer telling Buffalo stories. Mark your calendars for the 17th.

So let’s put it all together.

Josh Allen says Minnesota’s blitz is not just frequent, but extremely productive, and calls the defense one with no weaknesses, where if you turn your eyes, you’re wrong.

Buffalo’s plan is protection meetings, walkthroughs, an extra day to prepare, and trusting its base rules so it can adjust in real time.

Josh says Brian Flores’s defense looks a lot different than it did in Miami, and even different than last year.

He’s expecting an extremely loud crowd at U.S. Bank Stadium, and the team is blasting speakers in practice to get ready.

He revealed that the hurdle over Anthony Barr was the moment he felt he belonged in the NFL.

He shared the key to beating the blitz: make one player miss, and the receivers are running into open space.

And his production company’s first project, “The Buffalo Miracle,” premieres on the 17th.
