Let’s get into it.

First, the final score. The Buffalo Bills escaped Las Vegas with a 33 to 30 win over the Raiders. Three points. That’s how close this one was. It wasn’t pretty, but a win is a win, and in the NFL you take every single one of them. Buffalo goes into the bye at five and one, and several players deserve a lot of credit for making sure the Bills came out on top.

Let’s start with the man himself, Josh Allen.

Allen finished with 255 passing yards and two touchdowns. And here’s what’s interesting about this one. In the Monday night thriller against the Rams, Allen’s legs played a big role. This time, it was all about the arm. He finished with just one rushing yard on five carries. But he protected the football and avoided the kind of unnecessary mistakes that can flip a close game. That’s a controlled, mature performance, and it’s exactly what Buffalo needed on the road on a short week.

And of course, there was that moment. Allen’s 12-yard touchdown to Keon Coleman, a ball lofted high into the air, and Coleman going up and coming down with it in the end zone. That’s what happens when your quarterback trusts his receiver to win the ball. And right now, Josh Allen trusts Keon Coleman.

Which brings us to Coleman, because he deserves his own spotlight.

Coleman led the Bills in receiving with four catches for 59 yards and that touchdown. And here’s a stat every Bills fan should know: Coleman has now caught a touchdown pass in three straight games. That makes him the first Buffalo receiver to do that since Gabe Davis in 2023.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. With DJ Moore unavailable, Coleman has been asked to take on a bigger role, and he’s answering the call. His physicality is causing real problems for defensive backs. He’s even drawing penalties when opponents can’t stay with him. He’s becoming one of the most dependable options in this offense, and Joe Brady said it himself after the game: Keon’s best is still ahead of him.

Now let’s go to the defense, where there were a few bright spots in an otherwise rough afternoon.

Cole Bishop was one of them. He finished with seven tackles and two important pass breakups. The secondary as a whole struggled, but Bishop showed better instincts and more aggressiveness than we’ve seen from him. He hasn’t turned into the breakout star a lot of fans were hoping for under Jim Leonhard just yet, but this was a strong performance to build on, and the timing couldn’t be better given everything going on in the back end.

Then there’s Tyler Bass, who had a fantastic afternoon. Bass went four for four on field goals and three for three on extra points. That’s 15 of Buffalo’s 33 points, which is half of the Raiders’ entire point total. And he’s still perfect on field goals this season. In a game decided by three points, Bass wasn’t just good, he was essential. Without him, this is a loss.

Now, Joe Brady did say the offense kicked too many field goals in this game, and he’s right that some of those drives needed to end in touchdowns. But that’s not on the kicker. When Tyler Bass is called on, he delivers. And in close games, that reliability is gold.

Christian Benford also bounced back after a difficult start to the season. He made several strong plays in man coverage, including against Raiders tight end Brock Bowers. Jim Leonhard revealed that the defense had a big plan to put Benford on Bowers, called Bowers a special player, and said Benford stepped up in a huge way. With Davidson Igbinosun and Maxwell Hairston competing for the other starting cornerback spot, Benford remains a key piece of this defense.

And then there was Dorian Williams, who made one of the biggest plays of the game. Williams recorded the first interception of his career in the second half, and it set up a Bills touchdown that stretched the lead to 23 to 13. That turnover gave Buffalo breathing room at a time when the defense desperately needed it. A first career pick, at a huge moment, on the road. That’s one Dorian Williams will remember.

Okay, Bills Mafia. Now the part that isn’t so fun. Not everybody had a good afternoon in Las Vegas.

The most concerning individual situation, by far, involved C.J. Gardner-Johnson.

Gardner-Johnson was ejected after an incident involving Brock Bowers. According to a pool report, he was thrown out for spitting at the Raiders tight end. And the NFL followed up by suspending him for one game. That’s another blow to a secondary that was already struggling.

No matter how frustrating a game gets, that kind of behavior hurts the team. Joe Brady called it inexcusable. Jim Leonhard said Gardner-Johnson is a grown man who knows he crossed the line, and that it put the defense in a really tough spot, forcing a number of adjustments at halftime just to get through the game. With the defense already having problems, losing another player only made everything harder.

Next up, Joshua Palmer, who had a disappointing day.

Palmer opened the season strong, scoring two touchdowns in his first two games. But he’s struggled to keep that production going. Against Las Vegas, he did make a 37-yard catch, but later he dropped a pass that hit him right in the hands. And after that, he wasn’t targeted again.

Some reports have suggested Buffalo should consider making Palmer inactive for Week 8 against the Ravens. But let’s be clear: that’s an opinion, not something the coaching staff has announced. I still think Palmer has a chance to contribute to this offense. But he has to be more consistent. In games this close, Buffalo can’t afford wasted opportunities.

And now the biggest concern of all: Buffalo’s pass defense.

Kirk Cousins threw for 313 yards and two touchdowns. The Bills gave up too many explosive plays, including a 44-yard touchdown pass to Tre Tucker late in the fourth quarter. That play brought Las Vegas right back into the game and exposed the same coverage problems we’ve seen too often this season.

And this lines up with exactly what the coaches have been saying. Joe Brady pointed out that the Raiders scored 17 points in the fourth quarter, and said that can’t happen. Jim Leonhard said the defense’s ultimate job is keeping points off the board, that they’re not doing it right now, and that it’s a big problem.

But the secondary wasn’t the only issue. Buffalo didn’t record a single sack. The defense had six quarterback hits, and Greg Rousseau batted down a pass, but there wasn’t enough consistent pressure on Cousins.

Now, Leonhard sees it a little differently. He said the front is getting a ton of pressure and hitting the quarterback a lot, and he put significantly more of the blame on the back seven. He also gave Cousins credit for getting the ball out fast, saying he doesn’t think Cousins has been sacked on a five-man rush all season.

So who’s right? Honestly, it’s probably both. When a quarterback has time, defensive backs are going to struggle. And when coverage isn’t holding up, the pass rush has to get home. Against the Raiders, neither unit did its job consistently enough. That’s exactly what Leonhard and the staff have to fix during this bye week, especially with Lamar Jackson and the Baltimore Ravens coming up next.

And speaking of the secondary, that brings us to today’s roster move.

The Bills have released cornerback A.J. Woods from their practice squad. That’s the exit. And in his place, they’re bringing back a very familiar face.

Tre’Davious White is returning to Buffalo.

Bills Mafia, this is a name that brings back a lot of memories. White joined the Bills as a first-round pick in 2017 and became one of the best defensive players this franchise has had in years. In 2019, he earned first-team All-Pro honors. He made two Pro Bowls. And in that 2019 season, he recorded six interceptions and 17 passes defended. At his peak, Tre’Davious White was one of the best cornerbacks in the National Football League.

He spent his first seven seasons in Buffalo before playing for the Los Angeles Rams and the Baltimore Ravens in 2024. Then he came back to the Bills in 2025, started 16 regular-season games, and recorded 40 tackles, 10 passes defended, and one interception.

And now he’s back for a third stint with the organization.

Here’s a detail I really like. According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, White had opportunities with other teams, but he chose Buffalo. For a player with his history here, that connection to the organization clearly means something. He wanted to come home.

And think about the timing. White played for Baltimore in 2024. The Bills face Baltimore in Week 8. That’s a veteran who has been inside that Ravens building.

But there’s an important detail about this signing that we need to be clear on. White is joining the practice squad. He’s not immediately taking over a starting role. The expectation is that he’ll spend time learning Jim Leonhard’s defensive system before potentially being promoted to the active roster.

That distinction matters, Bills Mafia. Buffalo has not announced that White will start against Baltimore, and there’s no guarantee he’ll be promoted right away. So if you see anyone online saying he’s starting against the Ravens, that hasn’t been announced.

Still, adding an experienced cornerback makes a lot of sense given the problems we just talked about. White has dealt with significant injuries throughout his career, so the Bills will need to evaluate his physical condition and how quickly he adjusts to the scheme. But his experience could be exactly what a young, inconsistent secondary needs.

Remember what Jim Leonhard said about the coverage. He said too many times the defense looks confused, and he doesn’t think there are enough options in the scheme for them to be that confused. He said communication is the biggest thing they have to fix. Well, a veteran who has seen everything this league can throw at a cornerback, who knows this organization and this fan base, could help with exactly that. Even from the practice squad, a voice like White’s in that meeting room matters.

White gives Buffalo another option without forcing an immediate change to the starting lineup. Christian Benford remains a key part of the defense, and Davidson Igbinosun and Maxwell Hairston continue competing for snaps. Leonhard recently praised Igbinosun, calling him a confident, aggressive player who has earned a high majority of the snaps at corner. So White isn’t coming in to push anybody out right away. He’s coming in as insurance, depth, and experience.

And his return comes at a convenient time. With the bye week here, White gets a chance to study the playbook and get comfortable with the coaching staff before Buffalo faces Baltimore in Week 8.

Now let’s talk about the player who’s leaving, A.J. Woods.

His release ends a brief stay in Buffalo. Woods joined the practice squad on October 6th after spending time with the Atlanta Falcons. He played his college football at Pitt and entered the NFL as an undrafted free agent in 2024. Since then, he’s spent time with the Washington Commanders, the Philadelphia Eagles, and the Denver Broncos, but he has not appeared in a regular-season NFL game.

His release opens the practice squad spot for White, giving Buffalo a far more experienced option.

So here’s my honest take, Bills Mafia.

I like this move. Bringing back Tre’Davious White is smart. It’s low-risk, it adds experience, and it brings back a player who clearly wants to be here. But I don’t think one practice squad signing suddenly fixes this entire defense.

White can provide experience and depth. But Buffalo still needs better communication in coverage. It still needs more consistent pressure from the front. It still needs to cut down on penalties. Leonhard said he believes they’re 31st in the league in penalties. And above all, it needs to finish games. The Rams scored 20 in the fourth quarter. The Raiders scored 17 in the fourth quarter. That can’t keep happening, especially against a team like Baltimore.

The good news is that the coaches know all of this. Joe Brady said they’re treating the rest of the season like they’re zero and zero. Jim Leonhard said there are changes they couldn’t install until after the bye, and that they’re going back to re-evaluate everything. He even said that if 75 percent of the problems are on him, he’ll fix it. That’s a coaching staff that knows exactly what’s wrong and is attacking it head on.

So let’s sum it all up.

Buffalo enters the break at five and one, on top of the AFC East.

Josh Allen continues to lead this offense with control and poise.

Keon Coleman is stepping up, with touchdowns in three straight games.

Tyler Bass has been incredibly reliable and is still perfect on field goals.

Dorian Williams made a career-first interception at the biggest moment.

Cole Bishop and Christian Benford had performances to build on.

But the defensive concerns are real, especially after giving up more than 300 passing yards in Las Vegas. C.J. Gardner-Johnson’s suspension makes the secondary even thinner. And the Ravens are next.

Now Tre’Davious White is back, and we’ll see whether his experience can help this secondary turn the corner.
