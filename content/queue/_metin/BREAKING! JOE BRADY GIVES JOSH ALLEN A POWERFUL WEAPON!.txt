To understand how big this moment is, you have to go back to the beginning. Way back.

Firebaugh, California. A tiny farm town about 40 miles west of Fresno. Miles of farm fields in every direction. That’s where Josh Allen grew up. He worked on his family’s cotton farm, and he worked at the restaurant his mom ran in town.

Today, the football field at his old high school is called Josh Allen Field. But when Josh walked off that field for the last time as a high school player, not a single big college wanted him.

In his own words, he had zero offers. Not even a spot to walk on, meaning join a team without a scholarship.

So the Allen family did the only thing they could. They sent around 1,000 emails to college coaches. And almost nobody wrote back.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. A thousand emails. Silence.

So Josh went to Reedley, a small two-year junior college about 65 miles away. There, he grew to six-foot-five. His coaches told him the big offers would start rolling in.

They never did.

Only two schools offered him a scholarship: Eastern Michigan and Wyoming. And then Eastern Michigan pulled its offer when Josh visited Wyoming.

That left one door open. One. In the entire country.

Wyoming head coach Craig Bohl sat down at the Allen family’s kitchen table and told them he wanted Josh to be the face of Wyoming football. And that’s exactly what he became. The fans in Laramie fell in love with him. And he played like a bigger, more athletic version of Cam Newton.

And that comparison matters, because of two men who had spent years watching Cam Newton up close in Carolina.

In 2017, the Bills hired Sean McDermott as head coach. He had spent the previous six years running Carolina’s defense. A few months later, the Bills hired Brandon Beane as general manager. Beane had spent 19 years with the Panthers. Both of them watched Cam Newton win MVP in 2015 by beating teams with his arm and his legs.

So when a big, strong, fast quarterback from Wyoming showed up in the 2018 draft, it doesn’t feel like an accident that the Bills took him with the seventh overall pick.

And then came his first NFL game. Week 1, 2018. In Baltimore.

The Bills lost 47 to 3. It was ugly. Josh only came in once the game was already out of reach. And plenty of people walked out of that stadium thinking Buffalo had made a mistake.

Remember, Josh talked about that game in his press conference this week. He said it honestly feels like yesterday. He remembers the downpour, how tacky the football felt in the rain, the rush to halftime, and even the very first play they ran: a play called “Superman.”

The next week, Josh made his first career start, at home against the Chargers. The Bills lost 31 to 20. But you could already see something different.

And one week after that, he showed the whole country.

September 2018. Rookie Josh Allen and the Bills go to Minnesota as 16 and a half point underdogs. Almost nobody gave them a chance. Josh runs for two touchdowns and throws for another. And then he does something no quarterback is supposed to do: he hurdles right over Vikings linebacker Anthony Barr.

The Bills win 27 to 6.

Remember that stadium, Bills Mafia. It’s going to matter at the end of this video.

And guess who was watching? Jim Kelly. He posted that he could never have hurdled like that.

That’s the thing about Jim Kelly. He’s been in Josh’s corner from the very start. Back in 2020, Jim’s wife, Jill Kelly, shared something Jim had told her: that Josh was going to break every record he has.

Kelly saw this coming six years ago.

Now, there’s a part of Jim Kelly’s story that a lot of younger Bills fans have never heard.

Before the 1983 draft, Jim Kelly told his agent he didn’t want to play in Green Bay. He didn’t want to play in Minnesota. And he really didn’t want to play for the Buffalo Bills.

The Bills drafted him anyway, with the 14th pick. So Kelly signed with the Houston Gamblers of a different league, the USFL. He only came to Buffalo in 1986, after that league shut down.

And to be fair, once Kelly got here, he gave this city everything he had. He became Buffalo royalty. He led the Bills to four straight Super Bowls. His last game was the 1997 playoff loss to Jacksonville, and Buffalo has loved him ever since.

But Josh Allen never had to be talked into Buffalo. Josh made this city his own from day one.

After breaking the record on Sunday, Josh said he doesn’t take it for granted to be the quarterback of the Buffalo Bills. And he said God placed him here for a reason.

Think about that. The kid nobody wanted chose the city that’s always been overlooked. That’s why Buffalo connects with Josh Allen the way it does.

But loving Buffalo doesn’t make you the best ever. So let’s get to Sunday, and to the numbers.

Sunday. The Bills beat the Ravens 36 to 10.

Josh came into the game tied with Kelly at 258 touchdown passes, counting the regular season and playoffs together.

The first half wasn’t pretty. Two turnovers. A 39.8 passer rating at halftime. Josh admitted afterward that he was pressing. But in the second half, he settled down and took over.

Early in the fourth quarter, he hit DJ Moore for an 11-yard touchdown. Number 259. The record.

What a moment for DJ Moore, too. He spent the whole week in a red non-contact jersey, listed as questionable with that shoulder injury, and he ended up catching the record-breaking touchdown pass.

A few minutes later, Josh hit Dalton Kincaid for number 260. Another player who spent the week limited in a red jersey, and he delivered too.

Josh finished with 289 passing yards and two touchdown passes, and he ran another one in himself.

And the second that record fell, the cameras cut to Jim Kelly in the stands. He was cheering.

Jill Kelly later posted the moment and called it bittersweet. And honestly, that’s the perfect word for every Bills fan who grew up on Jim Kelly. You’re happy for Josh. You’re proud of what he’s done. But there’s a little piece of you that always wanted Jim’s name at the top of that list.

Now comes the part Jim Kelly fans are going to throw right back at me. And they have fair points.

Kelly still holds the record for regular-season touchdown passes, with 237. He still has the most passing yards in franchise history. He still has the most wins by a Bills quarterback.

And here’s the big one: Kelly took Buffalo to four Super Bowls. Josh hasn’t been to one yet.

People will also tell you the NFL is a passing league now, and it’s easier to throw the ball than ever before.

So is Josh really the best ever? Let’s break it down.

Start with speed. Josh reached 259 touchdown passes in 150 games. Kelly needed 177 games. That’s 27 fewer games for Josh.

And that regular-season record? Josh is at 231. He’s six touchdown passes away from tying Kelly’s 237, with ten games left this season. So that record is very likely falling this year too.

Next, winning. Sunday was Josh’s 102nd career win, counting the playoffs. That moves him past Tom Brady for the third-most wins ever by a quarterback in his first nine seasons. Only Patrick Mahomes, with 112, and Russell Wilson, with 107, had more.

Read that again, Bills Mafia. More wins in his first nine seasons than Tom Brady.

But here’s what the “passing league” argument keeps forgetting. Throwing the ball is only half of who Josh Allen is.

In 2025, Josh passed Cam Newton for the most rushing touchdowns by a quarterback in NFL history. The same Cam Newton that Sean McDermott and Brandon Beane watched in Carolina, the player Josh was compared to back in Wyoming. Josh didn’t just become like him. He passed him.

And ESPN looked at goal line carries going all the way back to 1977, and Josh tops the list. When he gets the ball near the end zone, he almost always scores. We saw it again on Sunday, when he reached the ball over the goal line on fourth down, even with Baltimore’s big bodies in the middle and a plan designed to stop the quarterback sneak.

And now, here’s the number that ends the debate for a lot of people.

Jim Kelly ran for 1,049 yards in his entire career.

Josh Allen walked into Sunday with 4,961 career rushing yards. He ran for 30 more against the Ravens. That puts him at 4,991.

That’s almost five times what Kelly ran for in his career. And it’s just nine yards short of 5,000.

Only four quarterbacks in NFL history have ever reached 5,000 career rushing yards: Lamar Jackson, Michael Vick, Cam Newton, and Russell Wilson.

Josh is nine yards away from joining them.

So here’s my answer, Bills Mafia.

Jim Kelly has the Super Bowl trips, and nobody will ever take that away from him. What he did, leading Buffalo to four straight Super Bowls, is one of the most remarkable runs in NFL history. He’s a legend, and he always will be.

But a quarterback who throws more touchdown passes in far fewer games, who wins more than Tom Brady did in his first nine seasons, who holds the record for rushing touchdowns by a quarterback, and who runs like almost no quarterback in history? Buffalo has never seen anything like that.

And think about how perfectly this story came together.

Josh Allen’s first NFL game was in Baltimore: a 47 to 3 blowout, in the pouring rain, where nobody saw any of this coming. Eight years later, against those same Ravens, with Jim Kelly watching from the stands, Josh took the crown.

And Kelly said it best himself: if Josh is breaking his records, that means the Bills are winning.

That’s the kind of class that makes Jim Kelly a legend. He’s not bitter. He’s proud. He’s been in Josh’s corner since that hurdle in Minnesota. And now he’s watching the kid nobody wanted become the greatest quarterback this franchise has ever had.

Josh Allen is officially the greatest quarterback in Buffalo Bills history. The numbers back it up. And he’s not done.
