And the Buffalo Bills keep coming up as one of the teams most likely to land him.

Just this week, ESPN’s Dan Orlovsky broke down Buffalo’s offense in detail, and what he said about this unit’s potential, about Keon Coleman, and about what this team still needs to become truly unstoppable fits perfectly with the idea of adding a weapon like Tyreek Hill.

Let’s get into it.

First, let’s set the scene. The Bills are five and one, sitting on top of the AFC East, coming out of their bye week. Next up is a massive AFC showdown with the Baltimore Ravens in Week 8. The Ravens have a physical divisional battle with Cincinnati first, and we’ll find out more about Lamar Jackson’s status in the coming days. But one thing is already clear: this is going to be one of the biggest tests of Buffalo’s season.

Now, five and one isn’t perfect. We all know there are things to fix on defense. But the offense? The offense is humming. Buffalo has been putting up 30 points on a regular basis, and here’s the crazy part: they’ve done it without their number one receiver, DJ Moore, in the lineup for each of the last two weeks.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. Your top receiver is out, and the offense doesn’t slow down. That tells you how deep, how creative, and how well coached this unit is.

And Dan Orlovsky pointed to one specific reason why.

One of the biggest keys to Buffalo’s offense recently has been how they use their running backs in the passing game. And Bills Mafia, the numbers here are off the charts. In advanced metrics like EPA per target, Buffalo’s backs have been miles ahead of the rest of the league. Ty Johnson, Ray Davis, and James Cook have all been extraordinarily productive catching the football.

And Orlovsky explained that each of them does it in a different way.

With Ty Johnson, it’s often the plus plays, the broken plays. Orlovsky said Johnson just has more feel for when and where to go when a play starts breaking down. He also brings something different on the perimeter in the screen game.

Ray Davis is used a bit more in the screen game too. You don’t see him as much in the traditional dropback or designed passing concepts, or in the broken-play stuff.

And James Cook is used more when the Bills want to run designed pass concepts. We’ve seen him come out of the backfield on the rail route, among other things.

Orlovsky called it a really nice three-headed monster. The Bills are using these guys as football players, not just as running backs, and each one brings his own element. He said the screen game is paramount for this offense, and that the timing of when Joe Brady and the staff call those screens has been pivotal. Those plays have become staples, and having three different backs who can all run them, each in a different way, matters a ton.

So here’s the picture: an offense that can beat you with the run, with the screen game, with the backs catching the ball, and with Josh Allen making plays out of nothing. Now imagine adding the most feared deep threat of his generation to that.

We’ll get there. But first, Orlovsky identified the one thing this offense needs to figure out, and this is where the Tyreek Hill conversation gets really interesting.

He was asked where this offense could still get better. And he said, outside of DJ Moore getting healthy, which everyone knows is a big deal, the big takeaway from the first six weeks is this: when Buffalo is able to get to its core principles, it’s basically unstoppable.

He named those core principles. The duo run game. The toss run game. The different screen games. The quarterback keeper game. And a few other go-to concepts. The Bills dress them up with different motions and formations, but when you watch this offense at its best, it’s getting to those core things.

And then Orlovsky pointed to the one game that stands out: New England. He said the Patriots didn’t allow Buffalo to get to those core principles for four quarters. And if he were Joe Brady and this staff, he’d be trying to figure out where to go when someone takes away the core stuff.

He was clear that this isn’t a reason to panic. You always want to be who you are, and you add new wrinkles every week. But you’re going to run into good defensive teams that do everything they can to take away those five or six core plays. And some days you won’t have your best stuff. Orlovsky said figuring out the answer to that is the next step for this team as it heads into the late-season push after the bye.

Later, he used a great analogy. He compared Buffalo’s offense to a pitcher with a two-seamer, a four-seamer, a slider, a curve, and a changeup, multiple pitches to choose from. But every so often you’re going to face a Barry Bonds, a hitter who can crush whatever you throw. And if this team wants to accomplish what it wants to accomplish, it’s going to face defenses like New England, Kansas City, Baltimore, and Cleveland that are very good at a lot of different things.

One of the hosts put it perfectly: if a team is good enough to force Buffalo to beat them left-handed, then Buffalo needs to be ambidextrous.

Bills Mafia, that’s the key. What happens when an elite defense takes away your favorite plays?

And this is exactly where Tyreek Hill comes into the conversation.

Because when a defense takes away everything underneath, everything in the run game, everything in the screen game, what’s the answer? You take the top off the defense. You make them pay deep. And there are very few players in NFL history who do that better than Tyreek Hill.

Now let’s be clear about exactly where things stand, because there’s a lot of noise out there.

Tyreek Hill is a free agent. Back in Week 4 of last season, playing for the Miami Dolphins against the Jets, he suffered a severe knee injury: a dislocation with multiple torn ligaments, including the ACL. The Dolphins released him in February as part of a big roster overhaul.

Since then, Hill has been working his way back. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said back in August that he didn’t expect Hill to sign before the season, because Hill wanted to take his time and find the right team. Hill’s doctors had recommended he push his comeback to sometime around October.

Well, Bills Mafia, it’s October.

Earlier this month, Rosenhaus said he planned to start negotiating with multiple teams. And throughout this process, the Buffalo Bills have been named again and again as one of the most logical landing spots.

NFL insider Albert Breer said both the Bills and the Chiefs are motivated to land Hill. And Breer explained why it makes sense for Buffalo: Hill could add an element this offense doesn’t have right now, and maybe unlock more downfield for Josh Allen. Breer also pointed out that Hill would add a dimension Buffalo hasn’t really had since parting ways with Stefon Diggs.

Even Bills general manager Brandon Beane, when he was asked about Hill back in February, called him a heck of a player.

But let’s be honest about the rest of the picture too, because you deserve the full story.

As of now, there’s no deal. In mid-October, Rosenhaus said nothing was imminent. The Kansas City Chiefs, Hill’s old team, have reportedly reached out and are considered a top contender for a reunion. There are still questions about his physical condition after such a serious injury, and he’s 32 years old. And there’s also an off-field situation: Hill was recently found liable in a civil trial in Miami involving his estranged wife, and the NFL has said it is reviewing the case. That could affect when, and whether, a team decides to sign him.

So nothing is done. But the door to Buffalo is still open. And Bills Mafia, if this happens, the ceiling of this team goes through the roof.

Now let’s go back to Orlovsky, because the other big part of his analysis was about a player who’s already changing the story of this season: Keon Coleman.

One of the hosts said he almost fell out of his chair when he saw that Keon Coleman is leading the entire league in EPA per target. Coleman has stepped into a much bigger role with DJ Moore out, he has touchdowns in each of the last three games, and he’s made several key third-down conversions.

And Orlovsky’s response was powerful. He called it one of the great stories in the NFL this season, across the whole league.

He pointed out that Coleman was a player many had written off. Orlovsky was careful to say he wasn’t being disrespectful, but he noted that even the team’s owner had talked publicly about him in that way.

And now, Orlovsky said, the two words that consistently show up on Coleman’s tape are reliable and trust.

He credited Coleman himself, Joe Brady and the coaching staff, Josh Allen, and the people in that locker room and meeting room. And then he said something huge: he doesn’t believe this team is five and one without Keon Coleman. He said he truly does not believe it.

Bills Mafia, that’s an ESPN analyst saying Keon Coleman is one of the reasons this team is five and one.

And then Orlovsky explained why the future of this passing game is so dangerous. If Coleman keeps going and stays grounded, Buffalo will have at least two real one-on-one problems for defenses, and two real broken-play problems: Coleman and DJ Moore. On top of that, there’s Khalil Shakir, who’s a matchup problem in a different way. And Joshua Palmer, who, as Orlovsky put it, seems to show up when it matters.

Orlovsky said that if Coleman stays on this track, this offense becomes, in his words, not impossible, but about as close to impossible to match up with and defend as it can be.

Now stop and imagine that group with Tyreek Hill added to it.

DJ Moore. Keon Coleman. Khalil Shakir. Joshua Palmer. James Cook, Ty Johnson, and Ray Davis out of the backfield. And Tyreek Hill, with that game-changing speed, forcing safeties to play deep on every single snap.

Who do you double? Who do you leave in single coverage? Where does your safety help go? The answer is: there’s no good answer. And that’s exactly what Orlovsky said this offense needs, a way to keep scoring when an elite defense takes away the core plays. A deep threat like Hill forces defenses to back up, which opens up the run game, the screens, and the underneath throws all over again.

There was one more part of Orlovsky’s analysis I really want to highlight, because it shows how much Josh Allen has grown with Keon Coleman.

One of the hosts said he’s seeing Allen throw with anticipation to Coleman in a way he didn’t see the last two years. Orlovsky said absolutely, and that’s exactly why he used the words trust and reliable.

Then he explained anticipation from a quarterback’s point of view. Part of it is reading the defense, seeing that defenders aren’t where they’re supposed to be and a window is opening. But the next level is about the receiver. When the ball leaves my hand, does the guy I’m throwing to value it the same way I do? Can I trust him the moment the ball comes out? Will he defend it, protect it, make sure it’s safe?

Orlovsky said it’s easy to anticipate based on the defense. But if you don’t trust your receiver to do everything he can to go get the ball, you can’t anticipate, because you don’t trust him. And that’s why he believes Josh Allen is now telling Keon Coleman: you’ve earned it. If I put the ball in a certain place, you’re going to come down with it, or no one will.

Bills Mafia, that’s chemistry. That’s a quarterback and receiver who are on the same wavelength. And when a quarterback has that kind of trust with multiple receivers, that’s when an offense becomes truly special.

Finally, Orlovsky was asked whether this offense can get back to what we saw early in the season, against Houston on opening day and Detroit in Week 2, when Buffalo was hanging high 30s and 40 points on people before the injuries hit.

Orlovsky said that’s the journey for Buffalo this week. Figuring out those answers. He reminded everyone that the Bills won’t face many defenses that can take away all of their “pitches,” but they will face some, and to accomplish what they want, they have to be ready for those.

And then he gave his verdict: this offense can score 30 a game, for sure. He said it starts and ends with the offensive line staying healthy, the team staying on top of the details, and he noted that Josh Allen has been much better recently with his eyes.

Thirty points a game, Bills Mafia. That’s what an ESPN analyst believes this offense can do, and that’s without Tyreek Hill.

So let’s bring it all together and answer the big question. Could Tyreek Hill be the piece that makes the Buffalo Bills champions?

Here’s my take.

This team is already five and one. The offense is already scoring 30 points a game. Keon Coleman is leading the league in EPA per target. The running backs are among the most productive in football as receivers. DJ Moore is going to come back healthy. And Josh Allen is playing with the kind of trust and anticipation that makes an offense nearly impossible to defend.

But Orlovsky also told us the one thing this team needs to figure out: what to do when an elite defense takes away the core of what Buffalo does. And in January, that’s exactly what you face. You face the New Englands, the Kansas Citys, the Baltimores. The defenses that are good enough to force you to beat them left-handed.

A healthy Tyreek Hill is the ultimate left-handed answer. He’s the kind of player who changes how a defense has to line up before the snap. He takes away the safety help. He opens up everything underneath. And when the game is on the line and nothing else is working, he can win with one route.

Now, let’s be realistic. Nothing has been announced. He’s coming off a major knee injury. He’s 32. There’s an off-field situation the league is still reviewing. And the Chiefs are in the mix too. So if Hill signs with Buffalo, there will be real questions about how much he has left and how quickly he can contribute.

But if he comes to Buffalo, and if he’s anywhere close to the player he was in Kansas City and Miami, then I believe this team has every weapon it needs to go win a Super Bowl. Josh Allen, the most dangerous quarterback in the league, with the most complete set of weapons he’s ever had. That’s a championship formula.

And imagine the twist of fate. Tyreek Hill, the man who tormented Buffalo for years in Kansas City and Miami, choosing Buffalo over the Chiefs and helping the Bills finally get over the top. Bills Mafia, that would be one of the greatest storylines in this franchise’s history.
