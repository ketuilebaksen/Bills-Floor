BILLS JUST PULLED OFF A SURPRISE MOVE AFTER RAVENS 36–10 WIN!

Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills just dismantled the Baltimore Ravens 36 to 10, they’re six and one, and Joe Brady went to the podium the day after with some real surprises. A behind-the-scenes change to how the team operates. A big move involving defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard. A coin toss strategy that goes against what most NFL coaches do. Strong words about a controversial goal line play involving Josh Allen. And a brutally honest take on penalties that might surprise you.

There’s a lot here, and some of it tells us exactly why this team is playing the way it is. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s get into it.

The first surprise came right away. Brady was asked for injury updates, and he said he didn’t have any yet. And then he explained why, and it’s a change in how this team runs its day after a game.

Brady said that at this point in the season, the team has a set flow on days like this. The players come in during the morning, they have their meetings, they spend time with the tape. And only after his press conference does he sit down with the medical team to go through evaluations, injury results, and potential roster moves.

He said they switched the schedule on purpose, so they didn’t have to rush the medicals. Players can get in, get MRIs and anything else they need, and the doctors and medical staff get time to process all the information.

A reporter pushed back, saying it seems counterintuitive not to find out as quickly as possible. And Brady explained his thinking. He said the team knows players are going to be hurt after a game, and they’re going to get MRIs. But rather than rushing to get results first thing in the morning, he’d much rather get through the meetings first. He said it has no bearing on how they game plan that day. It’s about giving the medical staff time. And he said it’s better for the players to get in, put closure on the game, and then move forward, as opposed to making a lot of decisions and going through a lot of things first, and then not starting on your next opponent until five or six in the evening.

Bills Mafia, that’s a first-year head coach thinking about process. Close the chapter on the last game the right way, give the doctors time to do their job, and then turn the page completely. It’s a small thing, but small things add up.

Now, the controversial moment from the game.

A reporter asked about a goal line play where Josh Allen scored a touchdown, and a Ravens defender was seen grabbing Josh’s helmet. What was Brady’s reaction, especially from a player safety perspective?

Brady said he didn’t know anything about it during the game. And then he revealed something interesting: if he had known about it, it probably would have affected some of the quarterback sneak calls later in the game, and how those ended up playing out.

He said that whether it was intentional or not, that’s obviously not something you want to see in football. He called the defender a great football player. But he said he saw Josh getting up with marks on his face, and at the time he thought it was just part of a quarterback sneak. He said he was surprised nothing got called. But, he added, that’s not his place.

Bills Mafia, remember what Josh said after the game. He said the Ravens had some really big guys in the middle and a really good plan against Buffalo’s sneak plays. Now we know Josh came out of one of those plays with marks on his face. Brady didn’t go overboard, but he made it clear: that’s not what anyone wants to see, and he’s surprised there was no flag.

Next, a question about the Ravens’ quarterback situation. Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley both went down, and Baltimore had to go to its third-string quarterback. Does that change how Brady evaluates the game a day later?

Brady said it has to. He said he tries to put himself in the position of the opposing play-caller. And he made a sharp observation: the quarterback who played in the second half had probably spent the whole week playing Josh Allen on Baltimore’s scout team in practice.

But then Brady said what he was most proud of: his players didn’t care. He talked about the idea of a nameless, faceless opponent. There was a standard, and the team was going to play to it no matter who was across the field. He loved the way the defense flew around and celebrated each other’s success.

And he gave Jim Leonhard big credit. He said the way Leonhard called the game was outstanding, with adjustments made as the game went on based on the different quarterbacks.

And then Brady pushed back on the idea that the result was just about Baltimore’s injuries. He said Lamar Jackson is one of the greatest quarterbacks in the league, so of course there are differences. But he said he doesn’t think the result was a product of facing backup quarterbacks. He said that, based on the way the team practiced during the week, he felt this was going to be the result of the game regardless.

Bills Mafia, that’s confidence. Brady saw how this team practiced after the bye, and he believed this kind of performance was coming no matter who was under center for Baltimore.

Then Brady was asked about something he’s said before: that as a play-caller, he knows opponents won’t play the same way against Josh Allen that they showed on tape. How long does it take him to notice that during a game?

Brady said sometimes it just depends on how long the offense is on the field. Some games are completely different from what they expected. Other games have just small differences: how the defense sets its front, how it rotates, what coverage structures it plays, how it adjusts when Buffalo goes under center versus the shotgun. He said a lot goes into it, and that’s partly because of Josh, but also because of all the different personnel groups Buffalo uses, including multiple tight end packages.

He also said you can’t overreact to a few plays early. Sometimes you think a defense is playing a certain way, and it turns out it was just the call on that play.

He said it’s not rocket science, but there are a lot of nuances. And he said that as the game goes on, especially with a quarterback like Josh, the call sheet keeps shrinking. That gives Josh the chance to anticipate and understand what’s coming, and gives the other players the same opportunity.

That explains a lot, Bills Mafia. Remember, Josh had a 39.8 passer rating at halftime and finished above 96. That’s the call sheet shrinking, the adjustments landing, and Josh taking over.

Now, here’s the big one. Jim Leonhard moved up to the coaching box for this game.

That’s a real change. Leonhard called the game from upstairs instead of from the sideline. And a reporter asked Brady what the conversations were like during the bye week when that decision was made.

Brady said he could speak from experience, as someone who’s been up there. He admitted that he loves being on the field now as a head coach, and he even said that if he could go back to being an offensive coordinator, he probably would have enjoyed being on the field a bit more. But he explained what’s special about the box.

He said there’s something about being up there: understanding where your eyes need to be, what you need to see, and not having to depend on other people. He made it clear that has nothing to do with the staff. It’s just part of being in the box. Certain coaches are watching certain things, you can call a play while you’re looking at something else, and you have an idea of what happened without having to wait for adjustments or look at the pictures afterward.

Then he gave Leonhard huge credit. He said Leonhard called a great game. Was that because he was in the box? Brady said Leonhard was going to call a great game regardless. But he said the communication from upstairs to downstairs was elite. He said Leonhard had a great understanding of what the Ravens were trying to do, and that being up there sometimes makes life a little easier.

Bills Mafia, think back to the whole season. Leonhard said communication was the number one problem. He said the secondary looked confused too often. He said there were changes he couldn’t make until after the bye. And now we know one of those changes: Leonhard moved to the box. The result? The defense held Baltimore to 10 points, and the communication was described as elite.

That’s not a coincidence. That’s a coordinator who made a move to fix the problem, and it worked.

Next, Brady was asked about Greg Rousseau, who has been having an outstanding season. The knock on Rousseau earlier in his career was inconsistency. How has he matured into such a consistent impact player?

Brady said Rousseau is very intentional about everything he does. He wants to be great. He has a great mindset and approach to his daily work, and Brady called him such a pro.

He said adding a player like Bradley Chubb only helps him, and the two of them feed off each other. Then you add T.J. Parker, and the whole outside linebacker room feeds off one another. Brady said Rousseau is benefiting from Chubb, from Deone Walker, from getting Ed Oliver back, and from seeing all those players working together.

And he said Rousseau is getting chances to stand up and win on the edge, he’s impacting the quarterback, and he’s making a lot of plays that don’t always show up in the stats, while also putting up the production. Brady said Rousseau is a lot of fun to watch right now.

Bills Mafia, even national analysts have been talking about Rousseau and Deone Walker this season. This defensive line is the real deal.

And speaking of the defensive line, Brady was asked about Ed Oliver, and how important he is to the defensive identity, especially off the field and in the building.

Brady’s answer: he’s what we’re about.

He said Oliver has continued to grow on and off the field and is a true pro now. He said Oliver flew around, made so many plays, and was so much fun to watch. He loved seeing Ed celebrate with his teammates. He brought up a Deone Walker play that he joked he still doesn’t know whether to call a strip sack, a fumble, or an incomplete pass, and said Ed was right there celebrating with him.

Brady said it was a lot of fun to watch Ed play football again. And he pointed to the whole defensive line: the number of hits on the quarterback, the times they touched the quarterback, and the throws they affected, even without piling up sacks, was huge. And he said Ed Oliver is critical to this organization and to the locker room. Seeing him get to go out and play free was a lot of fun to watch.

First game back since Week 4, Bills Mafia, and Ed Oliver immediately reminded everyone what he means to this defense.

Now, the penalties. And this is where Brady said something that might surprise you.

A reporter pointed out that the Bills had 10 penalties in this game, and asked how Brady can get his message across that discipline has to be the standard.

Brady said he doesn’t want to lead the league in fewest penalties.

He admitted that sounds crazy. But he explained it. He wants to be in the middle of the pack. He wants this team to toe the line and play with an edge. If you lead the league in fewest penalties, that might mean you’re playing a little more conservative.

But he said there are different types of penalties. Post-snap penalties and pre-snap penalties. And he said he has no time for pre-snap penalties. Those are unacceptable, because they’re about controlling what you can control. Post-snap penalties, depending on the technique, are sometimes things you can live with, but when they happen consistently, it’s about fundamentals and technique that have to be cleaned up.

And then he admitted: right now, they’re not in the middle of the pack. That’s the issue.

He did give the defensive backs credit. He said they did a really good job in this game cleaning up their intentionality, fundamentals, and technique on pass interference and holding calls. Remember, earlier this season Jim Leonhard said the defense was 31st in the league in penalties, and a lot of that came from the secondary. So that’s real progress.

But he said the offensive holding penalties showed up again. There were illegal formations. There was a penalty for celebrating a touchdown. They gave up a free play on an offsides, and there were two offsides penalties overall. Brady said those are the things you don’t have patience for, and they have to get cleaned up.

So Brady’s message is clear, Bills Mafia: keep the edge, but stop the dumb ones. Pre-snap penalties and celebration penalties have to go.

Next, another surprise. The coin toss.

A reporter pointed out that when Brady wins the coin toss, he’s been choosing to take the ball, while most NFL coaches defer to the second half by a wide margin. Why?

Brady said a lot goes into it, and it might change as the weather changes. But right now, the weather hasn’t been a factor. And then he said this: when that ball is kicked off, the other team has to play Josh Allen.

He said he’s said before that opponents have to play us, and he means it. He said the team did a lot of studies on things like the chance of “double dipping,” getting the ball at the end of the first half and again to start the second half. And he said there have been plenty of games where Buffalo got the ball first and still had it at the end of the first half too. You never really know how that will play out. But taking the ball first gives you a chance to grab leads going into halftime and feed off of that.

And then he admitted something important: this team hasn’t been up to its standard starting games this season. That was a big point of emphasis, and he said they did a great job of it against Baltimore. He said fast starts have usually been a strength of this offense.

He said it’s not something they’ll always do. But against Baltimore, knowing how the game was going to be played, he thought it was important for the Ravens to know they were going to have to play Josh Allen from the jump.

Bills Mafia, I love that. Most coaches defer. Joe Brady says: here’s the ball, here’s Josh Allen, deal with it.

Then came the fourth downs. Buffalo went for it six times, and with one conversion wiped out by a penalty, finished four for five. A reporter asked whether the decision-making changes depending on who’s playing quarterback for the other team.

Brady said it’s still all about Buffalo. What are the odds of making the kick? Where is the ball? Do they like their play calls in that situation? He said it’s never as black and white as going for it no matter what. He joked that it doesn’t matter if Joe Brady himself were playing quarterback on the other side, they wouldn’t automatically go on fourth and two.

He said you go into a game with a mindset, with calls you love in certain situations. But the flow of the game matters too. Do you have a chance to put the game away?

And then he said he never wants this team to have a conservative mindset. He’d much rather have the ball in the offense’s hands with a chance to go take the game. But that doesn’t mean being careless.

He also admitted there were too many fourth downs in this game, and that’s because they weren’t good enough on third down.

He pointed to the second drive, where they converted a fourth and two, but a penalty wiped it out. Then Tyler Bass bailed them out with a long field goal. Brady said Bass has made those kicks, but in fourth-and-short to medium situations, the percentages with Buffalo’s offense on the field should be an advantage.

And he summed it up perfectly: if he can keep the ball in 17’s hands, in the offense’s hands, usually good things are going to happen.

So let’s put it all together, Bills Mafia.

The surprise moves:

The Bills changed their post-game schedule, doing meetings and film first and medicals after, so players can put closure on the game and the doctors can do their job without rushing.

Jim Leonhard moved to the coaching box, and the communication from upstairs to downstairs was elite.

Joe Brady keeps taking the ball when he wins the coin toss, because he wants opponents to face Josh Allen from the very first snap.

And he’s staying aggressive on fourth down, because he’d rather keep the ball in Josh Allen’s hands.

The big takeaways:

The defense held Baltimore to 10 points, and Brady says it wasn’t just about the backup quarterbacks.

Greg Rousseau, Ed Oliver, Deone Walker, Bradley Chubb, and T.J. Parker are making this defensive line one of the best in football.

The secondary cleaned up its penalties, but the offense still has too many pre-snap mistakes.

And there’s still a question mark over that goal line play involving Josh Allen’s helmet.

Bills Mafia, this team is six and one. It’s coming off its most complete performance of the season. And its coaching staff is making smart, bold decisions. Now there’s a Monday night game ahead, with an extra day to prepare.
