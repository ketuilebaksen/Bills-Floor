BILLS JUST GOT A DOUBLE DOSE OF NEWS! THE 8-1 MONSTER GEARS UP FOR THANKSGIVING SHOWDOWN!

Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills are eight and one after dismantling the New York Jets 31 to 12. And the national debate about this team has completely exploded. Because something happened in that game that might be more frightening for the rest of the NFL than any Josh Allen highlight: Buffalo won by 19 points with Josh Allen playing as a supporting actor.

And the defense? It has now gone 33 straight drives without allowing a touchdown.

So today, we’ve got a double dose of big news. First, that defensive streak, and the heated argument it sparked on national TV about whether it’s real. And second, the case that Buffalo has finally become a complete football team, one that doesn’t need a superhero performance from Josh Allen to win. And all of it is building toward a massive Thanksgiving night showdown with Patrick Mahomes and the Kansas City Chiefs.

Let’s get into it.

First, a quick recap of the win over the Jets.

Josh Allen accounted for three touchdowns. James Cook ran for 157 yards on just 18 carries, including a 74-yard touchdown sprint that Josh said was the fastest he had ever seen Cook run. And Buffalo’s defense hit Jets quarterback Geno Smith so hard that it knocked him out of the game in the third quarter.

And here’s the number that has everyone talking. The Bills defense has now gone 33 straight drives without allowing a touchdown.

Thirty-three drives, Bills Mafia. Think about how hard that is in today’s NFL, where offenses are built to score. Over the last few games, against Baltimore, Minnesota, and now New York, opponents have driven the field, gotten close, and walked away with field goals or nothing at all.

When one host on FS1’s “First Things First” brought up the stat, he joked: don’t ask who it’s been against. And then he asked the panel the big question: are you ready to say the Bills are a complete football team?

And that’s where the debate started.

Chris Broussard said no. He said he likes the Bills, but he’s not ready to call them complete. He said he expected the defense to gradually get better and become more than mediocre, maybe even good eventually. But he said this isn’t it yet.

And he said you can’t just ignore who Buffalo has been playing. He listed the quarterbacks: Joe Fagnano, the undrafted rookie who replaced the injured Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley for Baltimore. Carson Wentz, who started for Minnesota. Geno Smith, before the Bills knocked him out. And rookie Cade Klubnik, who came in for the Jets after that.

Broussard said that while Geno Smith has had a good season, the Jets as a team are just bad. And he pointed out that the last time Buffalo faced a really good quarterback, the Bills gave up 30 points.

Then Nick Wright stepped in to defend the Bills. And remember, Nick Wright is a huge Kansas City Chiefs fan. He’s also the same analyst who said recently that Josh Allen is overrated in the MVP race. So when he defends the Bills, it means something.

Wright said the Bills definitively have an excellent quarterback, an excellent running back, and an excellent offensive line. And he said the defense, over the last month, has given us evidence that it won’t be one of the worst defenses in football this year. It’s not going to be a catastrophe.

And then he made a smart comparison. He said that just like he expects the Chiefs’ defense to keep improving as the season goes on, because it has so many young players, you can expect Buffalo’s defense to improve too, because it has a new defensive coordinator and a new scheme. He said you can bake some improvement into that over the course of the season.

He did acknowledge that, looking at the full season, Buffalo’s defensive numbers are decidedly average. But that’s with the bad early weeks included.

Bills Mafia, Wright is exactly right about the trend. Remember where this defense was before the bye. Going into Week 8, Buffalo was allowing 28.5 points per game, the third-most in the NFL. It ranked second-worst in total yards and passing yards allowed. Its DVOA was around 28th in the league.

And since the bye? Baltimore scored 10 points. Minnesota scored six. The Jets scored 12, all on field goals. Jim Leonhard moved up to the coaching box, and Joe Brady said the communication from upstairs to downstairs was elite. Leonhard blitzed 64.5 percent of the time against Baltimore, and just 11.5 percent against Minnesota, and dominated both games.

And now Amani Hooker is in the back end, tied for the league lead with five interceptions. Greg Rousseau is tied for second in the NFL in sacks, and he leads the entire league in pressures. Ed Oliver is healthy. Deone Walker is drawing double teams. This defense has real difference-makers now.

So yes, the quarterbacks haven’t all been elite. But a defense that was this bad and has become this good, this quickly, is not a fluke.

Before we get to the second piece of big news, smash that like button if you believe in this defense. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

Now, the second piece of big news, and it might be the scariest thing about this team: Josh Allen doesn’t have to be a superhero anymore.

Nick Wright made this point, and it’s a big one. He said this was now the second, or arguably the third, win this season where Josh Allen was a supporting actor.

Against the Jets, Josh finished with just five rushing yards. His two rushing touchdowns were both one-yard goal line plunges. And Wright said that if you listed the reasons Buffalo won this game, Josh wouldn’t even make the medal stand.

And Wright said that’s a good thing for Buffalo. Because it means the Bills don’t need great Josh Allen games to beat everybody.

Now, let’s be fair to Josh. Those points all count. Both of those one-yard touchdowns were big. And Josh also threw a 45-yard touchdown pass to DJ Moore. According to reports, with his three touchdowns in this game, Josh passed Peyton Manning for the most total touchdowns through a player’s first ten seasons, with 325.

But Wright’s point stands. For years, the book on beating Buffalo in January was simple: force Josh Allen to put on the superhero cape, wait for the defense to crack in the fourth quarter, and watch the season end.

That’s not this team anymore.

Against Minnesota, James Cook carried the ball 31 times, the most by any player in any game this season, and Buffalo held the ball for over 42 minutes. Against the Jets, Cook ran for 157 yards on 18 carries. Josh said himself after the Jets game that the offense didn’t even play that well. And Buffalo still put up 31 points and won by 19.

When a franchise quarterback can play in second gear and his team still blows out opponents, the championship math changes completely.

And Josh has embraced it. Remember what he said after the Ravens game: he admitted he was pressing early, and needed to take a breath, settle his feet, and go through his progressions. Against Minnesota, analysts said he beat the blitz with patience, not heroics. He’s not trying to do everything himself anymore. He’s trusting the team around him.

So what’s behind this transformation?

On “The Rich Eisen Show,” former NFL defensive lineman Marcus Spears pointed to two things: first-year head coach Joe Brady, and a healthy DJ Moore.

Spears said that when Moore is healthy, Buffalo’s offense is completely different. That’s exactly what we saw against the Jets, with the 45-yard touchdown from Josh to DJ. And it’s what a national analyst said a couple of weeks ago: when you have a true number one receiver, the number one becomes the number two, the number two becomes the number three, and everything opens up.

And then Spears told a great story about Joe Brady.

Spears said he was at a Bills game recently and watched Brady prepare. He watched Brady walk around the field during pregame warmups, completely locked in. And Spears joked that he was upset with Brady, because Brady didn’t stop to say hello to him, even though Spears said he was Brady’s biggest advocate when Brady was at LSU, and his biggest advocate for becoming a head coach. But then Spears said he wasn’t going to hold it against him, because the man had business to handle. He was getting ready for a game.

And then Spears said something that should make every Bills fan proud. He said that right now, Brady has a pulse on his team the same way Ben Johnson does in Chicago.

That’s high praise, Bills Mafia. Ben Johnson has been called one of the best young coaches in football. And now Brady is being mentioned in the same breath.

And it lines up with what Josh Allen said about Brady last week. Josh said Brady hasn’t changed one bit since becoming head coach. He’s the same person he was as a quarterbacks coach and as an offensive coordinator. And Josh said the players understand who Brady is as a person, and that he cares so much about the game. The players rally behind that.

Spears also pointed out that Buffalo’s run game is a huge reason for its success. But he said we’ll learn a lot about how far this team has come in the second half of the season, with games against Green Bay, Chicago, Denver, and Kansas City still to come.

Before we get to Thanksgiving, smash that like button if Joe Brady is your Coach of the Year. Let’s hear it, Bills Mafia.

Former NFL head coach Eric Mangini also weighed in, and he pointed to something that could be Buffalo’s hidden weapon: takeaways.

Mangini said the Bills are tied for sixth in the league in takeaways. He acknowledged that you can’t count on turnovers every single week. But he said that, typically, teams that generate takeaways keep doing it through the rest of the season. And he said that if Buffalo stays in the top 10 in takeaways, and the quarterback stays out of the top 10 in giveaways, that’s a team that can go a long way.

That’s a key point, Bills Mafia. Josh Allen said after the Jets game that turnovers on offense have to stop. And if the defense keeps taking the ball away while the offense protects it, that’s a championship formula. A defense that steals possessions gives Josh a margin for error he’s never really had before.

And with Amani Hooker, the league’s co-leader in interceptions, now in the secondary, those takeaways could keep coming.

Mangini did admit he might be harder on the Bills than anyone else on the panel, because of the quality of quarterbacks Buffalo has faced. But he said he’s excited about those takeaway numbers.

And now, everything builds toward one massive game: Thanksgiving night. Kansas City Chiefs at Buffalo Bills.

First, Buffalo faces the Dolphins. If the Bills win that game, they’ll be nine and one. And the Chiefs, at eight and two, will come to Buffalo for one of the biggest regular-season games of the year.

And the Chiefs are hot. Patrick Mahomes just torched Atlanta for 340 yards and five touchdowns, and he did it with a rookie running back, despite missing Kenneth Walker III and three starting offensive linemen.

So this is the test everyone has been waiting for. Broussard said the Bills defense hasn’t faced a quarterback with a pulse during this streak. Well, Patrick Mahomes has a pulse.

When the panel asked whether they were more open than ever to the idea that Buffalo’s defense is good enough to win a Super Bowl, one analyst said he would give his answer on Thanksgiving night. He said it’s hard to feel anything about the Dolphins game. But if Buffalo puts up a real fight against the Chiefs, he said, then he’ll be convinced.

And remember what Nick Wright said a couple of weeks ago, as one of the biggest Chiefs fans in the media: he said he expects the Chiefs to get their teeth kicked in on Thanksgiving night in Buffalo.

Bills Mafia, the stage is set. A nine and one Bills team, if they take care of the Dolphins. A defense on a 33-drive touchdown-free streak. A home crowd in the new Highmark Stadium. On Thanksgiving night. Against Patrick Mahomes.

And there’s a lot on the line. Buffalo has never had the number one seed in the Josh Allen era. It’s never had a first-round bye. It’s never hosted the AFC Championship Game with Josh at quarterback. A win over Kansas City on Thanksgiving night would be a massive step toward changing that.

And finally, the biggest question of all: can Buffalo actually win the Super Bowl?

Marcus Spears picked the Bills to win the Super Bowl before the season, and he isn’t backing off. He acknowledged that Joe Burrow and the Bengals are going to be a nightmare in the playoffs, especially with Burrow throwing five touchdowns seemingly every game. And he admitted Josh’s playoff game in Denver wasn’t his best, and that Josh would want that one back.

But Spears said he’s leaning toward Buffalo, and here’s why. He said Josh Allen has played too good of football, for too long, not to eventually break through in one of those three-hour playoff windows.

Bills Mafia, that’s what we’ve all been waiting for. And this year, with a balanced offense, a dominant run game, a defense that finally has difference-makers, and a head coach with his finger on the pulse of the team, it feels different.

Big news number one: the Bills defense has gone 33 straight drives without allowing a touchdown. Chris Broussard says the quarterbacks haven’t been elite. But Nick Wright says the defense is no longer a catastrophe, and that you can expect it to keep improving under Jim Leonhard.

Big news number two: Josh Allen was a supporting actor in a 31 to 12 win. And Nick Wright says that’s a great sign, because Buffalo no longer needs a superhero performance from Josh to beat anyone. Even so, Josh’s three touchdowns reportedly pushed him past Peyton Manning for the most total touchdowns through ten seasons.

Marcus Spears says Joe Brady has a pulse on this team like Ben Johnson does in Chicago, and that a healthy DJ Moore changes everything.

Eric Mangini says Buffalo’s top-10 takeaway numbers could carry the team a long way.
