To understand why Bills fans are so angry, you have to go back to the moment it happened.

Buffalo is rolling over the Baltimore Ravens. The Bills would go on to win 36 to 10, one of their most dominant performances of the season. And in the middle of that game, they’re facing fourth down at the goal line.

Josh Allen takes the snap and dives into the pile on a quarterback sneak. Bodies everywhere. Baltimore’s biggest players in the middle. And somewhere in that pile, Roquan Smith grabs Josh Allen’s face mask and violently twists his head.

Josh pushes through anyway and gets the ball across the goal line for a touchdown.

But no flag is thrown.

Not one.

Bills fans watching at home couldn’t believe it. The replay was clear. The face mask was grabbed, and the head was twisted. And yet the officials let it go.

So what did Josh do? Exactly what you’d expect from him. He got back up, gave a Ravens defender a shove, and kept playing. He didn’t let it rattle him. He didn’t let it take him out of his game. He finished that afternoon with two touchdown passes and a rushing touchdown, and one of those touchdown passes broke Jim Kelly’s franchise record for total touchdown passes in Bills history, with Kelly himself cheering in the stands.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. On the same day Josh Allen became the greatest touchdown passer in franchise history, he had to deal with somebody trying to twist his head off at the goal line.

Now, the NFL’s response.

The league eventually stepped in after reviewing the film. Roquan Smith was fined 17,911 dollars for unnecessary roughness on Josh Allen.

But the league didn’t stop there.

Smith also received a second fine, another 17,911 dollars, this time for striking Bills wide receiver DJ Moore while Moore was defenseless over the middle of the field.

Let’s talk about that second one, because it doesn’t get enough attention.

DJ Moore had been battling a shoulder injury. He missed the Rams game and the Raiders game with a re-aggravated AC joint sprain. The week before the Ravens game, he practiced in a red non-contact jersey, meaning nobody was even allowed to touch him in practice. He was listed as questionable right up until kickoff.

He played anyway. He made an unbelievable catch on a deep post that Josh called a spark for the whole offense. He caught the record-breaking 259th touchdown pass. And during that same game, he took a hit while defenseless that the NFL decided was worth a fine.

A player coming back from a shoulder injury, taking a shot over the middle while he can’t protect himself. That’s exactly the kind of hit the league’s rules are supposed to prevent.

So add the two fines together, and Roquan Smith is paying 35,822 dollars for his actions in one game against the Buffalo Bills.

That’s a staggering number for a single afternoon.

Before we go any further, smash that like button if you think the NFL needs to protect players like Josh Allen and DJ Moore better. Let’s hear it, Bills Mafia.

But here’s the part that makes this even more serious.

This wasn’t Roquan Smith’s first face mask incident this season.

Back in Week 2, Smith committed a similar infraction against New Orleans Saints quarterback Tyler Shough. The NFL fined him 11,941 dollars for that play.

So the Josh Allen incident wasn’t a one-time thing. It was Smith’s second face mask-related offense of the season. Both against quarterbacks.

And when you add the Week 2 fine to the two fines from the Buffalo game, Smith has now been fined more than 47,000 dollars this season.

That’s a pattern, Bills Mafia. And patterns are exactly what the league’s discipline system is supposed to address.

Now let’s look at how Buffalo reacted, because the Bills’ response tells you a lot about this team.

Joe Brady was asked about the play the day after the game, specifically from a player safety perspective. And his answer was calm, but it carried a lot of weight.

Brady said he didn’t know anything about the play during the game. And then he revealed something important: if he had known in the moment, it probably would have affected some of the quarterback sneak calls he made later in the game.

Think about that. The head coach is saying that if he’d seen what happened to his quarterback in that pile, he might not have kept sending Josh back into it.

Brady said that, whether it was intentional or not, it’s obviously not something you want to see in football. He made a point of saying Smith is a great football player. But he said he saw Josh getting up with marks on his face. At the time, he thought it was just the normal physicality of a quarterback sneak. And then he said he was surprised nothing was called. But he added that it’s not his place to make that call.

That’s classic Joe Brady. He doesn’t attack the officials. He doesn’t go after the opposing player. But he makes it crystal clear: that play should have been flagged.

And Josh Allen? When reporters asked him about it during the week, he shrugged it off. He said he doesn’t think that kind of thing happens very often at the goal line. But he said it’s football, a physical game, and stuff like that is going to happen.

No complaints. No calls for a suspension. No drama.

Bills Mafia, that’s why this team follows him. Josh Allen doesn’t need the league to fight his battles. He just keeps scoring touchdowns.

A lot of Bills fans believe the punishment doesn’t go far enough. Dave Meyers of the “Built in Buffalo” podcast didn’t hold back at all. He said Smith should be suspended for trying to pull Josh Allen’s head off.

And that’s exactly where the controversy continues. A lot of fans believe Smith deserved a suspension, especially considering his history of fines this season for plays involving opponents.

So let’s look at this debate honestly, because there are real arguments on both sides.

The case for a suspension starts with the pattern. Smith was fined for a face mask against a quarterback in Week 2. Then he did it again in Week 8, this time to Josh Allen. And in that same game, he was fined for hitting a defenseless receiver. That’s three fined plays in one season, and two of them involve the face masks of quarterbacks.

If the purpose of discipline is to change behavior, the argument goes, then the first fine clearly didn’t do the job. So the league should have escalated.

And think about the stakes. Josh Allen isn’t just Buffalo’s quarterback. National analysts have called him the best player in the league. One wrong twist of the neck in that pile and Buffalo’s entire Super Bowl run could be over. If the NFL truly cares about protecting its quarterbacks, critics say, repeat face mask violations should come with more than a fine.

Now, the other side.

Suspensions in the NFL are usually reserved for the most flagrant and dangerous plays, or for players with a long history of repeat violations. A fine for unnecessary roughness is a standard response, even for a second offense. And there was no flag on the play, which some people will argue makes it harder for the league to justify a suspension, even though the league can still fine players for plays that weren’t penalized.

Brady himself said “whether it was intentional or not.” In a goal line pile, with so many bodies, some people will argue it’s not always clear what’s intentional. And Josh Allen, the player who was actually hurt, said it’s part of a physical game.

And let’s be honest: more than 47,000 dollars in fines in one season is not nothing. The league sent a message. It just didn’t send the message a lot of Bills fans wanted.

So where do I land, Bills Mafia?

I think the fines were the bare minimum. A second face mask offense on a quarterback, plus a hit on a defenseless receiver, all in the same game, should be taken seriously. The NFL hit Smith’s wallet. But if there’s a third incident, I don’t see how the league can avoid a suspension.

And I think the bigger issue is that there was no flag. That play happened at the goal line, right in front of the officials, during one of the most important moments of the game. That should have been called on the field. If it had been, the conversation would be completely different.
