BIG BLOW! BILLS CUT C.J. GARDNER-JOHNSON!

Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills just released C.J. Gardner-Johnson, one day after his suspension ended. This is the safety who spit toward Brock Bowers in Las Vegas, and Buffalo let him go the moment he was eligible to play again. He was only six games into his time with the Bills. He started all six. And now he’s gone.

If you think this was all about the spit, I understand. That’s what it looks like from the outside. But the people who cover this team every day are telling a very different story. And today, we’re breaking down the real reason the Bills made this move.

Let’s get into it.

The Bills made it official on Tuesday, November 3rd. To understand why, we have to go back to Las Vegas.

Week 6, October 18th. Bills at Raiders. Right before halftime, Gardner-Johnson and Brock Bowers were jawing at each other after a play. Then Gardner-Johnson spit in Bowers’s direction. A flag came out, and he was ejected. His day was over. The referee said the umpire saw the whole thing, and the league office in New York backed up the call.

We all remember what happened next. The Raiders tacked on a field goal and tied the game at halftime. Buffalo had to scramble at safety for the entire second half. Damar Hamlin stepped in and calmed things down. And the Bills still won, 33 to 30.

But after the game, Joe Brady did not hold back. He said the discipline of this team has to improve, and that what happened absolutely can’t happen. He said the team is bigger than that, the situation is bigger than that, everything is bigger than that.

Gardner-Johnson spoke after the game too. He said he had to control his emotions. But when a reporter asked him directly whether he spit on a player, he wouldn’t say yes. He described it as competing in the heat of the moment.

Then 49ers fullback Kyle Juszczyk jumped on social media. He claimed Gardner-Johnson had spit on him last year, too, back when Gardner-Johnson was with the Bears. And Juszczyk said this was the least surprising thing ever.

On the other sideline, Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak praised his players for not retaliating. He said his team knew going into the game that certain players would try to get under their skin, and that anyone who took the bait would be standing next to him on the sideline. He didn’t name names. But the Raiders were clearly ready for exactly this kind of game.

Later that night, Gardner-Johnson posted an apology on Instagram, directed at Bowers, his teammates, and both organizations. He wrote that there was no excuse for his actions and that he took full accountability.

So the apology was there. The real question was whether his play on the field would back it up.

The next day, the NFL made its decision: a one-game suspension without pay, for violating the league’s sportsmanship rule. That meant he would miss the Ravens game on November 1st.

And here’s the part that makes Tuesday so strange.

That same Monday after the Raiders game, Joe Brady stood up for him. Yes, Brady called the spit inexcusable. But he also said the team would not add any punishment of its own on top of the league’s. And then he said this: C.J. is a member of the Buffalo Bills, and they brought him here for a reason.

So the head coach backs him. The league suspends him for one game. He serves it. And one day later, he’s released.

If the spit was the reason, why didn’t they release him right after the spit? Why wait more than two weeks?

Keep that question in your head, Bills Mafia. The answer is the whole story.

Let’s start with how he actually played on the field.

Pro Football Focus grades every player in the league, and the higher the number, the better. Gardner-Johnson’s overall grade this season was 55.2. Against the run, it was even worse: 50.0. For a player Buffalo signed to be a starter, that’s near the bottom of the league.

Then there’s the tackling. He missed nine tackles in just six games. Two of those came in the first half against the Raiders, before he was even ejected.

And Bills Mafia, a safety is the last line of defense between the ball carrier and the end zone. When a safety misses a tackle, a short gain can turn into a long one, or a touchdown. Joe Brady said just last week that the defense had to clean up its tackling. Jim Leonhard said the defense wasn’t doing its most important job, keeping points off the board.

In coverage, quarterbacks completed 64.7 percent of their passes when throwing at Gardner-Johnson, with a passer rating of 83.5. Put simply, throwing at him wasn’t a bad idea for opposing offenses.

To be fair, it wasn’t all bad. He made 26 tackles and had one interception, which he returned 41 yards. He had his moments. But a starting safety needs to be more than a player who has moments.

And then came the part that really changed everything.

After the bye in Week 7, the Bills had to play the Ravens without him. And they won 36 to 10.

Damar Hamlin and Geno Stone played alongside starter Cole Bishop, and they were steady. The defense held Baltimore to 10 points, the fewest Buffalo had allowed all season. The Ravens finished with just 68 passing yards. Zay Flowers, one of the most dangerous receivers in the league, had zero catches on five targets. And Damar Hamlin credited Jim Leonhard’s game plan for taking away the space Flowers usually gets.

Now, let’s be honest. Baltimore was banged up. The Ravens lost Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley, and had undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano at quarterback for most of the day. So it wasn’t the hardest test.

But it answered the one question the Bills needed answered: can we win without him?

Yes.

ESPN reports that the feeling inside the building is that the players already on the roster played well enough to earn a longer look, with or without Gardner-Johnson.

And there’s another detail most fans missed. Through the first six weeks, Gardner-Johnson didn’t play a single special teams snap. Meanwhile, Damar Hamlin, Geno Stone, and rookie Jaylon Kilgore all contribute on special teams.

And remember what Joe Brady said this week about special teams. He said one metric site ranks Buffalo’s special teams number one in the league, and he called those units the ethos of the entire football team. He said if you want to see what kind of team you want to be, watch your special teams.

So if Gardner-Johnson isn’t clearly the best safety on the field, he’s taking a roster spot from a player who can do more for the team.

And remember, before Buffalo, he had already played for six teams in the past five years. The Bills knew he came with a short leash.

Now, back to the big question: why wait?

Here’s how the timing worked. While Gardner-Johnson was suspended, he didn’t count against the 53-man roster, the list of players a team is allowed to carry for games. So on October 31st, the Bills used that open spot to sign defensive back Te’Cory Couch to the active roster.

Once the suspension ended, Gardner-Johnson needed his roster spot back. Somebody had to go.

The Bills kept Couch. They let Gardner-Johnson go.

So the suspension didn’t cause the release. It just set the date.

But that still doesn’t explain why Buffalo picked a depth defensive back over a Super Bowl champion. So here’s the real reason.

NFL insider Cameron Wolfe reported that the release had more to do with how inconsistent Gardner-Johnson’s play was than with the spitting incident. He reported that the team felt there were too many plays where people were left wondering what he was doing.

That’s the key. Not the spit. The plays.

Newsweek reported it the same way: it was the inconsistency that led to the release, not so much the spitting. And they pointed out that this goes against what Brady said back in October, when he defended Gardner-Johnson. The Bills signed him to a one-year deal worth up to 6 million dollars, hoping he would bring energy to the back end of this defense.

And Pro Football Talk heard it from another angle. A source told them the Bills decided Gardner-Johnson was simply too high-maintenance. In other words, the talent and production weren’t big enough to be worth the headaches.

And honestly, Buffalo knew this was possible.

Last season, the Houston Texans released him after just three games. Reports said there was friction behind the scenes. He wasn’t happy with his role, and he wanted to blitz more. Then he went to the Ravens and never played a game for them. He finished the season with the Bears.

And back in 2024, he was ejected in a game against the Commanders and made an obscene gesture toward the crowd on his way off the field.

Gardner-Johnson told a different story about Houston this summer. He said the trouble started when someone close to the Texans insulted him during training camp. But Texans head coach DeMeco Ryans said the release was his decision.

Either way, it’s the same pattern. He shows up, he brings the energy, and then it ends fast.

And players around the league know who he is too. In The Athletic’s anonymous player poll, he was voted the biggest trash talker in the NFL in 2023. The following year, he won the poll’s “most annoying player” category.

And this is the part that stings. Before the season, Pro Football Talk reported that general manager Brandon Beane did his homework on Gardner-Johnson and made sure he understood the ground rules. Gardner-Johnson knew people already expected the Bills to release him, and he vowed that he was going to stick around.

He made it six games.

So what were the Bills actually betting on?

The 2024 version. That year, with the Super Bowl champion Philadelphia Eagles, PFF graded him at 77.0. He had six interceptions and helped Philadelphia win a title.

Then in 2025, bouncing between teams, his grade dropped to 51.4.

This season, he was at 55.2.

Buffalo was hoping to get the 2024 player back. What they got looked a lot more like the 2025 player, plus the drama.

Now, here’s what it costs.

The Bills had about 3.93 million dollars in cap space before this move. Releasing Gardner-Johnson frees up roughly 500,000 dollars in cap space, but it also leaves 2.02 million dollars in dead money. Dead money is salary that still counts against the cap for a player who’s no longer on the team.

So the Bills are paying for him not to play here. That tells you how sure they are about this decision.

And with the trade deadline on November 10th, every dollar matters. We’ve talked about how hard it would be for Buffalo to add help at safety or linebacker with less than four million in cap space. This move doesn’t open up a lot of room, but it does clear a roster spot and send a message about the kind of team Buffalo wants to be.

Not everybody agrees with the move, though.

Former NFL linebacker Emmanuel Acho said the Bills just lost their enforcer, and that sometimes you need a player like Gardner-Johnson to win. His point is that Buffalo doesn’t have a tough guy on defense who sets the tone.

And look, there’s something to that. In 2024, with the Eagles, Gardner-Johnson had six interceptions and helped win a Super Bowl. Edge matters in January.

But that wasn’t the player Buffalo was getting this year.

And let’s be honest about where things stand. Buffalo is six and one. Damar Hamlin and Geno Stone just helped hold a team to 10 points. The defense finally found its identity. Joe Brady said the style, the way the players flew around and played for one another, is now the expectation. Davidson Igbinosun said the defense has created its identity. And Sports Illustrated is already floating names the Bills could trade for if they want more help at safety before the deadline.

For now, Damar Hamlin is in line to start.

And Bills Mafia, that’s a big moment. Every Bills fan knows Damar’s story. The cardiac arrest on the field in January 2023. The comeback. Now he’s a Nation of Lifesavers ambassador with the American Heart Association. Joe Brady said this week that Damar has a unique skill set, knows what to do, plays winning football whenever he’s in there, and that he’s earned it. Jim Leonhard has called him a true veteran who communicates well and doesn’t get enough credit for everything he does.

And now, heading into Monday Night Football against the Vikings, Damar Hamlin is the starter.

As for Gardner-Johnson, he’s on waivers now. That means any team can claim him. If no one does, he becomes a free agent and can sign anywhere. Some people are already calling for him to return to Philadelphia, where he won that Super Bowl.

So here’s the bottom line.

The real reason the Bills released C.J. Gardner-Johnson wasn’t the spit. The spit just set the date.

The real reason was what happened on the field. The 55.2 PFF grade. The nine missed tackles in six games. The inconsistent plays. And a team that decided the drama wasn’t worth it anymore, especially after it saw what the defense could do without him.

And honestly, Bills Mafia, I think it’s the right call. This defense just played its best game of the season. It found an identity built on trust, communication, and playing for one another. Joe Brady said he doesn’t want anyone playing outside the system. And the best way to protect that identity is to keep the players who fit it.
