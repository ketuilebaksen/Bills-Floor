Let’s set the scene first.

Stephen A. was in ESPN’s New York studios, fired up as always, joined by Jeff Saturday and Chris Canty. Before he even got started, he was joking that the other two were already biased against him and that he was “operating at a deficit.” Classic Stephen A. Then he got into it: his top five teams in the National Football League.

And Bills Mafia, the timing of this matters. Buffalo just finished a brutal road stretch. They left home after a loss, flew across the country, won a Monday night thriller against the Rams, stayed out west, and then went into Las Vegas and beat the Raiders. Two road games, two wins. Now the Bills are in their bye week, resting up and resetting before they return to action in Week 8. And while the players are recovering, the national media is talking about them. That tells you exactly where this team stands right now.

So let’s go through the list the way Stephen A. did, from number five up to number one, because where Buffalo landed only makes sense once you see who’s around them.

At number five, Stephen A. put the Chicago Bears.

He was hyped about Caleb Williams. He even said Caleb is going to win a Super Bowl one day. He pointed to Chicago’s two running backs, the wide receivers, and a defense he said has improved a lot from last year. And he gave a ton of credit to head coach Ben Johnson, saying Johnson has a real connection with his young quarterback. He contrasted that with the noise you sometimes hear about Dan Quinn and Jayden Daniels in Washington, and said you don’t hear that kind of thing in Chicago.

His bottom line: the Bears are going to be a team to be reckoned with. They just aren’t top four.

And that’s where our guys come in.

At number four: the Buffalo Bills.

Let’s go through exactly what Stephen A. said, because there’s a lot packed into it.

He started with the obvious. Josh Allen is just special.

Then came the critique. Stephen A. said he is not sold on Buffalo’s defense yet. He didn’t sugarcoat it. But he followed it with what might be one of the biggest compliments you can give a quarterback in this league: no matter what’s happening, somehow, someway, Buffalo is going to be in the game, because Josh Allen is on the other side of the ball.

Think about how powerful that statement is, Bills Mafia. Stephen A. is basically saying that the floor of this team is high because of one man. Whatever goes wrong, whatever chaos happens, Josh Allen keeps you alive.

Then he went through the supporting cast. He said it could be Keon Coleman. It could be DJ Moore. It could be James Cook. And then he added a line that’s going to get a reaction from a lot of Bills fans. He said they could catch a win one day and fumble the next, and it doesn’t matter.

You know he’s talking about that late moment in the Raiders game, when James Cook had the ball security issue in the four-minute situation. That’s what people outside Buffalo noticed.

And then Stephen A. went right at the controversy. He said you can have guys doing “stupid stuff” like spitting and getting themselves suspended, and it still doesn’t matter, because Josh Allen still gives you a chance.

Of course, he’s referring to C.J. Gardner-Johnson, who was ejected from the Raiders game after the officials ruled he spit toward a Raiders player. Stephen A. said “suspended” on air. What we know for sure is that he was disqualified from that game after a review from New York, and Joe Brady made it crystal clear afterward that it can’t happen and that it would be addressed. So keep that in mind when you hear that clip.

Stephen A.’s conclusion was that Josh Allen is going to keep producing victories, and right now he has the Bills as a top four team in the league.

So let’s sit with this for a second.

On one hand, this is a huge compliment. Stephen A. Smith ranked Buffalo fourth in the entire NFL. Out of thirty-two teams, he has only three ahead of the Bills.

On the other hand, listen to how he framed it. The ranking is almost entirely about Josh Allen. The defense, he isn’t sold on. The penalties and discipline issues, he mentioned. The fumble, he mentioned. It’s basically: this team has flaws, but it has Josh Allen, so it’s dangerous.

And here’s the honest truth, Bills Mafia. That’s a fair critique. We talked about it on this channel after the Raiders game. Buffalo’s defense gave up a lot of points. Joe Brady was asked directly whether this team can keep giving up 30, and he said all that matters is finding a way to win, but that the defense is going to clean it up. Brady himself said this team has not played its best football yet. So Stephen A. isn’t saying anything the Bills don’t already know.

But here’s where I push back a little. Stephen A. talks about Josh Allen like he’s carrying a broken team on his back, and that just isn’t the full picture. Keon Coleman has been on fire, with three touchdowns and 167 yards over his last three games. Khalil Shakir keeps turning quick throws into big plays. James Cook, fumble aside, was outstanding against the Raiders, and Joe Brady said he’s going to keep handing him the football. And on defense, Dorian Williams made a game-changing interception against Kirk Cousins, and Christian Benford stepped up to take on a tough matchup.

So yes, Josh Allen is the engine. But this is not a one-man show. This is a team that has found a way to win through a cross-country road trip, a Monday night prime-time game, an ejection, and a short week. That’s not just a quarterback. That’s a culture.

Drop a comment right now: do you think Stephen A. is giving the rest of the roster enough credit, or is he right that it’s all about Josh? I really want to know, because I think this one splits Bills Mafia right down the middle.

Now let’s keep going through the list, because the teams above Buffalo matter just as much.

At number three, Stephen A. put the Minnesota Vikings.

The Vikings are undefeated, and Stephen A. went right to their defense with some impressive numbers. He said Minnesota’s defense ranks first in points allowed, second in yards allowed, third against the pass, and fifth against the run.

That’s a dominant defense, Bills Mafia. That’s the kind of unit that can carry a team through January.

He also talked about Kyler Murray, who’s quarterbacking Minnesota. Stephen A. said Murray isn’t knocking anybody’s socks off, pointing to two touchdowns and two interceptions, but he also said you can’t ignore that he’s completing 68 percent of his passes. Then he mentioned that Minnesota has weapons like Aaron Jones, Justin Jefferson, and T.J. Hockenson, and said whatever they get from the offense, that defense is going to secure them. He called them a team to be reckoned with, top three with no doubt, but not top two.

Now look at the contrast with Buffalo, because this is the heart of the whole debate.

Minnesota: elite defense, steady but unspectacular quarterback play, undefeated.

Buffalo: superstar quarterback, explosive offense, a defense that’s still finding itself.

Those are two completely different ways to build a contender. And Stephen A., at least this week, gave the edge to the defense-first team. Hold that thought, because one of the other panelists had something very interesting to say about that matchup, and it involves our Bills.

At number two, Stephen A. put the Kansas City Chiefs.

He pointed to Patrick Mahomes, and then he went off about a player he called “K9,” the nickname for Kenneth Walker III. Stephen A. called him a difference maker and the elite newcomer in the NFL this year.

And then he took a shot at Seattle. Stephen A. said the Seahawks, the defending Super Bowl champions, have to be asking themselves how they let that guy walk out the building. Should’ve paid the man, he said, should’ve kept him. He argued that Seattle’s offense might look better and their defense might be more rested with that kind of running back moving the football.

He also joked that it isn’t just about Taylor Swift in the booth clapping every time Travis Kelce catches a pass. He said it’s this new running back, running and catching, along with Mahomes, Eric Bieniemy, and Andy Reid, that makes Kansas City a top two team.

Later in the segment, one of the panelists added a stat about K9: he’s the first player in the Super Bowl era to have four multi-touchdown games in his team’s first five games. That’s a historic start, and for any team that has to face Kansas City, including Buffalo, it’s a real problem to think about.

And at number one, Stephen A. put the San Francisco 49ers.

He hit them with a red zone stat. He said the Niners have scored touchdowns on 17 of their 20 red zone trips this year, an 85 percent rate. And he said they’ve been winning whether Nick Bosa is out or other key players go down, crediting Kyle Shanahan and Brock Purdy for keeping that machine running. He said they looked close to flawless, especially in the first half of their Monday night game.

So here’s Stephen A.’s full top five after Week 6:

Number one, San Francisco.
Number two, Kansas City.
Number three, Minnesota.
Number four, Buffalo.
Number five, Chicago.

Now let’s get to the reactions, because this is where it got good.

The first reaction was surprisingly positive. One of the panelists joked that maybe it was the suit or maybe Stephen A. had a good night at the Garden, but he said Stephen A. got the right five teams.

That’s significant, Bills Mafia. Even the people who were supposedly “biased” against Stephen A. agreed that Buffalo belongs in the top five teams in the NFL. That’s not one person’s opinion anymore. That’s a consensus.

But he did have a nitpick. He said he would flip-flop one and two, putting Kansas City first, because he believes the 49ers are becoming, in his words, “the 48ers” with all their injuries. He was also big on K9 and brought up that historic multi-touchdown stat.

Stephen A. pushed back, arguing that he saw how good San Francisco looked even with their injuries. The other side argued that he didn’t expect to see a key Niners player back for Week 7, so he was making the prediction ahead of time. Stephen A. said these are power rankings, not standings, and that’s what analysts do. They had a fun little back and forth.

And then came the moment that matters most for us.

The other panelist said the list was solid. But he said he could possibly flip Minnesota and Buffalo.

His reasoning? He acknowledged that Minnesota hasn’t lost. But he said that if the two teams played today, he might lean toward Buffalo because Buffalo can score.

Bills Mafia, there it is. That’s the detail that changes the whole conversation.

Stephen A. ranked the Vikings above the Bills because of that elite defense. But another analyst sitting right next to him looked at the same two teams and said that, if they played head to head right now, he might take Buffalo, because of what this offense can do.

And that’s exactly the argument a lot of us have been making. Defense wins in a lot of ways, sure. But when you have Josh Allen, an explosive group of pass catchers, and a running back like James Cook, you can score on anybody. You can beat anybody. That’s the kind of team nobody wants to see in January.

So let’s step back and figure out what all of this means for Buffalo heading into Week 8.

Number one: the national respect is real.

There was a time when the Bills had to fight for every bit of national attention. Not anymore. Stephen A. Smith ranked them fourth. The panel agreed they belong in the top five. One analyst said he might put them third. Buffalo is now viewed as one of the true contenders in this league, and that’s a direct result of Josh Allen and how this team keeps finding ways to win.

Number two: the outside world sees the same flaws we see.

The defense. The discipline. Ball security. Stephen A. hit all three. That isn’t hate, Bills Mafia. That’s the honest checklist this team has to address. And the good news is that Joe Brady already said this bye week is about growth in all three phases: offense, defense, and special teams. Brady said the offense would be evaluated just as much as the defense, and that this would be an important week for the coaching staff. The flaws are known, and the staff is attacking them.

Number three: Josh Allen is still the ultimate trump card.

Stephen A. basically said that no matter what goes wrong, Josh Allen keeps you in the game. And we just watched that happen in Las Vegas. On third and eight, Maxx Crosby is closing in, and Allen escapes, keeps his eyes downfield, and hits Keon Coleman for a touchdown. That’s the play that defines this team. When you have a quarterback who can do that, your ceiling is a championship.

Number four: Buffalo’s best football is still ahead.

This is what makes the ranking so exciting. Joe Brady said after the Raiders game that his team hasn’t played its best football yet, and that the players know it and aren’t satisfied. So think about it. Buffalo is ranked as a top four team in the NFL by Stephen A. Smith while playing below its own standard. What happens if the defense tightens up during the bye? What happens if the penalties go down? What happens if this team actually plays its best football?

Then fourth won’t be the ceiling. Then we’re talking about number one.

Number five: the road ahead runs through these teams.

San Francisco, Kansas City, Minnesota, and Chicago. If Buffalo wants to win it all, these are the kinds of teams standing in the way. Kansas City has Mahomes and K9. Minnesota has that elite defense. San Francisco has that red zone machine. Every one of them is a serious test. But every one of them also has to deal with Josh Allen. And as Stephen A. said himself, he still gives you a chance.

So here’s my final take, Bills Mafia.

Stephen A. Smith ranking Buffalo fourth is a sign of respect, but I think it’s a little conservative. The reasoning that Josh Allen carries the team undersells the growth of Keon Coleman, the reliability of Khalil Shakir, the talent of James Cook, and the plays the defense has made in big moments. And I completely agree with the analyst who said that if Buffalo and Minnesota played today, he might take the Bills. When your offense can score on anybody, you’re a nightmare matchup for everybody.

But the critique of the defense is fair, and this team knows it. That’s exactly what this bye week is for. The coaches are going to dig into the tape, clean up the discipline, and come back sharper. And when the Bills return in Week 8, the whole country is going to be watching to see whether this team is a top four contender or something even bigger.

I believe it’s something bigger. I believe this team’s ceiling is a Super Bowl, and I think by the end of the season Stephen A. is going to have the Buffalo Bills higher than fourth.
