Now, the strangest story of the week: the kneel-down investigation.

Nick Wright noticed something about how the Bills end games. In four straight blowout wins, against the Ravens, the Vikings, the Jets, and the Dolphins, Buffalo has left Josh Allen in for the fourth quarter, and then pulled him at the very end, so that backup quarterback Kyle Allen can take the final kneel-downs.

Why does that matter? Because every kneel-down counts as a rushing attempt for negative yardage. So if Josh takes the kneel-downs, his rushing numbers go down.

And Wright's theory is that Buffalo is protecting Josh's rushing totals, because he said the passing numbers won't win him the MVP, so it has to be about the rushing numbers.

So Wright's show did what it called a multi-year investigation. And here's what they found.

This season, there have been two games where a backup quarterback's only snaps were kneel-downs, and Kyle Allen accounted for both of them. The rest of the league combined: zero.

Games where a quarterback played fewer than five snaps, all for kneel-downs: Kyle Allen has done it four times. The rest of the league combined has done it three times.

This year, Kyle Allen has taken eight kneel-downs, and zero pass attempts. And back in 2023, he had 13 kneel-downs and zero pass attempts.

Now, Bills Mafia, is it really about protecting Josh's stats? Maybe. But there's another obvious explanation: protecting Josh. Kneel-downs aren't totally risk-free, and when you're up big, there's no reason to have your franchise quarterback on the field longer than necessary. Plenty of coaches would make the same decision.

Either way, it's a fun story. And it shows just how closely the national media is watching everything this team does.
