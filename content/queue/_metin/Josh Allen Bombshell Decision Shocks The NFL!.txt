Bills Mafia, let me say something I truly believe: this is the year Josh Allen brings a championship to Buffalo.

I know. We’ve said it before. We’ve felt it before. And we’ve had our hearts broken before. Kansas City. Arrowhead. Thirteen seconds. Those January nights that still hurt. But Bills Mafia, this year feels different. And I’m going to tell you exactly why.

Start with the man himself. National analysts have called Josh Allen the best player in the league. He’s second in the MVP odds. This season alone, he passed Jim Kelly for the most total touchdown passes in franchise history, with Kelly cheering in the stands. He became only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards, joining Lamar Jackson, Michael Vick, Cam Newton, and Russell Wilson. And according to reports, after his three touchdowns against the Jets, he passed Peyton Manning for the most total touchdowns through his first ten seasons.

But here’s what’s really different this year. Josh Allen doesn’t have to carry this team alone anymore.

For years, the book on beating Buffalo in January was simple: force Josh to put on the superhero cape, wait for the defense to crack in the fourth quarter, and watch the season end. Not anymore. Nick Wright, one of the biggest Chiefs fans in sports media, pointed out that in two or three of Buffalo’s wins this year, Josh was just a supporting actor, and Buffalo still dominated. Against the Jets, the Bills put up 31 points, and Josh said himself that the offense didn’t even play that well.

And when Josh does need to take over, he’s doing it the smart way. Against Minnesota, without his starting left tackle and left guard, facing the most aggressive blitz Next Gen Stats has ever tracked, analysts said he beat the blitz with patience, not heroics. That’s a mature, complete quarterback. That’s a champion’s mindset.

Now look around him. James Cook, who just ran for 157 yards and a 74-yard touchdown against the Jets, and who scored in his eighth straight game. A healthy DJ Moore, who changes everything for this passing game. Keon Coleman, Khalil Shakir, Dalton Kincaid, Dawson Knox. And a defense that has gone 33 straight drives without allowing a touchdown, with Greg Rousseau leading the league in pressures and Amani Hooker tied for the league lead in interceptions.

Marcus Spears picked the Bills to win the Super Bowl before the season, and he isn’t backing off. He said Josh Allen has played too good of football, for too long, not to eventually break through in one of those three-hour playoff windows. And I agree with him.

The Bills are eight and one. They’ve never had the number one seed in the Josh Allen era. They’ve never had the first-round bye. They’ve never hosted an AFC Championship Game with Josh at quarterback. And right now, they’re in the driver’s seat for all of it. Imagine January in the new Highmark Stadium, snow falling, Bills Mafia roaring, and the rest of the AFC having to come to Buffalo.

That’s the path. And Josh Allen is the man to lead this team down it.

Now, let’s get into the latest news, because Joe Brady just stepped to the podium with a lot of updates ahead of the Dolphins game.

First, an injury concern: Christian Benford.

A reporter asked how concerned Brady is about Benford, a key player who has been banged up a few times this season and wasn’t able to finish the last game.

Brady said there’s always concern whenever players are nicked up or can’t finish a game. He said the team was only doing a walkthrough that day, and that the natural focus right now is helping everyone get their bodies back. He said he’d have a better answer about all of the injury situations at the end of the week.

But he pointed to Spencer Brown as an example. He said everyone saw last week how Brown did everything he could to get ready. And he said Benford is always going to do everything he can to get ready to go. And if he can’t, Brady said he feels confident in the players on the depth chart to step in, the way they have all season.

Bills Mafia, that depth has been one of the stories of the season. Against Minnesota, Tylan Grable and Austin Corbett stepped in on the left side of the line and held up against the toughest blitz in football. Damar Hamlin stepped in at safety after C.J. Gardner-Johnson’s ejection and suspension. And this team keeps finding a way.

A reporter then pointed out something important about the schedule. After the Dolphins game, Buffalo plays the Kansas City Chiefs on Thanksgiving night, just four days later. If a player is close to returning, does Brady approach it differently, knowing there’s such a quick turnaround?

Brady’s answer was simple. He said the only game is this week. And if they ever approached it differently, then they wouldn’t be doing everything they can to win this football game. He said they’ll worry about next week, next week.

That’s exactly the mindset Josh Allen described last week. He said this team tries to go one and oh every single week. No looking ahead. Not even to Thanksgiving night.

Next, the rookies.

A reporter pointed out that this is usually the time of year when rookies hit the “rookie wall,” the point in a long season when the grind catches up with them. How have Buffalo’s rookies handled it?

Brady said it’s always tough. He remembered his own first year in the NFL. In college, you usually hit Thanksgiving, get ready for a bowl game, and then get a nice month-long break. In the NFL, Thanksgiving is just the halfway point, and the season is long.

He said there are only so many ways to fight that. But he said the expanded College Football Playoff may actually make life a little easier for today’s rookies, because they’re more used to playing longer seasons.

And Brady said he hasn’t felt any kind of rookie wall from his group. He said there’s always a component of it for every player, the monotony of the NFL season and the grind, when it feels like there’s no light at the end of the tunnel. But he said his rookies keep progressing and getting better.

And he singled out one rookie in particular: T.J. Parker. Brady said Parker played his best football last week.

He said the coaches check in with the rookies regularly and communicate with them, to make sure they understand what it takes to play what could be 20 games this season. And he said the team rotates players to keep them as fresh as possible, because their best football has to be ahead of them.

Twenty games, Bills Mafia. That means a deep playoff run. That’s the plan.

Later, Brady was asked whether T.J. Parker has earned more playing time. Brady said every game is different, and that the opponent’s offensive structures and personnel groupings can affect who plays and how much. He said he’s never been too concerned about snap counts, as much as the game plan and how the game is going.

But he said Parker is playing confidently and understands what’s expected of him and his role. He said the whole outside linebacker room is playing really good football, and keeping those players fresh, especially late in games, only makes the team better. He said rotations often come down to how long a drive is and how many plays players are getting.

And then he said Parker is developing exactly the way the team expected. And now, like with every player, the challenge is: you had a good game, can you go do it again?

Now, the opponent: the Miami Dolphins.

A reporter pointed out that the Dolphins have only one win, but are a young team that plays with a lot of effort. What does Brady see?

Brady said they’re an extremely well-coached team. He said they had an incredible draft, and that it’s showing in how their players are performing. He praised Dolphins head coach Jeff Hafley, saying he’s been incredible at every stop of his career.

And then Brady repeated something he says all the time: he never focuses on records. It’s all about what the tape shows. And he said that if you watch the Dolphins’ games, you’ll see seven-point games in the fourth quarter, and Miami holding leads. He said it’s a division game on the road, and there will be nothing easy about it. It’s going to be as tough as it gets, just like last week was. And he said Buffalo is going to get Miami’s best shot, which is something the Bills are used to.

Bills Mafia, that’s exactly how a championship team thinks.

Then a reporter asked about the quarterbacks Buffalo has faced. In recent weeks, the Bills have faced several quarterbacks with limited NFL experience. How has that prepared them for Miami?

Brady said that, at the end of the day, you have to focus on your scheme and how you handle things. He said the previous week was a huge adjustment, because the Jets played very different types of quarterbacks during the game, just like Baltimore did a couple of weeks earlier. Remember, against the Jets, Buffalo’s defense knocked Geno Smith out of the game in the third quarter, and rookie Cade Klubnik came in. Brady credited his staff for being able to adjust and communicate the differences in each quarterback’s skill set.

But he said the team will always naturally focus on itself, making sure the communication is where it needs to be no matter who is playing quarterback on the other side.

And then he talked about Miami’s quarterback, Kyle. Brady said Buffalo liked him a lot coming out of college. He said he’s a really good football player, that he has a bit more experience now after playing in Miami’s last game, and that he did what he had to do. Brady said the Dolphins had an opportunity to win that game. And he said this will be a huge test for Buffalo.

And that matters, because the national debate about this defense has been all about the quarterbacks it has faced. Chris Broussard pointed out that the 33-drive touchdown-free streak came against Joe Fagnano, Carson Wentz, Geno Smith, and Cade Klubnik. But Brady isn’t looking at names. He’s looking at his own team’s communication and execution.

Now, here’s one of my favorite answers from the press conference.

A reporter pointed out that Buffalo has a reputation for getting every team’s best shot, especially this year, sitting at the top of the AFC. How much does Brady welcome that, and what are the challenges that come with being at the top of the mountain?

And Brady said something that really fired him up. He said it has always driven him crazy when people say a certain week is a “winnable game.” He said he doesn’t know what that even means. He said there has never been a week in his coaching career where he worked any less, or felt like the game was any easier. He said he always walks out of games absolutely exhausted from the chess match.

And then he said that it has always felt disrespectful to the players, and to the profession, when people suggest one game is less important or will be easier.

So when he says Buffalo is going to get every team’s best shot, he said, it’s because this is the NFL. You can never roll out of bed and expect to go out and execute. It’s too hard. He credited the players in the locker room for not approaching any game differently.

And then he said: when you have Josh Allen, people always want to play their best against the best.

Bills Mafia, that’s the respect Josh Allen commands around this league. Every team circles the Bills on its calendar, because they want to measure themselves against the best.

Next, Tre’Davious White.

Buffalo brought White back on the practice squad a few weeks ago. A reporter asked how he’s doing, and whether he could be ready to contribute on the field if needed.

Brady made it very clear: the Bills didn’t bring Tre’Davious White back just to be part of the organization. He said there are plenty of veterans in the locker room who can lead. They brought White back so that, if given the opportunity, he can go in and help the team play winning football. And Brady said White played really good football at the end of last season.

And then Brady had some fun describing him. He said White is genetically kind of a freak. He’s the kind of player who can roll out of bed, maybe not do it for a long time, and still look like a former first-round pick, because that’s what he is.

He said that whenever White’s time comes, there won’t be any holding your breath, because White is so calm, cool, and collected. He said you just have to watch the way White backpedals and the way he sees the field. He’s so smooth, and he’s always in control.

And then Brady laughed and said that yes, White might talk a lot. He’s a Louisiana guy through and through, and you love him for it. But Brady said there’s a real composure to his game, he’s never out of control, and he’s as bright as they come. And then he joked that when he says calm, he means on the field, because when White is talking, there’s never any calmness to him.

Bills Mafia, with Christian Benford banged up, Tre’Davious White’s experience could matter sooner than we think. A former All-Pro, ready when called upon.

Then a reporter asked about rookie wide receiver Skyler Bell, who has been on the field in three games but hasn’t caught a pass yet. How does Brady keep him involved and make sure he doesn’t get frustrated?

Brady said it’s tough. And he made an interesting point. He said people sometimes think that when a team is winning, players won’t be selfish, that they’ll be fine not getting touches. But he said Buffalo has a locker room full of players who want to keep helping the team, regardless of how many opportunities they get.

And he said Bell has never once complained. Bell understands that whatever his job is, he’s going to go do it.

Brady said the way Bell is playing reminds him a bit of Khalil Shakir’s rookie year. He acknowledged Shakir had more opportunities back then, and that Buffalo has been fortunate to have a receiver room where players keep stepping up. But he said he’s loved what he’s seen from Bell, especially his approach and his development, regardless of how the opportunities have gone. And he said the team has full confidence that if an opportunity comes, Bell will make the play.

And here’s a great detail. Brady said Bell is attached to DJ Moore’s hip. The two of them sit next to each other in meeting rooms. So Bell has a veteran showing him the way. Brady called the way Bell has approached the season very impressive.

That’s the “everybody eats” culture Josh Allen talked about last week. Everyone does their job, and eventually, it comes back around to you.

And finally, a quick roster update. Brady was asked about Tyrell Shavers. And with a smile, he said he could finally say it correctly: Shavers is now on the 53-man roster. Brady said they’ll see how the week goes before deciding whether Shavers will be active on game day.
