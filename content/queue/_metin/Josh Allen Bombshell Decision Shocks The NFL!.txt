JOSH ALLEN’S POWERFUL MESSAGE AFTER BILLS ADD THE NFL’S INTERCEPTION LEADER!

Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills are seven and one, the number one seed in the AFC, and Josh Allen just stepped to the podium with a message that every fan needs to hear. He revealed the secret behind how this team recovers from brutal games on a short week. He welcomed new safety Amani Hooker, whom he called the league leader in interceptions, and shared what the front office’s move says about the team’s belief. He opened up about who Joe Brady really is behind closed doors. He explained why James Cook gets better as games go on. And he revealed that the famous “everybody eats” mentality is alive and well in Buffalo, even if you don’t hear it out loud anymore.

Halfway through the season, this was one of the most revealing press conferences Josh has given all year.

Welcome back to Bills Floor, your daily home for everything Buffalo Bills. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on notifications, because this team is rolling and we’re covering every moment. Smash that like button too, it really helps the channel. And stick around until the end, because I want to know which part of this press conference fired you up the most.

Let’s get into it.

First, the challenge of a short week.

Buffalo just came off a brutal, physical Monday Night Football game in Minnesota, a 23 to 6 win against a defense that blitzed Josh Allen on 68.6 percent of his dropbacks, the most he’s ever been blitzed in his career. And now the Bills have a short week, another road trip, and another physical defense waiting for them.

So how do you get your body ready?

Josh said it’s about making sure everyone is doing everything they can to get their bodies right. He said players are in the training room, using the hot tub, the cold tub, the sauna, and working out to get their bodies moving and their blood flowing. And they’re taking care of whatever aches and pains they’re dealing with.

But then he made a bigger point. He said that on a short week, players have to step up. Some players may not be able to go, or they may be trying to work their way back during the week. And the players taking those reps in practice have to have the mindset that this is their week. They’re up. They’re going.

And Bills Mafia, we just saw exactly what that looks like. Against Minnesota, Dion Dawkins and Alec Anderson were both inactive. Tylan Grable stepped in at left tackle. Austin Corbett stepped in at left guard. And against the most aggressive blitz Next Gen Stats has ever tracked, they held up. Bills reporter Chris Brown said they handled it. Joe Brady gave the credit to the depth on his offensive line.

That’s the “this is my week” mentality Josh is talking about.

And Josh said he believes this is a pretty mature team, with a lot of veteran leadership in a lot of different position rooms, players who can pass that message on to everyone else.

Then a reporter asked whether Josh felt any different on Tuesday morning after facing that Minnesota defense and everything it put him through.

Josh said it’s football. It’s a contact sport. You’re going to feel the bumps and bruises throughout the season. He said you tend to feel them more early in the season than later. But he admitted it definitely gets compounded on a short week. So again, it’s about making sure everyone is doing everything they can to get their bodies right.

Next, the upcoming opponent.

Josh was asked what he sees in the defense Buffalo is about to face, a group with some young players who could be a problem.

He said the players are flying around. And then he mentioned a familiar face: former Bill Harrison Phillips, who Josh said is playing some really good football right now. That’s a reunion every Bills fan will be watching.

Josh said this defense isn’t afraid to come up and play press man coverage. He said they do a good job of disguising some of their zone coverages. And he said they’re playing together right now.

And he made a point about their head coach. He said that with a defensive-minded head coach, you know that defense is going to be extremely well coached.

So Josh said the Bills have to have a good week of practice, get the right looks from the scout team, and ultimately make sure they execute on Sunday.

Think about the challenge here, Bills Mafia. Last week, it was Brian Flores’s blitz. This week, it’s press man coverage and disguised zones. Two very different defensive styles, back to back, on a short week. That’s going to test Josh’s eyes and his receivers’ ability to win at the line of scrimmage.

But this offense has answers. Against Minnesota, when the Vikings rushed four or fewer, Josh went 9 of 11 for 126 yards and a touchdown. One analysis said Josh beat the blitz with patience, not heroics. That kind of discipline will matter against any defense.

Before we keep going, smash that like button if you’re ready for Sunday. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

Now, the big addition: Amani Hooker.

At the trade deadline, Brandon Beane sent a second-round pick to the Tennessee Titans for Hooker. And Josh was asked what that move does for the team, and whether it reinforces the message that management believes in this group.

Josh said that’s exactly what the message is: the belief is there.

And then he revealed something that should get every Bills fan excited. He said Buffalo went out and added the guy who’s leading the league in interceptions right now.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. The Bills didn’t just add a safety. They added a ball hawk. And Brandon Beane said it himself: he compared Hooker to Micah Hyde, pointing to his instincts, his ball skills, and the way he takes the ball away. Both played at Iowa. And Beane said Hooker is the kind of player who can play the deep post and track the ball, which lets Cole Bishop keep playing closer to the line of scrimmage, where Jim Leonhard has been using him.

Josh said anyone would be happy about a move like this. He said the front office did what it did, and everyone is extremely excited to have Hooker.

And then Josh talked about what comes next. He said the defensive scheme will come with time, but the most important thing is making sure Hooker feels like part of the team as quickly as possible. Making sure he’s in tune with the culture. Making sure he feels like a Buffalo Bill.

And when a reporter asked what the crash course on Bills culture looks like, Josh shared a great detail. He said he got to meet Hooker that morning, sitting down for lunch, while Hooker was going through a little tour of the facility and learning where everything was.

He said the goal is simply to welcome him. Everybody pulling him in, getting their hands on him, and telling him: hey man, we’re happy to have you here.

And then Josh described this locker room in a way that says everything. He said it’s a very, very tight-knit unit, and it’s been that way for a long time. And they want Hooker to feel like he can be exactly who he is in this building at all times.

Bills Mafia, that’s the same thing Brandon Beane said after the trade. He said what he likes most about this team is that it’s a bonded group. And you can feel it.

Next, Joe Brady.

A reporter pointed out that it can get lost in the middle of the season, but this is still Brady’s first year as a head coach, and he has the Bills off to a seven and one start. What has stood out to Josh about his approach?

Josh said every week has been the same approach. He said the team tends not to focus on the opponents, the names and faces. Instead, they focus on what they can keep getting better at, week after week.

He said the mindset is: we’re trying to go one and oh each week, and that’s the best we can do. It’s all about what they can improve on, about helping their teammates, about being the best teammates possible. And about making sure that everyone on the field feels like they can make the play, based on how they’re approaching it.

That’s exactly what Brady has been preaching. Remember before the Vikings game, Brady said the team’s standards should never rise or fall based on the opponent. He said if the players look at records or stats, they won’t play to their standard. He said it should always be about Buffalo first. And he described competitive stamina as simply playing the next play.

Then a reporter asked if anything had surprised Josh about how Brady has approached his new job.

Josh said he doesn’t know if anything has surprised him. But he said he’s been extremely happy with one thing in particular. Sometimes, when people move into positions like this, they tend to change a little. And Josh said Brady hasn’t changed one bit.

He said Brady is the same Joe Brady he was as a quarterbacks coach, the same as he was as an offensive coordinator, and the same as he is now as a head coach. The same person he’s been since Josh has known him.

And Josh said the players feel that. They understand who Brady is, not just as a coach, but as a person. He said Brady cares so much about this game, and the players can rally behind that.

Bills Mafia, that’s a huge compliment from your franchise quarterback. Brady is four for four on the road in his first season, the Bills are seven and one, and the locker room believes in him because he’s genuine.

And we’ve seen that genuineness. When Brady talked about releasing C.J. Gardner-Johnson, he said he had nothing but love for him, and shared that he had been called into an office and let go himself once, so he knows how it feels. When Brady talked about James Cook after a fumble, he said he was going to hand him the football. That’s a coach who leads with honesty and care.

Before we get to James Cook, smash that like button if you love what Joe Brady is building in Buffalo.

Now, James Cook.

A reporter pointed out that we don’t often see Cook get the kind of workload he had on Monday night. He carried the ball 31 times, the most by any player in any NFL game this season. How did Josh see him handle that?

Josh said Cook just continues to excel. He said Cook wants the ball, and Josh has been saying it for a long time: the more times Buffalo gets James Cook the ball, the better the offense is.

And Josh pointed to a specific play. Cook got hit at the line of scrimmage and still muscled out a nine-yard run. And then he capped off that 15-play drive with a touchdown. Josh called it a gritty drive, one where the team relied on Cook a lot.

That drive took 8 minutes and 32 seconds off the clock, Bills Mafia. By the end of the game, Buffalo had the ball for more than 42 minutes. The Bills ran 79 plays, and the Vikings ran just 46. And Cook’s touchdown made it a two-score game. That was his seventh straight game with a touchdown.

And then Josh said something really interesting. He said he feels like Cook tends to get better as the game goes on. As the game wears on, Cook wears on the defense.

And Josh said that’s because Cook worked extremely hard in the offseason to make sure he has the stamina, and the competitive stamina, to keep going as the game goes on.

There’s that phrase again, Bills Mafia: competitive stamina. It’s Joe Brady’s phrase. And it’s clearly become part of this team’s language.

Oh, and remember, in that same game, both James Cook and Josh Allen passed 5,000 career rushing yards, becoming the first teammates in NFL history to both reach that mark. And Cook even had to deal with a viral moment afterward, when a slow-motion replay sparked some ridiculous internet rumors, which he shut down himself with a little humor. But on the field, he was a workhorse.

Next, the halfway point.

A reporter noted that the Bills have played eight games, with nine left in the regular season. So what has Josh learned about this team that he didn’t know eight weeks ago?

Josh said he doesn’t know if he’s learned anything new. But he said a lot of things have been affirmed. The resiliency this team has. The love the players have for one another. He said no game ever feels like they’re out of it, and they’re going to keep fighting. As long as there’s time on the clock, they believe they have a chance, no matter the situation.

Bills Mafia, think about how many times we’ve seen that this season. The Monday night thriller against the Rams. The 33 to 30 win in Las Vegas, after losing a starting safety to an ejection right before halftime. The Ravens game, when Josh had a 39.8 passer rating at halftime and then took over in the second half. And then Minnesota, when the left side of the line was missing and the blitz came all night.

This team never feels out of it. That’s an identity.

And finally, the “everybody eats” mentality.

A reporter said we haven’t heard that “everybody eats” motto as much as we did last year, but that when you look at the offensive stats, especially among the pass catchers, it still looks like that’s how this offense works. Is it still something the team thinks about?

Josh said definitely. He said it might not be talked about outwardly as much. But internally, in the meeting rooms, everybody knows the mentality: I’m going to do whatever I can to help my teammate.

And he explained what that looks like on the field. Receivers who are willing to run off the safety, clearing space for someone else. Receivers who are willing to block on bubble screens. And Josh said that when you have a group full of players with that mindset, at some point it’s going to come back around to you. You’re going to get your opportunity to make a play.

He said the players have kept making plays, and he feels like they’re in sync right now.

And then Josh rattled off the numbers of his targets: DJ Moore, number 10, number 86, number zero, number 88. Khalil Shakir. Dalton Kincaid. Keon Coleman. Dawson Knox. And he said Buffalo has a lot of guys in the passing game who are simply where they’re supposed to be and doing their jobs. And he said it’s fun to have that back.

Bills Mafia, that’s exactly what ESPN’s NFL insider said a couple of weeks ago: this might not be the best skill group Josh Allen has ever had, but it’s probably the most balanced. There’s no single number one receiver like Stefon Diggs in his prime. But there are weapons everywhere. Keon Coleman leading the league in EPA per target. DJ Moore back and catching record-breaking touchdowns. Khalil Shakir winning on quick throws. Dalton Kincaid finding the end zone. Dawson Knox doing the dirty work. And three running backs who can all catch the ball.

And against Minnesota, Josh even threw a touchdown to a teammate who hadn’t been targeted all season. That’s “everybody eats” in its purest form.

So let’s put it all together.

On a short week after a brutal game, the Bills are using every recovery tool they have, and the backups have the mindset that “this is my week.”

Josh praised the upcoming defense, including former Bill Harrison Phillips, and said they play press man and disguise their zones well.

He said the trade for Amani Hooker, the league’s interception leader, shows the belief in this team, and that the priority is making him feel like a Buffalo Bill.

He said Joe Brady hasn’t changed one bit since becoming head coach, and that players rally behind how much he cares.

He said James Cook gets better as games go on, thanks to the competitive stamina he built in the offseason.

He said the halfway point has affirmed this team’s resiliency and love for one another.

And he said the “everybody eats” mentality is alive and well inside the building.

Bills Mafia, this team is seven and one, bonded, balanced, and still improving. And now it has a ball-hawking safety in the back end.
