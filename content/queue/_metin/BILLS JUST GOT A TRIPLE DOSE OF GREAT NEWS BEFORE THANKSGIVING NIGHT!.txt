Great news number one comes straight out of Kansas City.

Chiefs running back Kenneth Walker III missed practice again on Monday with a groin injury. He's already missed the last two games, and Monday's report didn't show him getting any closer to returning.

And Bills Mafia, this isn't some backup. Even after missing two games, Walker still ranks fifth in the NFL in rushing, with 872 yards. He's averaging 5.5 yards every time he carries the ball. He's Kansas City's bell-cow back.

Chiefs fans are already worried, and you can see it all over social media. And Andy Reid has been careful with him. Reid said that if playing won't further injure Walker, he'll play. In plain English: if there's real risk, Walker sits.

And with Thursday's game coming just four days after Sunday, there isn't much time to heal. Plus, after Thursday, Kansas City gets 10 days before its next game, which might make the Chiefs even more cautious.

Why does this matter so much? Because Buffalo's biggest defensive weakness right now is the run. Against Miami, the Bills allowed about five yards per carry. If Kansas City can run the ball well, it can control the clock, keep Josh Allen on the sideline, and avoid obvious passing downs where Greg Rousseau and Buffalo's pass rush can take over.

So Walker being out would be a big break for Buffalo.

But here's the part no one in Buffalo wants to hear: the Chiefs won both games without him. They beat Atlanta 35 to 13, and Arizona 34 to 7. Rookie running back Emmett Johnson stepped in against the Falcons and ran for 101 yards. He also caught six passes for 70 yards and scored two touchdowns.

So a missing Walker helps Buffalo, but it doesn't mean Kansas City can't run the ball. Keep an eye on the final injury report. If Walker practices, this story flips.

Great news number two: something Bills fans have wanted for months. Three big-name receivers are now available, or close to it, and one of them has already worn a Bills uniform.

Let's start with Brandin Cooks.

The 49ers waived Cooks on Saturday, and he cleared waivers. That means no team claimed him, and he can now sign with any team.

Bills Mafia, you know this guy. Buffalo signed Cooks on November 25th, 2025, almost exactly one year ago. And in last season's Wild Card win over Jacksonville, he caught three passes for 58 yards. He already knows this offense. He already knows Josh Allen.

But there's a catch. Niners Nation pointed out that the 49ers could bring him right back to their practice squad now that he's cleared waivers. So Cooks might not be on the market for long.

Next, a name Bills fans have been dreaming about for two years: Brandon Aiyuk.

Aiyuk is officially done in San Francisco. The 49ers waived him on Monday, ending a standoff that lasted months. He had to sit on waivers for 24 hours before becoming available.

And I know a lot of you want him in Buffalo. But NFL Network's Ian Rapoport says his most likely landing spot is the Washington Commanders' practice squad.

And here's where it gets wild. Aiyuk hasn't played a single game since he tore two ligaments in his knee on October 20th, 2024. And the team he got hurt against? The Kansas City Chiefs. Plus, Washington's quarterback, Jayden Daniels, is a close friend of his from Arizona State.

So for now, Aiyuk to Buffalo is a fan dream, not a report.

But the third name has real numbers behind him: Tyreek Hill.

Hill is still a free agent. And on prediction markets for his next team, the two teams at the top are the Chiefs and the Bills. Kansas City sits at 18 percent. Buffalo sits at 14 percent.

That's a big jump. In a snapshot from November 21st, Buffalo was at just 6 percent. In a matter of days, the Bills' chances more than doubled.

Now, the reality check. Hill's agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said Hill hasn't been cleared yet, and he's still working to get back to 100 percent. Hill dislocated his left knee and tore his ACL on September 29th, 2025. He hasn't played since, and he just flew to Germany for treatment.

So Tyreek Hill can't help anyone on Thursday night.

But think about January. If the Chiefs bring back their old number one receiver, he could be lining up against Buffalo in the playoffs. So keeping Hill away from Kansas City might matter as much as getting him.

And remember, Buffalo's receiving room is already strong. DJ Moore just became the first Bill since Emmanuel Sanders in 2021 to catch four touchdowns in his first eight games with the team. Khalil Shakir just had 101 receiving yards against Miami. Four different Bills receivers have had 100-yard games this season. So any addition would be a luxury, not a necessity.

Great news number three: Josh Allen's legs against the Chiefs.

Against Miami, Josh didn't have a single rushing attempt. ESPN's Field Yates pointed out that, if you leave out games where Josh left almost right away, that was the first time in his entire career he didn't run the ball once.

And Kansas City defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo noticed. And he isn't buying it.

Spagnuolo said he thinks it was a setup. He said he believes Buffalo will have a good number of designed quarterback runs on Thursday night. A designed run is a play called for the quarterback to keep the ball and run it himself.

And Spags has a point. Kansas City knows exactly what Josh Allen's legs can do. In his last 10 games against the Chiefs, Josh has 100 carries for 506 yards and seven rushing touchdowns. That's about 50 rushing yards per game against Kansas City.

Now, Josh never said that zero carries was part of a plan. He gave Miami credit, saying the Dolphins did a great job of staying sticky on his receivers and taking away the space when he got out of the pocket. Some analysts guessed it was by design, to protect Josh from hits before the Chiefs game.

So was Sunday a setup, or did Miami just take it away? We'll find out on Thursday. But either way, Josh Allen will be fresh, and Kansas City knows what's coming.

Now, the news that should worry you.

The story everybody is talking about isn't Josh's legs. It's his arm.

ESPN's Dan Orlovsky went on "The Pat McAfee Show" and said that the Miami game was probably the most concerning game he's seen from Josh in two or three seasons. He said Josh threw two interceptions, and it could have been four.

And the numbers back him up. Josh has thrown five interceptions in his last four games, after throwing just four in his first six. And against Miami, a third interception was wiped out by a holding penalty.

That brings Josh to nine interceptions through 10 games. That's just one fewer than he threw in the entire 2025 regular season.

To his credit, Josh owned it. After the game, he said one interception was tipped and the other was a bad decision, and that he has to clean that up.

And interestingly, Orlovsky got roasted the next morning for praising Miami's defense before the game. Some fans said he was covering for Josh. But whatever you think of Orlovsky, the numbers are the numbers.

And that brings us to the story I believe could decide Thursday night. It's not a trade. It's not an injury. It's the weather.

WGRZ's John Scott said his station's weather team told him conditions for Thursday night will be about as poor as it gets: steady rain, and temperatures in the upper 30s, before, during, and after the game.

And there's even a perfect Buffalo twist. The Goo Goo Dolls are playing at halftime, with rain expected. And their famous 2004 performance of "Iris" in Buffalo also happened in the pouring rain. Bills Mafia, it doesn't get much more Buffalo than that.

But rain changes football. A wet ball is harder to throw and harder to catch. It's harder to hold onto. And it usually means more running and fewer big passing plays.

Now, put it all together.

The Bills are nine and one and hold the AFC's number one seed. The Chiefs are eight and two, and Patrick Mahomes has thrown eight touchdown passes over his last two games.

Josh Allen and Patrick Mahomes have split their 10 meetings, five and five. But look at how it breaks down. Josh is five and one against Mahomes in the regular season. And Mahomes is four and oh against Josh in the playoffs.

That's what makes this number one seed so important. Whoever wins it gets to make the other team come to them in January. And for Buffalo, that would mean a potential playoff rematch in the new Highmark Stadium, instead of Arrowhead.

So here's my answer to which story decides Thursday night: Josh Allen protecting the football.

Think about it. You've got a wet ball. A quarterback with five interceptions in his last four games. And a Chiefs team that turns mistakes into points. Yahoo Sports put it this way: against Kansas City, a turnover in Bills territory is likely seven points the other way.

And remember Spagnuolo's "setup" theory. If Josh does run more on Thursday, that might be the smartest thing he can do in the rain. Tuck it, take the yards, and don't hand Mahomes a short field.

And don't forget James Cook. In the rain, a running back who just broke an NFL record with nine straight games of 75-plus rushing yards and a touchdown is exactly the kind of weapon you want. Joe Brady said after the Miami game that Cook is playing as well as anybody in the NFL right now, and that the team didn't give him the ball enough. Rain or no rain, Cook should be the centerpiece on Thursday night.

And on defense, Buffalo has gone 43 straight drives without allowing a touchdown. In bad weather, a defense that forces field goals instead of touchdowns becomes even more valuable.
