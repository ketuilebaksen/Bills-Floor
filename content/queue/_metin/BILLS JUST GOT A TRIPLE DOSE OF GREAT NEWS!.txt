BILLS JUST GOT A TRIPLE DOSE OF GREAT NEWS!

Bills Mafia, listen up, because the national experts are talking about the Buffalo Bills, and what they’re saying could completely change how you look at this season. After everything this fan base has been through, you know how long we’ve been waiting for a moment like this. And right now, the Bills are sitting in a position that could set up the most important January in years.

Today we’ve got a triple dose of great news. Joe Brady is off to a historic start as a first-time head coach. Josh Allen is climbing the MVP race. And the number one seed in the AFC, with every playoff game going through Buffalo, is in Buffalo’s hands. But the experts also raised one concern you need to hear before Sunday’s game against the Jets.

Smash that like button too, it really helps the channel. And stick around until the end, because I want to know if you agree with the experts.

Let’s get into it.

First, the matchup. This Sunday, it’s Bills at Jets. And when the panel previewed it, the first thing they said was simple: Buffalo had better block David Bailey.

Remember, Josh Allen mentioned this Jets defense in his press conference this week. He said the players are flying around. He said they aren’t afraid to play press man coverage, and that they do a good job disguising their zone coverages. He said that with a defensive-minded head coach, you know the defense is going to be extremely well coached. And he gave a shoutout to a familiar face, former Bill Harrison Phillips, who Josh said is playing really good football right now.

So Buffalo has to be ready for pressure from David Bailey, press coverage on the outside, and disguised zones. On a short week. On the road. After a physical Monday night game in Minnesota.

But this offense is rolling. And that brings us to great news number one.

Joe Brady is off to the best offensive start for a first-time head coach since Sean McVay in 2017.

Let that sink in, Bills Mafia. Sean McVay was the coach everyone called an offensive genius when he took over the Rams. And Joe Brady, in his very first year as a head coach, is matching that kind of start.

The Bills are averaging 32 points per game. And they’re the number one seed in the AFC heading into Sunday’s game.

So the panel asked a great question: Brady was already calling plays in Buffalo for the last couple of years as offensive coordinator, so what has changed? Has becoming head coach unlocked this offense, and Josh Allen, even more?

One analyst, Pete, gave a few reasons.

First, DJ Moore. Pete said that when Moore is on the field, he opens everything up. When you have a true number one receiver, everybody else moves down a notch. The number one becomes the number two, the number two becomes the number three. And that makes the whole group more dangerous.

Second, Dalton Kincaid being healthy. Pete said that’s important to Buffalo’s passing game.

And third, the Bills are running the ball when they need to. Pete said that combination is why the offense is so good right now.

And then he said something that every Bills fan needs to hear. He said that when you talk to people around the league about the playoffs, teams, personnel people, general managers, they’ll say they feel good about where they’re at, and that the AFC playoffs are going to be tough. But what they can’t account for is Buffalo. What they can’t account for is Josh Allen.

Pete said that when Josh goes above the X’s and O’s and just becomes Josh Allen, there’s nothing you can do about it. He pointed to the Rams game, when Josh won by running all over the place. And he said you can’t account for everything Josh can do, and that’s what makes this offense truly special.

Bills Mafia, that’s coming from people around the league. The thing they fear most in January? Josh Allen.

The panel also brought up the touchdown against Minnesota, when Josh broke contain and found the team’s fourth tight end on the depth chart for a touchdown. That was the player’s first career touchdown, on his first target of the season. And they joked that if you’re Brian Flores, you’re standing on the sideline thinking: what else am I supposed to do?

And then another analyst made the point that really explains this Bills offense. He said Joe Brady has done a masterful job attacking each opponent based on what that team does well, and having a counterpunch ready.

He walked through it, game by game.

Week 1, against the Houston Texans. The year before, Josh Allen had been battered by Houston’s defensive line. So Brady decided he wasn’t going to let Josh get touched. Buffalo came out in heavy personnel, with extra blockers, to protect Josh. And then they took shots down the field. The analyst said you could tell the Texans weren’t ready for it. And Buffalo hit those shots.

Then the Rams game. The Rams’ cornerbacks are really good in man coverage, and Buffalo’s receivers weren’t getting separation. So what did Josh do? The analyst joked that Josh put his cape on and ran. Josh took over with his legs.

And then Minnesota. The panel said they expected Buffalo to try to establish the run early, and the Bills did try with James Cook. But the Vikings were up to the challenge early. So what did Brady do? He recognized that Minnesota’s offense was struggling. So he decided not to put the ball in harm’s way. He understood that a punt was a win at that point. He played the field position game. And he knew that eventually, Minnesota’s defense would wear down.

And when it did, Brady leaned on James Cook in the fourth quarter and sealed the game.

The panel also pointed out that Brady used quick screens to the perimeter to counter all the pressure Minnesota was sending up the middle. Once the Bills got the ball outside, it opened up the run game. They admitted it took a little while to figure out, but Brady got there.

Bills Mafia, that’s exactly what Dan Orlovsky said earlier this season. He said Buffalo’s next step was figuring out where to go when a great defense takes away its core plays. Against Minnesota, the defense that took away everything, Brady found the answer.

And the numbers back it up. Cook carried the ball 31 times, the most by any player in any game this season. One drive went 15 plays and burned 8 minutes and 32 seconds. Buffalo held the ball for more than 42 minutes. And Minnesota scored just six points.

Before we go any further, smash that like button if Joe Brady is your Coach of the Year. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

Another analyst said he loves what Brady is doing because the team is playing great complementary football. Offense, defense, and special teams, all working together. He pointed to all the big special teams plays this season: kickoff returns, punt returns, and even an opponent muffing a punt with Buffalo recovering it. He said it seems like the Bills are doing everything right, and that’s why they’re the number one seed.

That lines up with what Joe Brady said recently. He told reporters that one metric site ranks Buffalo’s special teams number one in the league, and that the special teams units represent the ethos of the entire football team.

The same analyst said this offense is clicking at every level. Yes, DJ Moore is unlocking everything. But he said you have to realize Keon Coleman is stepping up too, which makes everything better. And he pointed to the emergence of James Cook this season.

And that’s the balance ESPN’s NFL insider talked about a few weeks ago: maybe not the best skill group Josh Allen has ever had, but probably the most balanced. Josh said it himself this week, listing off DJ Moore, number 10, number 86, number zero, and number 88, and saying that everyone is where they’re supposed to be and doing their job. The “everybody eats” mentality is alive in Buffalo.

Now, here’s the concern the panel raised. And it’s fair.

One analyst said he still has some concerns about Buffalo’s defense. He called the Amani Hooker trade a big move, and said Hooker is a really good player who should help a secondary that has struggled. And he acknowledged the defense has played better.

But then the panel pointed out who that defense has been playing. Against Minnesota, it was Carson Wentz. And against Baltimore, Lamar Jackson went out early, and the Ravens were basically running a Wildcat offense with Derrick Henry. So the panel said they’ll learn more about this defense going forward, because the Jets can actually push the ball down the field.

Bills Mafia, I understand that argument. But let’s look at the full picture. Against Baltimore, Jim Leonhard blitzed on 64.5 percent of dropbacks and held the Ravens to 68 passing yards. Zay Flowers, one of the league’s most dangerous receivers, had zero catches. Against Minnesota, Leonhard blitzed just 11.5 percent of the time and still generated more pressure than Brian Flores’s defense. The Vikings went zero for the night on third down. Two completely different game plans, two dominant results.

Joe Brady said after the Baltimore game that he didn’t think the result was just about the backup quarterbacks. He said, based on how the team practiced, he expected that result regardless.

And now, Amani Hooker joins the secondary. Josh Allen called him the league leader in interceptions. Brandon Beane compared him to Micah Hyde.

So yes, the Jets game is a test. But this defense is trending in the right direction.

Now, great news number two: the MVP race.

The panel pointed out that Josh Allen is number two in the MVP odds, right behind Joe Burrow, and that those two quarterbacks lead the two best offenses in the league.

Pete said it’s still a race, and way too early to crown anyone. He said Josh will be in the mix, along with Joe Burrow, Brock Purdy, and Trevor Lawrence. And then the panel added Jordan Love, though they noted Green Bay already has three losses.

One analyst listed his top three as Burrow, Purdy, and Love, with Josh at number four. But another analyst pushed back, saying that if Buffalo locks up the number one seed and Josh keeps doing his thing, he’s going to shoot up the list.

And that’s the key, Bills Mafia. In the MVP race, team success matters. And right now, Buffalo is the number one seed in the AFC.

And think about what Josh has done just in the last few weeks. He passed Jim Kelly for the most total touchdown passes in franchise history. He tied Peyton Manning’s touchdown total through ten seasons. He became only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards. And he beat the most aggressive blitz Next Gen Stats has ever tracked with patience instead of heroics.

If that’s not an MVP résumé, I don’t know what is.

And now, great news number three, and maybe the biggest: the number one seed.

The panel spent a lot of time on this, and for good reason. If Buffalo finishes as the number one seed in the AFC, it gets a first-round bye. And every playoff game in the AFC has to come through Highmark Stadium.

One analyst asked whether anyone on the panel had ever visited Buffalo in mid-to-late January. He said it’s not on anyone’s bucket list, not with those conditions. Frigid temperatures. That fan base. Imagine having to go there and play for a trip to the Super Bowl.

He said that’s why this number one seed is so critical. And he pointed specifically to the Jacksonville Jaguars, a Florida team. Do they really want to go up to Buffalo in late January for an AFC Championship-type game? Under those conditions, it could be very difficult.

He said the Bills understand where they are in their division, but their eyes are on a bigger prize: the top seed, the first-round bye, and every game coming through Buffalo.

And then he dropped a stat that should get every Bills fan’s heart racing. The last time Buffalo started seven and one, the Bills went to the Super Bowl. And that season, they had the number one seed, and the road went through Buffalo.

Bills Mafia, that was 1993. And this is the first time since 1993 that Buffalo has started seven and one.

The panel said the only team that should really worry Buffalo in the current playoff picture is Jacksonville, because most of the other contenders are cold-weather teams that wouldn’t be bothered by traveling to Buffalo. So the pressure is on the Jaguars to get the top spot themselves. And that’s why Brandon Beane was asked about Jacksonville making multiple moves at the deadline. But Beane said he doesn’t feel pressure from other teams, only from himself, to help Joe Brady and Josh Allen be the best they can be.

But the panel said Buffalo is sitting in the catbird seat. The number one seed means one less game on players’ bodies. It means every opponent has to deal with that crowd. And, as one analyst noted, while it’s a brand-new Highmark Stadium now, you still want to play at home, with one less game, needing just two wins to reach the Super Bowl.

Remember what Josh Allen said after breaking Jim Kelly’s record, and what former safety Justin Simmons said a few weeks ago: if Buffalo can make everyone come through Buffalo, Josh Allen might just be playing past the AFC Championship Game.

Then the panel got into a fun conversation about the cold. One analyst, a Louisiana guy, joked that anything below 46 degrees is cold. And another told a story about playing in conditions so cold that when a coach came over to give adjustments on the sideline, the tears coming out of his eyes froze on his face. He said that was Pittsburgh cold, and that Buffalo cold is different.

He made it clear that a warm-weather team could still go to Buffalo and win. But he said it would be very difficult, because those teams aren’t used to those conditions. And he said it’s especially hard for teams not named the Buffalo Bills, because Buffalo can run the football.

And Bills Mafia, can Buffalo run the football? Just ask the Vikings. James Cook carried 31 times. He has a touchdown in seven straight games. He and Josh Allen both just passed 5,000 career rushing yards, the first teammates in NFL history to do it. That’s exactly the kind of offense that wins in January weather.

So let’s put it all together.

Great news number one: Joe Brady is off to the best offensive start for a first-time head coach since Sean McVay in 2017. The Bills are averaging 32 points per game, and Brady has found a counterpunch for every defense he’s faced.

Great news number two: Josh Allen is second in the MVP odds, behind only Joe Burrow, and people around the league say the one thing they can’t account for in the playoffs is Josh Allen.

Great news number three: the Bills are the number one seed in the AFC. If they hold on, every playoff game goes through Buffalo, in January, and the last time Buffalo started seven and one, it went to the Super Bowl.

The concern: the experts want to see the defense prove itself against a Jets team that can push the ball downfield. And Buffalo has to block David Bailey.

My prediction for Sunday: Joe Brady finds the counterpunch again, the offensive line keeps David Bailey in check, Amani Hooker makes his presence felt, and the Bills move to eight and one.
