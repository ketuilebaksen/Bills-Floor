BUFFALO BILLS JUST GOT GOOD NEWS! BILLS DESTROY NEW YORK JETS 31-12!

Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills went into MetLife Stadium and handled business. Final score: Bills 31, Jets 12. Buffalo is now eight and one. And this win came with a lot of good news: an explosive second half, a 74-yard touchdown run from James Cook, DJ Moore back making plays, and a defense that didn’t allow a single touchdown.

Twelve points. That’s four field goals and zero trips into the end zone for the Jets.

And yet, after the game, Josh Allen said something that might surprise you. He said the offense didn’t play that well. He said this team still isn’t playing its best football. And that’s exactly why the rest of the AFC should be worried.

So today, we’re breaking down everything from this win: what clicked in the second half, why James Cook is on another level, how this defense has completely transformed, what Josh Allen said after the game, and what’s coming next as Buffalo chases the number one seed.

First, let’s set the scene.

Before this game, the national experts were confident. Richard Sherman said Buffalo would win easily. He pointed out that the Jets were frustrated, with Geno Smith on the sideline the week before, and that there were big questions about the team’s future and head coach Aaron Glenn. Another analyst noted that if the season ended today, the Jets would own the fourth, sixth, and 13th picks in the draft.

But Josh Allen didn’t overlook this team. During the week, he warned that the Jets’ defenders fly around, aren’t afraid to play press man coverage, and do a great job of disguising their zone coverages. He said that with a defensive head coach, you know the unit will be extremely well coached. And he gave credit to former Bill Harrison Phillips, who he said is playing really good football.

Plus, this was a short week. Buffalo was coming off a brutal Monday Night Football game in Minnesota. Josh said players spent the week in the training room, the hot tub, the cold tub, and the sauna, doing everything they could to get their bodies right.

So this wasn’t automatic. And the first half proved it.

Josh admitted after the game that the first half didn’t go the way they wanted. He said the team turned the ball over, and that they shouldn’t be doing that. There were pre-snap penalties. The offense wasn’t executing the way it wanted to.

But Josh also said he was proud of how the team played. And in the second half, everything changed.

Let’s talk about that second half, because it was explosive.

When a reporter asked if something clicked for the offense after halftime, Josh said yes, they were really explosive. And it started right away.

On the very first play of the second half, Josh hit Dalton Kincaid for a huge gain. Josh said he wasn’t sure exactly how long it was, maybe 50 or 54 yards. And a couple of plays later, Buffalo was in the end zone.

Then there was a big pass to DJ Moore. And then, James Cook broke off a 74-yard touchdown run.

Josh said it simply: you love those.

Bills Mafia, that’s what makes this offense so dangerous. It can grind you down for a half, and then, in a matter of minutes, it can blow the game wide open. One explosive play after another.

And it says a lot about the supporting cast. Dalton Kincaid, who spent a week in a red non-contact jersey with a chest injury before the Ravens game, ripped off a 50-plus-yard catch. DJ Moore, who missed two games with a re-aggravated AC joint sprain, was back in the middle of everything.

Josh said it was good to see Moore involved again. He said that when DJ is on the field, he’s a threat to score from anywhere, and that he did a great job in this game.

And remember what one national analyst said last week: when you have a true number one receiver like DJ Moore, everyone moves down a notch. The number one becomes the number two, and the number two becomes the number three. That’s what makes this passing game so hard to cover.

Now, the star of the show: James Cook.

Eighteen carries. One hundred fifty-seven yards. A 74-yard touchdown run.

That’s nearly nine yards per carry, Bills Mafia.

And here’s what makes it even more impressive. The week before, against Minnesota, Cook carried the ball 31 times for 107 hard-fought yards, the most carries by any player in any NFL game this season. Then, on a short week, he came out and did this.

A reporter pointed out the contrast: last week was all about tough yards, and this week Cook broke one for 74. And Josh made a brilliant point about how the two are connected.

He said the long run is a product of the tough runs. It’s the result of getting those four- and five-yard carries early in the game and in the third quarter. Cook keeps chipping away, and eventually, a crease opens up that’s big enough for him to get through.

And then Josh said something every Bills fan will love. He said Cook put his head down and was really moving. He didn’t know Cook’s speed on that play, but he said it was the fastest he thinks he’s ever seen him run. He said Cook found a second gear, and it was awesome to see.

A reporter noted that Cook has had about 120 carries over the last five games, and asked how he’s handling that workload.

Josh said there’s no doubt: Cook wants it. He said Cook is doing a great job of getting in the training room and putting in extra work. But Josh also said the team has to make sure it’s protecting him.

And Josh repeated his message from the whole season: the more touches they can get James Cook, the better this offense is.

When a reporter asked about the emphasis on the run game over the last two weeks, Josh’s answer was perfect. He said: we’ve got James Cook. Go run. Heck yeah.

Bills Mafia, think about what James Cook has done over the last few weeks. He passed 5,000 career rushing yards on the same night Josh Allen did, making them the first teammates in NFL history to both reach that mark. He carried the ball 31 times in Minnesota. And now he’s run for 157 yards and a 74-yard touchdown against the Jets. With that touchdown, he’s scored in eight straight games.

And remember what Joe Brady said about him after the Raiders game earlier this season? When someone asked what he was going to do with Cook after a late fumble, Brady said: I’m going to hand him the football. He said that when Cook holds the ball, he holds the whole organization.

Brady was right.

Josh also gave credit to the coaching staff for the run game. When asked about the side-to-side running plays the Bills used early, he said it came from film work by the coaches, and from getting the run game going with offensive line coach Pat. And he said the protection plan was awesome too, crediting assistant offensive line coach Austin Gunn.

That’s important, because Buffalo’s offensive line has been dealing with injuries. Heading into this game, right tackle Spencer Brown was listed as doubtful with an ankle injury after getting rolled up the week before. Just a week earlier, Dion Dawkins and Alec Anderson had both been inactive in Minnesota. And yet, this line keeps finding a way. Against the Jets, whose pass rush ranks among the best in the league, the protection held up, and the run game exploded.

Now, the defense. And Bills Mafia, this is the real good news.

The Jets scored 12 points. Four field goals. Zero touchdowns.

When a reporter asked Josh how important it was that the defense kept the Jets to field goals instead of touchdowns, he called it fantastic stuff. He said the defense is playing really good right now. He said they got their hands on some balls, and recovered a fumble late in the game. And he said forcing turnovers gives the offense more advantages and more opportunities.

And look at this three-game stretch:

Against Baltimore: 10 points allowed.

Against Minnesota: six points allowed.

Against the Jets: 12 points allowed, and no touchdowns.

That’s 28 points allowed in three games, Bills Mafia. Under 10 points per game.

Remember where this defense was before the bye. Going into Week 8, the Bills were allowing 28.5 points per game, the third-most in the NFL. They were giving up 386.8 total yards and 285.8 passing yards per game, both second-worst in the league. Buffalo’s defensive DVOA was around 28th out of 32 teams.

And now? Buffalo’s defense has climbed to around 18th in DVOA, and its EPA per play since the bye has been negative, meaning it’s actually taking points away from opponents.

Jim Leonhard deserves a ton of credit. During the bye week, he said there were changes he couldn’t install until after the break. He moved up to the coaching box. And Joe Brady said the communication between upstairs and downstairs was elite.

Against Baltimore, Leonhard blitzed on 64.5 percent of dropbacks and held the Ravens to 68 passing yards. Against Minnesota, he blitzed just 11.5 percent of the time and still generated more pressure than the Vikings did. Different plans. Same result.

And the difference-makers are finally here.

Greg Rousseau is tied for second in the NFL with eight sacks, and he leads the entire league in pressures with 51. Ed Oliver has been playing at a different level since returning healthy. Deone Walker has been a force multiplier, drawing double teams and freeing up everyone around him. The Bills rank in the top five in stuff rate, and they’re third in the league in yards before contact allowed in the run game.

And in the back end, there’s Amani Hooker, the safety Brandon Beane traded a second-round pick to acquire. He’s tied for the league lead in interceptions with five, including three against Joe Burrow in one game. Hooker called joining Buffalo a dream come true. Beane compared him to Micah Hyde. And paired with Cole Bishop, he could give Buffalo a top safety tandem for years.

Here’s a stat to watch: if Hooker finishes first in interceptions and Rousseau finishes first in sacks, it would be the first time in franchise history that two Bills players led the NFL in those categories in the same season.

But Josh made sure to keep everyone grounded.

He said the offense can’t turn the ball over. And when asked about the pre-snap penalties, he admitted there were a few too many, said they have to clean them up, and said it would be a point of emphasis this week.

That lines up with what Joe Brady has said all season. Brady said he has no time for pre-snap penalties, because those are about controlling what you can control.

And then a reporter asked where this game ranks among Buffalo’s most complete performances this season. Josh’s answer was honest. He said that offensively, he doesn’t think they played that well. He acknowledged that you can look at the stat sheet and see 31 points, but said the first half didn’t feel like they executed the way they wanted to.

When a reporter said this team keeps finding different ways to win each week, Josh agreed, but said Buffalo can only control how it plays. And then he said: we’re not playing our best football right now. He said there’s still so much to learn and grow from, and that as the season goes on, they believe they’ll keep getting better and start putting it all together.

Bills Mafia, read that again. Eight and one. A 19-point road win. A defense that didn’t allow a touchdown. And the quarterback says the team hasn’t played its best football yet.

That’s the same message Joe Brady has preached since the bye. He said this team’s best football has to be ahead of it, and that if you’re playing your best football in September, there’s a problem. Brandon Beane said the goal is to be playing your best football in January.

And finally, the fans.

Josh was asked how it felt to have so many Bills fans travel to New York and basically turn it into a home game. He said it was cool. He said the offense didn’t have to use the silent count very much, only on the occasional loud third down. And he said that when he looked up into the stands, he saw a lot of red, white, and blue.

And then he said: you’ve got the best fans in the world.

That’s you, Bills Mafia.

So let’s put it all together.

The Bills beat the Jets 31 to 12 and improved to eight and one.

The second half was explosive: a 50-plus-yard catch by Dalton Kincaid on the first play, a quick touchdown, a big play to DJ Moore, and a 74-yard touchdown run by James Cook.

Cook finished with 18 carries for 157 yards, and Josh said it was the fastest he’s ever seen him run.

The defense held the Jets without a touchdown, forced turnovers, and has now allowed just 28 points over its last three games.

And Josh Allen said the turnovers and pre-snap penalties have to be cleaned up, and that this team still hasn’t played its best football.

Now Buffalo heads into a six-game stretch that could decide the AFC’s number one seed. That includes the Dolphins, and then the Kansas City Chiefs on Thanksgiving night in Buffalo. Even Nick Wright, one of the biggest Chiefs fans in sports media, said he expects the Chiefs to get their teeth kicked in on Thanksgiving night. And the Patriots game in early December, the next big game where Buffalo will really want Spencer Brown back healthy, looms after that.

The Bills have never had the number one seed in the Josh Allen era. They’ve never had a first-round bye. They’ve never hosted the AFC Championship Game with Josh at quarterback. And the last time Buffalo started eight and one, well, it’s been a long time. Back in 1993, the last time the Bills started seven and one, they had the top seed and went to the Super Bowl.

This team is on that path.
