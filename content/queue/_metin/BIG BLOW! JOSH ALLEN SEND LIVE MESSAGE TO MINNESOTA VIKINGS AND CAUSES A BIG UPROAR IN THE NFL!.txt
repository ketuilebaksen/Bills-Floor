JOSH ALLEN SENDS A MESSAGE TO THE VIKINGS BEFORE MONDAY NIGHT SHOWDOWN!

Bills Mafia, it’s game day. Tonight, the Buffalo Bills walk into U.S. Bank Stadium for Monday Night Football against the Minnesota Vikings, the number one offense in the NFL against the number one defense. And before this showdown, Josh Allen sent a clear message to Minnesota. He showed the Vikings real respect, but there was also a challenge in his words. And the more you listen, the more you realize Buffalo is walking into this game with exactly the right mindset.

Today we’re breaking down everything Josh said, how it connects to the latest injury news, and what it all means for tonight.

Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the respect, because Josh didn’t hold back in praising this Vikings defense.

The biggest topic all week has been Minnesota’s blitz. Brian Flores’s defense sends pressure at an incredible rate. And when Josh was asked if he’s ever seen anything like it, he made a point that changes how you think about this matchup.

He said it isn’t even about how often they blitz. It’s about how productive they are when they do.

Then he described what makes this defense so dangerous. They bring pressure from both sides. They’ll blitz up the middle. And they’ll drop players into coverage who you were sure were coming after you. He called it a very in-tune defense, full of very smart players who play extremely well together.

And then he said something you rarely hear from a quarterback about an opponent. He said it’s hard to game plan for this team because there aren’t any weaknesses.

No weaknesses, Bills Mafia. That’s Josh Allen talking.

And then he gave the line that sums up this whole week: you turn your eyes, and you’re wrong.

Think about what that means. Against most defenses, a quarterback can take a peek at one side of the field, come back to the other, and still have time. Against Minnesota, looking the wrong way for a split second is already a mistake. The pressure is coming before you’ve finished your read.

Josh said he has to stay on top of it. He has to make sure Buffalo’s protection rules are exactly where they need to be. The Bills need a plan going in, and if that plan isn’t working, they have to adjust during the game.

And then he showed how much he respects what Flores has built. He said that, from a football perspective, it’s fun to watch this defense. But from an opposing quarterback’s perspective, it’s hard to watch. He said Buffalo is still trying to figure it out, and he called it a really good system.

Now let’s talk about why this defense is so hard to prepare for.

Josh was asked if this week’s preparation is even tougher than usual. He said yes, he thinks it is. He said Minnesota shows so many different looks, and they use a few different personnel packages where the same look can mean completely different things.

That’s the trick, Bills Mafia. Most defenses give you clues. If certain players are on the field, you can predict what’s coming. But with Minnesota, the same alignment before the snap can turn into two totally different plays after it. So Josh said Buffalo has to know exactly who’s on the field and where each player is lined up. And he said it’s up to the players and the coaching staff to decipher it, have the best possible plan, and adjust.

Joe Brady said the same thing this week. He explained that Minnesota can be in the same personnel grouping on third and 25 as on the first play of the game, run the same look, and still have success. He said it’s part of a growing trend in the NFL, defenses trying to one-up offenses with versatility, but that it takes a special coach and a special group of players to trust a scheme like that. And he called Flores one of the best in the game, someone who keeps reinventing his defense.

Josh was even asked whether Flores’s defense looks anything like the one he faced when Flores was coaching in Miami. And Josh said not really. He said Flores has done a great job of adapting. Even compared to last year, the defense looks a lot different. And he said it’s going to take all hands on deck.

So here’s the respect part of Josh’s message to the Vikings: you are the best defense in football, and we know it.

But now, here’s the challenge.

Josh was asked about the matchup itself. The number one offense against the number one defense. Does that intrigue him?

He said yes and no. He said Minnesota is number one for a reason, and that they’ve given a lot of good offenses problems. He said it’s going to be a hard week, and the team understands that. But then he said they’re excited for the opportunity.

He pointed out that, around the midway point of the season, teams are pretty much who they are. And he called this an awesome test for Buffalo, a chance to fight through adversity and figure things out as they go.

And then the competitor in him came out.

Josh said that as a competitor, your reaction is: heck yeah, I want to play the best. He said he wants to go out there and prove why Buffalo is the number one offense. He acknowledged that’s statistically speaking, but said it’s still a chip on their shoulder. They want to be the best offense they can be, and they have a great opportunity against a fantastic defense.

Bills Mafia, that’s the message. We respect you. And we want to beat you.

Before we go any further, smash that like button if you’re fired up for tonight. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

So how does Buffalo actually beat this defense?

Josh revealed the plan. He said there have been a lot of protection meetings and a lot of protection walkthroughs. He said the Monday night game helps because it gives the team an extra day to game plan. And he said he’s sure they’ll have a few walkthroughs before the game in a conference room at the team hotel.

But he was realistic. Buffalo could game plan all week, and Minnesota could come out and do something completely different. So he said the key will be understanding the team’s base rules and attacking the defense that way.

And then, when Josh was asked about the secret to his success throughout his career, he gave what might be the single most important key to tonight’s game.

He started by giving credit to everyone else. He said it takes the other 10 players on the field understanding and being in tune. He said Buffalo has had some great offensive line coaches who develop a protection plan each and every week. And he said that this year, with the way the coaches and players are talking and communicating, it helps so much to understand where the problems are and to have answers ready when those problems show up.

And then he said this: it helps when you can make one person miss.

He explained that if a defense is bringing pressure, and you can make one rusher miss, there’s one less defender, maybe two less, in the back end. And now your receivers are running into some pretty healthy zone areas, with not many people around.

Bills Mafia, that’s the blueprint for tonight. Minnesota is going to blitz. A lot. But every blitz means fewer defenders in coverage. If Josh can make one man miss, there’s open grass behind it. And with DJ Moore stretching the field, Keon Coleman winning one-on-ones, Khalil Shakir finding soft spots, and the running backs leaking out of the backfield, those healthy zone areas could turn into explosive plays.

But the protection has to hold up first. And that brings us to the latest injury news.

Joe Brady’s final injury report listed Joshua Palmer as out for tonight’s game. And five players were listed as questionable: Dion Dawkins, Alec Anderson, Greg Gaines, Jordan Hancock, and Tyrell Shavers.

The biggest name there is Dion Dawkins, the man who protects Josh Allen’s blind side. Dawkins didn’t practice all week. But Brady said he had a conversation with Dion early in the week, and that some players have played so much football that he trusts them to know their assignments and do their jobs without practice reps. He said he felt the same way about Spencer Brown the week before.

And if Dawkins can’t go, Brady has confidence in Tylan Grable, the backup swing tackle who has already stepped up twice this season. Brady said that when backups like Grable come in, he doesn’t feel like he has to call a different football game. But he admitted that moving from right tackle to left tackle is a real challenge, with differences in your dominant hand, how you punch, and how you set.

So keep a close eye on the left side of that offensive line tonight, Bills Mafia. Against this blitz, that could decide the game.

And Brady said something else about the running backs that ties right into Josh’s blueprint. He said this week will test their pass protection more than pretty much any other opponent. He said this team doesn’t care who makes the play, and that blocking for each other in the passing game is just as important to the running backs as running the football. If Cook, Johnson, and Davis pick up those blitzers, Josh gets the extra second he needs to make someone miss.

Now, let’s talk about the stadium, and one of the greatest moments of Josh Allen’s career.

Josh is returning to U.S. Bank Stadium. When he was asked what it means to go back, he said honestly, not much. He joked that they like playing indoors, with no wind and no weather to deal with, which is always a plus.

But he made it clear that the crowd will be a factor. He said Minnesota’s fans will be loud, especially with how well the team is playing and how outstanding the defense has been. He said Buffalo is expecting an extremely loud crowd, and that the team would be practicing with speakers blasting to simulate it. And he gave a shoutout to the Vikings’ Skol chant, calling it an awesome tradition, and said it will be a fun experience.

And then came the question everybody was waiting for: the hurdle.

September 2018. A rookie Josh Allen, in only his third NFL game and his second start, leaps right over Vikings linebacker Anthony Barr. The Bills, 16 and a half point underdogs, win 27 to 6. T-shirts were printed. The clip went everywhere. Even Jim Kelly said he could never have hurdled like that.

Josh was asked whether the reaction surprised him. He said that as a football fan, you love seeing plays like that, but he admitted he had never really thought about it much.

But when a reporter asked whether that play was when he started to feel more familiar with what he could do, Josh opened up.

He said the play honestly surprised him too. He said he was a good athlete in college, but in high school he was not. As he got older and started lifting more weights, he became more athletic. And he said it surprised him what he was able to do, especially early in his career, with the running.

And then he joked that he still runs a little bit now.

A little bit, Bills Mafia. Josh Allen is the all-time leader in rushing touchdowns by a quarterback. And tonight, he needs just nine rushing yards to become only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000 career rushing yards, joining Lamar Jackson, Michael Vick, Cam Newton, and Russell Wilson. And he could do it in the exact building where that hurdle happened.

But the most powerful part was what Josh said next. He said that hurdle was the moment he thought: you know what, I do belong here.

Think about that. The kid from Firebaugh, California, with zero college offers. The family that sent around a thousand emails and almost never heard back. The junior college player. The Wyoming quarterback with one door open in the entire country. And in Minnesota, in his third game, he finally knew he belonged.

Now he’s going back as the greatest quarterback in Buffalo Bills history, having just passed Jim Kelly for the most total touchdown passes in franchise history.

And Joe Brady had a perfect line about the hurdle this week too. He said he hopes there are no hurdles tonight. No more “hurdle avenue.” But he said that play showed the Josh Allen experience: a player who will lay it all on the line for his teammates every week, and who will do whatever it takes to win.

A few more moments from the press conference that are worth mentioning.

Josh was asked about the face mask incident from the Ravens game, when Roquan Smith grabbed his face mask and twisted his head on a goal line sneak. The NFL has since fined Smith 17,911 dollars for that play, and another 17,911 dollars for hitting DJ Moore while he was defenseless, for a total of 35,822 dollars. And it was Smith’s second face mask-related fine of the season.

But Josh kept it calm. He said he doesn’t think that kind of thing happens very often, but that it’s football, a physical game, and stuff like that is going to happen.

That’s the Josh Allen way. No drama. Just football.

He also talked about his alma mater, Wyoming. He said their season didn’t start the way they wanted, but they’ve picked up steam, they’re sitting at five and three, and they’re doing well in their conference. He said he loves watching them every week and cheers them on as hard as he can.

He also talked about “The Buffalo Miracle,” the first real project from his production company. He said the story is fantastic, the project turned out great, and he’s excited for the premiere on the 17th.

And there was a fun moment at the end. A reporter asked about Spencer Brown’s mask, apparently part of a team costume contest around Halloween, and whether it was the best part. Josh said it was not, and joked that Brown had probably asked the reporter to bring it up. Josh revealed that Brown came in third place, and that Ray Davis took the top spot.

That’s the kind of locker room chemistry that makes this team special, Bills Mafia. Even in the most intense week of the season, they’re still having fun together.

And that ties into what Joe Brady said about the mindset for tonight. He said the team’s standards should never rise and fall based on the opponent. He doesn’t want his players feeling like they have to be heroes or make the game bigger than it is. He said that’s when teams start playing outside the system. Instead, he described competitive stamina as simply playing the next play.

So let’s put it all together.

Josh Allen’s message to the Vikings was respect and challenge at the same time. He said Minnesota’s defense has no weaknesses, that the blitz is extremely productive, and that if you turn your eyes, you’re wrong. But he also said that as a competitor, he wants to play the best and prove why Buffalo is the number one offense.

The plan: trust the protection rules, adjust in the game, and make one blitzer miss, because that opens up healthy zones behind the pressure.

The injury report: Joshua Palmer is out. Dion Dawkins is questionable but trusted to play without practice. Tylan Grable is ready if needed.

The stadium: an extremely loud indoor crowd, the Skol chant, and the place where a rookie Josh Allen hurdled Anthony Barr and realized he belonged in the NFL.

And the milestone: nine rushing yards to become only the fifth quarterback in NFL history with 5,000.

Carson Wentz will start at quarterback for Minnesota, so the Bills defense, coming off its best game of the season, will face a veteran who’s played games this year but isn’t the starter the Vikings opened with. And Justin Jefferson, whom Brady coached at LSU and called the best receiver in football, will test this secondary on every snap.

My prediction, Bills Mafia: this will be a chess match. Brian Flores is going to throw everything at Josh Allen. But Josh is going to make a few blitzers miss, find those open zones, get his nine yards early, and lead Buffalo to a big prime-time win. Bills move to seven and one.
