First, the big picture. With Week 7 almost complete, here’s how the AFC playoff picture looks right now, if the season ended today.

At the top, still holding the number one seed despite their first loss of the season, the Kansas City Chiefs. Right behind them, the other three division leaders: the Buffalo Bills, the Cleveland Browns, and the Jacksonville Jaguars.

So Buffalo, at five and one coming out of the bye, is sitting firmly in the AFC’s top tier. Division leader, second seed, right on the heels of Kansas City.

And Bills Mafia, the fact that the Chiefs just took their first loss is a huge deal for us. Because the race for that number one seed, and the home-field advantage that comes with it, just opened up.

Let’s talk about how Kansas City lost, because it matters. The Chiefs were beaten on Sunday Night Football by the Seattle Seahawks. Because home playoff games are going to be crucial for any Super Bowl run.

If Buffalo can find a way to take over the top of the AFC and make everyone come to Buffalo, and go through Buffalo to get to the Super Bowl, then Josh Allen might find himself playing past the AFC Championship Game.

That’s a massive game, and Bills Mafia, it’s one we should all be watching. Because if Denver beats Kansas City, the Chiefs drop again, and the race for the number one seed gets even more wide open.

As Simmons said, six or seven weeks in, this race is looking intense.

The Baltimore Ravens aren’t in the playoff picture.

Analyst Bryant McFadden said the most glaring thing on the AFC board for him was the absence of Baltimore. The Ravens just suffered a disappointing loss to the Cincinnati Bengals. And McFadden’s big question was: when will we see Lamar Jackson back in uniform? And not just in uniform, but able to do the things we’ve seen him do throughout his career. He called that a major question mark.

The host added more context. Baltimore missed the playoffs a year ago. Lamar missed games due to injury. And this year, they’re on the outside looking in again. It’s still early, but the urgency in Baltimore has to be peaking.

Bills Mafia, think about what that means for Week 8.

Buffalo comes out of the bye at five and one, rested, healthy, with two weeks of coaching adjustments behind them. Joe Brady said they treated the bye like the team was starting over at zero and zero. Jim Leonhard said there were defensive changes they couldn’t install until after the bye. And now they’re facing a Ravens team that just lost to Cincinnati, sits outside the playoff picture, and doesn’t know for sure what it’s getting from its star quarterback.

That’s a desperate team. And desperate teams are dangerous. Baltimore is going to come into this game playing like its season depends on it, because in a lot of ways, it does. But this is also exactly the kind of game a contender has to win. If Buffalo wants that number one seed, you take care of business against a team that’s struggling.

McFadden also brought up the New England Patriots, our AFC East rivals. He said we had finally started to see Drake Maye look like the Drake Maye of 2025. But then, on Thursday night, some of the bad habits showed up again, along with health problems at wide receiver. McFadden called it a big disappointment.

That matters for Buffalo too, because New England is the team that gave this offense its biggest problems this season. Dan Orlovsky recently said the Patriots were the one defense that kept Buffalo from getting to its core principles for four full quarters. So any sign of the Patriots struggling is good news for Buffalo’s grip on the AFC East.

Now, the trade rumors.

The NFL trade deadline is November 10th, and Buffalo’s name is coming up in a lot of conversations. And one pitch in particular involves a running back.

The idea is that Buffalo could trade for New York Giants running back Cam Skattebo.

Here’s the background. Skattebo has had a rough year with the Giants. Head coach John Harbaugh has clearly grown frustrated with him, and that frustration reached a peak in Week 6, when Skattebo was benched after fumbles became an issue that cost the team. ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported that the Giants would like to make changes at running back, that the new coaching staff has soured on Skattebo, and that the team recently released Najee Harris.

So the pitch is that Buffalo could swing a late-round pick, maybe a sixth or seventh, for a physical, tough runner who might just need a fresh start.

Now, let’s be realistic about this, Bills Mafia. This is a trade pitch, an idea from an outlet, not a deal that’s been reported or announced. And honestly, I’m not sure Buffalo needs it.

Think about what Dan Orlovsky said about this backfield just days ago. He called Buffalo’s running backs a three-headed monster in the passing game. James Cook, Ty Johnson, and Ray Davis have been miles ahead of the rest of the league in EPA per target. Each of them brings something different: Cook in designed pass concepts, Johnson on broken plays and perimeter screens, Davis in the screen game. And the screen game, Orlovsky said, has become a staple of this offense.

That’s not a position of need. That’s a position of strength.

And there’s another angle. Ball security. Joe Brady made it clear after the Raiders game how important holding onto the football is. He said when James Cook holds the ball, he holds the whole organization. So bringing in a back whose problems in New York were tied to fumbles? That’s a tough fit for a coach who preaches exactly the opposite.

There’s also been talk in the other direction. Some analysts have suggested Buffalo could actually trade one of its backs. Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox named Ty Johnson as a possible trade candidate, since he’s set to be a free agent in 2027, which could open up a bigger role for Ray Davis. Others have floated Ray Davis himself, who was Buffalo’s only first-team All-Pro in 2025 as a kick returner, but whose role has shrunk this season.

So when you put it all together, Bills Mafia, I think the bigger question at the deadline isn’t whether Buffalo adds a running back. It’s whether Buffalo moves one, and what it uses its resources on instead.

And based on everything we’ve heard from this coaching staff, the answer seems obvious. The secondary.

Jim Leonhard said himself that the defense isn’t doing its most important job right now, keeping points off the board. He said the coverage has to communicate better. Buffalo gave up more than 300 passing yards to Kirk Cousins in Las Vegas. C.J. Gardner-Johnson was suspended for a game. The team just brought back Tre’Davious White on the practice squad. And outlets covering the team have been profiling defensive backs as potential deadline targets, names like Derwin James, Jessie Bates, and Amani Hooker.

If I’m Brandon Beane, that’s where I’m looking. A difference-making defensive back could be the move that turns this team from a contender into the favorite.

Now let’s take a quick look at the NFC, because if Buffalo makes the Super Bowl, one of these teams will be waiting.

The NFC North is a war right now. The Minnesota Vikings are six and oh, the only unbeaten team left in the NFL. And the Bears, Lions, and Packers are all lined up in the wild card picture. One analyst said he can’t remember the last time he saw all four teams from one division sitting at the top of the leaderboard like this.

Minnesota’s defense has been spectacular. But here’s an interesting stat: the host pointed out that Minnesota has scored the fewest touchdowns over an 11-game win streak in the history of the league. So the defense is carrying that team.

Out west, San Francisco is leading, and in the NFC East, the New York Giants are on top. The NFC South has once again been a soap opera, with Carolina leading the way at five hundred and Tampa sitting one game behind after beating the Panthers.

Two big names are on the outside looking in. The Seattle Seahawks, the defending champions, who just beat Kansas City, are out of the picture right now because of a tiebreaker with the Packers. And the Rams, often called a super team, are also on the outside.

And remember, Buffalo already beat those Rams on Monday night out west. That win is looking better and better.

Then came the fun part: who’s the best team in the NFL?

One analyst picked San Francisco. He said they lost this week, but they’re well coached, they play the game the right way, and the big key for them is getting healthier. He noted that the Niners have been two different offenses with and without Mike Evans.

The other analyst went with Minnesota. He said defense wins championships, and that even though the offense doesn’t always look pretty, it doesn’t have to with a defense like that. He said they have a tough schedule coming up and we’ll learn more about them, but as it stands, he trusts that defense. The host added that defense wins championships if you have a run game to go with it, pointing to Seattle last season.

And here’s something interesting for Bills Mafia: the teams with the shortest odds to win the Super Bowl were the Rams and the Ravens.

Think about that. The two teams with the shortest odds in that segment? Buffalo just beat one of them, and is about to play the other.

So let’s bring this all together. What does this AFC picture mean for the Buffalo Bills?

Number one: the number one seed is in play. The Chiefs took their first loss. They’re heading to Denver for a game that could knock them out of first place in their own division. And Buffalo, at five and one, is right there. If the Bills take care of business, the road to the Super Bowl could finally run through Buffalo.

Number two: Week 8 is a statement game. The Ravens are outside the playoff picture, coming off a loss to Cincinnati, with questions about Lamar Jackson. They’re desperate. Buffalo is coming off a bye with fresh legs and a revamped plan. This is a game a championship team wins.

Number three: the AFC is wide open, but Buffalo is in the best position it’s been in for years. The Chiefs are beatable. The Ravens are struggling. The Patriots are inconsistent. The Browns and Jaguars are leading their divisions, but neither has Josh Allen.

Number four: the trade deadline is coming, and Buffalo has to be smart. A running back like Cam Skattebo might be an interesting idea, but this backfield is already one of the most productive in football. The bigger need is in the secondary, and that’s where a deadline move could make the biggest difference.

And number five: home-field advantage could change everything. Justin Simmons said it best. If Buffalo can make everyone come through Buffalo, Josh Allen might just be playing past the AFC Championship Game.
