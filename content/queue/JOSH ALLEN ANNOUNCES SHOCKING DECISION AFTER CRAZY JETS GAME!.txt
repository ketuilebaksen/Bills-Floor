JOSH ALLEN’S BRUTALLY HONEST REACTION AFTER BILLS PUT 31 ON THE JETS!

Bills Mafia, the Buffalo Bills went into MetLife Stadium and came out with another win. Thirty-one points. An explosive second half. A 74-yard touchdown run from James Cook. DJ Moore making plays. And a defense that kept the Jets out of the end zone and forced turnovers.

Buffalo is now eight and one.

But when Josh Allen stepped up to the podium after the game, he didn’t sound like a quarterback celebrating. He sounded like a quarterback who knows this team has another level. He said the offense didn’t play that well, even with 31 points. He said the team isn’t playing its best football yet. And he made it clear that turnovers and pre-snap penalties have to go.

So today, we’re breaking down everything Josh said, what clicked in that second half, why James Cook is becoming unstoppable, and what this all means as Buffalo chases the number one seed.

Let’s get into it.

Josh opened his press conference with honesty. He said the team executed, but turned the ball over, and that they shouldn’t be doing that. But he said he was proud of how the team played.

That’s Josh Allen in one sentence, Bills Mafia. Proud of the win. Honest about the mistakes.

And before the game, the experts all said this would be easy. Richard Sherman said Buffalo would win easily. The Jets came in frustrated, with Geno Smith on the sideline the week before and questions about head coach Aaron Glenn’s future. But Josh had warned during the week that this Jets defense plays press man coverage, disguises its zones well, and is extremely well coached. And he was right. This wasn’t a perfect game. But it was a win, and in the second half, it was a statement.

Let’s start with the good news on offense: DJ Moore.

A reporter said it was good to see Moore get back involved. And Josh agreed, absolutely. He said that when Moore is on the field, he’s a threat to score from anywhere. And Josh said he thought DJ did a great job tonight.

Think about Moore’s journey over the last few weeks. A re-aggravated AC joint sprain in his shoulder. He missed the Rams game and the Raiders game. He practiced all week in a red non-contact jersey before the Ravens game. Then he came back and caught Josh Allen’s record-breaking 259th touchdown pass, passing Jim Kelly. And now, against the Jets, he was a big part of an explosive offense.

As one national analyst said this week, when you have a true number one receiver like Moore, everybody else moves down a notch. The number one becomes the number two, and the number two becomes the number three. That’s what makes this passing game so tough to defend.

Then a reporter asked about the second half. Was there something that clicked for the offense after halftime?

Josh said yes, they were really explosive. He pointed to the very first play of the second half, a big catch by Dalton Kincaid. Josh said he wasn’t sure exactly how long it was, maybe 50 or 54 yards. And then Buffalo scored a couple of plays later.

He listed the explosive plays: the run game, the pass to DJ Moore, and then James Cook taking one 74 yards to the house.

And Josh said: you love those.

That’s what this offense can do, Bills Mafia. One play, 50-plus yards to Kincaid. A couple of plays later, touchdown. Then another big pass to Moore. Then a 74-yard touchdown run. When this offense gets going, it can score from anywhere on the field.

Before we get to James Cook, smash that like button if you loved that second half. Let’s go, Bills Mafia.

Now, James Cook.

A reporter pointed out that the week before, against Minnesota, everyone was talking about Cook grinding out tough yards, carrying the ball 31 times, the most by any player in any game this season. And this week, Cook broke one for 74 yards.

And Josh made a brilliant point about how those two things are connected. He said the long run is a product of the tough runs. It’s the product of getting those four- and five-yard carries early in the game and in the third quarter. Cook keeps chipping away, and finally, a crease opens up big enough for him to get through.

And then Josh said something that every Bills fan will love. He said Cook put his head down and was really moving. He said he didn’t know Cook’s speed on the play, but it was the fastest he thinks he’s ever seen Cook run. He said Cook found a second gear, and it was awesome to see.

Bills Mafia, think about that. The fastest Josh Allen has ever seen James Cook run. After 31 carries the week before.

And that’s exactly what Josh said in his press conference before the game. He said Cook tends to get better as the game goes on, because he wears defenses down, and because he worked so hard in the offseason on his stamina, and his competitive stamina. Against the Jets, we saw the payoff.

A reporter then pointed out that Cook has had something like 120 carries over the last five games. How is he responding to that workload? What’s his temperament like on the sideline?

Josh said there’s no doubt about it: Cook wants it. He said Cook is doing a great job of making sure he’s in the training room getting extra work on his body. But Josh also said the team has to make sure it’s protecting him too.

And then Josh gave Cook’s stat line from the game: 18 carries for 157 yards.

Eighteen carries. One hundred fifty-seven yards. That’s nearly nine yards per carry, Bills Mafia.

And Josh repeated what he’s been saying all season: the more touches they can get Cook, the better they’re going to be.

And with that 74-yard touchdown, Cook has now scored in eight straight games. He and Josh Allen just became the first teammates in NFL history to both reach 5,000 career rushing yards. And as Joe Brady said after the Raiders game earlier this season, when James Cook holds the football, he holds the whole organization.

Then a reporter said it feels like this team keeps finding a different way to win every week, a little bit of everything.

Josh agreed. But he said the team can’t control how other teams play. It can only control how it plays and how it executes. And then he said this: there’s still so much to learn from and grow from, and we’re not playing our best football right now.

He said that as they get later into the season, they believe they’ll keep getting better, learning from games like this, and hopefully start putting it all together soon.

Bills Mafia, eight and one, and the quarterback says the team isn’t playing its best football yet. That’s the same message Joe Brady has been preaching all season. Brady said this team’s best football has to be ahead of it. He said you never want to peak in September. And Brandon Beane said the key is playing your best football in January.

When the best team in the AFC says it hasn’t played its best yet, the rest of the league should be nervous.

Now, the defense.

A reporter asked Josh how important it was that the defense held the Jets to field goals instead of touchdowns.

Josh called it fantastic stuff. He said the defense is playing really good right now. He said they got their hands on some balls, and that there was a fumble recovery late in the game. And he said forcing turnovers gives the offense more advantages and more opportunities.

Bills Mafia, think about what this defense has done in the last three games. Against Baltimore, 10 points allowed. Against Minnesota, six points allowed, and zero third-down conversions for the Vikings. And now against the Jets, holding them to field goals in the red zone and forcing turnovers.

Remember, a few weeks ago this defense ranked near the bottom of the league in points allowed per game. Jim Leonhard said the defense wasn’t doing its most important job: keeping points off the board. And now? It’s one of the hottest defenses in football.

And this was the first game with Amani Hooker in the building, the NFL’s interception leader, whom Brandon Beane traded a second-round pick to acquire.

Before we keep going, smash that like button if you think this defense is the real deal. Let’s hear it, Bills Mafia.

But Josh made sure to point out the other side. He said that offensively, they can’t turn the ball over.

And then a reporter asked whether he’s getting tired of the pre-snap penalties, because there were a bunch of them in this game.

Josh admitted there were a few. He said they have to clean them up, and that it will be a point of emphasis this week.

That’s a theme Joe Brady has hammered all season. Brady said he has no time for pre-snap penalties. He said those are unacceptable, because they’re about controlling what you can control. He said he doesn’t want to lead the league in fewest penalties, because he wants his team to play with an edge, but pre-snap mistakes have to go.

So that’s the to-do list for this week: protect the football, and clean up the pre-snap penalties.

Then a reporter asked where this game ranks among Buffalo’s most complete performances this season, with the defensive turnovers and the big plays on offense.

And Josh’s answer was striking. He said, honestly, he thinks the offense didn’t play that well. He acknowledged that you can look at the stat sheet and see 31 points. But he said the first half didn’t feel like they executed the way they wanted to.

So again, he said, there’s a lot to learn from and a lot to grow from, and they’re going to keep getting better.

Bills Mafia, 31 points on the road, and Josh is saying the offense didn’t play well. That’s a quarterback with an incredibly high standard. And that’s exactly what you want from your leader.

Next, the run game. A reporter noted that, over the last two games, there’s been a big emphasis on running the football.

Josh’s answer was perfect. He said: yeah, we got James Cook. Go run. And then he added, heck yeah.

He was also asked what the team saw during the week that made them emphasize the side-to-side running plays early in the game. And Josh credited the coaching staff. He said it came from film work by the coaches, and getting the run game going with offensive line coach Pat. And he said the protection plan was awesome too, crediting assistant offensive line coach Austin Gunn.

He said it’s about trusting the staff to find plays that will work, and then going out and executing them. And he thought they did that tonight.

Bills Mafia, remember what Joe Brady said about this offensive line group? He said Pat is doing an amazing job, and that Austin Gunn spends so much time with the players, staying after every practice. Just a week earlier, this line held up against the most aggressive blitz Next Gen Stats has ever tracked, without Dion Dawkins and Alec Anderson. And now it helped pave the way for 157 rushing yards from James Cook alone.

And finally, the fans.

A reporter asked how great it was to have the Bills crowd travel to New York and basically turn it into a home game.

Josh said it was cool. He said they didn’t have to use the silent count much. They still used it on the occasional third down when it got a little louder than expected. But he said when he looked up into the stands, there was a lot of red, white, and blue.

And then he said: you’ve got the best fans in the world.

Bills Mafia, that’s you. Traveling to MetLife Stadium and taking it over. Josh Allen noticed. The whole team noticed.

So let’s put it all together.

The Bills put up 31 points against the Jets and improved to eight and one.

DJ Moore was back involved, and Josh called him a threat to score from anywhere.

The second half was explosive: a 50-plus-yard catch by Dalton Kincaid on the first play, a touchdown a couple of plays later, a big pass to DJ Moore, and a 74-yard touchdown run from James Cook.

Cook finished with 18 carries for 157 yards. Josh said it was the fastest he’s ever seen Cook run, and that the long run was a product of all the tough runs that came before it.

The defense held the Jets to field goals, got hands on the ball, and recovered a fumble late.

But Josh said the offense didn’t play that well, especially in the first half. He said the turnovers and pre-snap penalties have to be cleaned up. And he said the team isn’t playing its best football yet.

And he called Bills Mafia the best fans in the world.

Now Buffalo looks ahead to a six-game stretch that could decide the AFC’s number one seed. That includes the Dolphins, and then the Kansas City Chiefs on Thanksgiving night in Buffalo. Even Nick Wright, one of the biggest Chiefs fans in media, said he expects the Chiefs to get their teeth kicked in on Thanksgiving night.

The Bills have never had the number one seed in the Josh Allen era. They’ve never had the first-round bye. They’ve never hosted the AFC Championship Game with Josh at quarterback. And right now, at eight and one, they’re in the driver’s seat.
