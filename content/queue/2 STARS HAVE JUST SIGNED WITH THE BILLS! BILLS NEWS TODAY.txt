Bills Mafia, let’s be honest with each other. You know the situation with this defense right now. The communication isn’t where it needs to be. The coverage has been way too passive. And because of that, the guys up front aren’t getting a real chance to take over games. With the trade deadline coming on November 10th, most people expect Buffalo to do the usual thing: go out and grab another pass rusher.

But I don’t think that’s where this should go.

With only around four million dollars in cap space, there’s a very specific plan that makes a lot more sense, and it’s built around two spots in the middle of this defense. Today I’m going to break down exactly who I’m talking about, the two stars I’d go get, and how this could all come together.

Let’s get into it.

First, let’s talk about why I keep landing on the middle of this defense, and it comes down to how the Bills actually play.

Buffalo runs a lot of zone: quarters, Cover 3. And they spend a ton of time in sub packages, including dime looks. That means more defensive backs and more coverage players on the field. And that means there are a lot of chances to get better players onto the field in the exact spots where this defense is struggling.

Safety and linebacker. Those are the two positions that jump out to me. Those are the two spots I most want to see Buffalo upgrade before the deadline.

And it lines up exactly with what Jim Leonhard has been telling us. He said the problem is significantly more on the back seven than on the front. He said the pass rush is getting a ton of pressure and hitting the quarterback, but the coverage is too often confused. He said communication is the biggest thing they have to fix. He even said he doesn’t think there are enough options in the scheme for the defense to be that confused.

So what do you do when communication is the problem? You go get players who are great communicators. Players who can line everybody up, play fast, and make plays. Field generals.

Let’s start at safety.

And I have to start with C.J. Gardner-Johnson. Bills Mafia, I really hope the Bills aren’t counting on him to stabilize this secondary. Between his performance on the field and everything around him, including the ejection in Las Vegas and the one-game suspension that followed, this hasn’t been the steady presence this defense needs. Joe Brady said the team brought him here for a reason, and I respect that. But if I’m in that front office, I’m looking at the safety spot and saying: we need an upgrade. We can’t be depending on hope here.

So if an upgrade is the goal, here are the candidates.

First, one of my favorites: Budda Baker from the Arizona Cardinals.

Baker is 30, and Buffalo would be taking on just under 10 million dollars right now, which is a healthy number. But he’s a physical presence, a tone setter, and versatile enough to play anywhere in the secondary.

What really stands out is his connection to Buffalo’s defensive staff. Baker played under Vance Joseph, and Jim Leonhard worked under Vance Joseph for the last few years in Denver. That matters a lot right now. Buffalo already has communication problems, and the last thing you want is to add a new piece who doesn’t speak the language and makes those problems worse. With Baker, there should be some familiarity with the terminology Leonhard uses.

The concerns? He turns 31 in January, and that’s a lot of cap to absorb. But he’s an obvious upgrade. And I’m sure he’d love a shot at winning, since Arizona has finished above .500 only once since he entered the league in 2017.

Next, Derwin James from the Los Angeles Chargers.

James is a different conversation. He’s 30, Buffalo would take on just under 8 million dollars, and he’s in the first year of a three-year, 75-million-dollar deal. That’s a real commitment.

The appeal is obvious. He’s one of the best safeties in football, a true field general, and extremely versatile. But the age, the cost, and the injuries give me pause. He’s dealt with a fractured pinky and a hamstring injury that has kept him out of the last three games. I wouldn’t be mad if Buffalo got him, but he’s the riskiest name on this list, and he’s not my favorite idea.

Honestly, I might be more interested in another Chargers safety: Elijah Molden.

Buffalo would take on about 4 million dollars. Molden is a converted slot player, a good coverage defender who can tackle, and he’s younger and cheaper. Given Buffalo’s cap situation, he might be the most responsible move on the board.

Then there’s Amani Hooker from the Tennessee Titans, and this is another one of my favorites.

Hooker is 28, and Buffalo would take on about 6 million dollars. He’s a legit coverage player with really good ball skills, and he tackles well. And here’s an interesting detail: he’s played under five different defensive coordinators in his career. He’s had to learn a lot of new systems, and I think that would help him adjust quickly in Buffalo. Again, communication.

The question is whether Tennessee is willing to move him. The Titans are one and six, and if they lose Sunday they’ll be one and seven, in the first year of the Robert Saleh era. Hooker wasn’t brought in by this general manager or this coach. And at 28, he’s not a rental. He’s a starter for the next couple of seasons.

Next, another favorite: Camryn Bynum from the Indianapolis Colts.

Bynum is 28, and Buffalo would take on about 7 million dollars. He’s gotten better and better throughout his career. Good size, a very good tackler, very good ball skills, and I believe he can handle those deep post responsibilities. Honestly, I don’t have any concerns with him.

And the Colts might be motivated sellers. They just fired their general manager, and their first-round pick belongs to the Jets. They should be looking to add draft picks. I think Bynum makes a ton of sense.

And then there’s the big name: Jessie Bates III from the Atlanta Falcons.

Bates is 29, Buffalo would take on about 8.6 million dollars, and he’s not signed beyond this season. So you could be paying a decent price for a rental.

But Bills Mafia, he’s a premier safety. A true ballhawk who also tackles, and an ideal deep post player whether you’re in Cover 3, Cover 1, or quarters. And here’s a big bonus: adding Bates would let Cole Bishop play lower in the defense, closer to the line, which is where I think Bishop is at his best. Remember, Bishop just had seven tackles and two pass breakups against the Raiders. Put him in his best spot, put Bates behind him, and suddenly the whole secondary looks different.

The concern is that keeping Bates long-term would mean a huge contract. And I’m not sure Atlanta should see itself as out of the race in the NFC South. But Bates wasn’t brought in by the current regime, so if they’re not planning to keep him, a fourth-round pick might get it done.

So here’s where I land at safety. If Buffalo came away with Jessie Bates, Camryn Bynum, Amani Hooker, or Budda Baker, I’d love it. Derwin James is the riskiest, and I’m not sure that’s where I’d tie up resources.

Because as much as I like those safeties, there’s a linebacker I’d put ahead of every single one of them. If I could pick one player for the Bills to trade for, it’s Zack Baun from the Philadelphia Eagles.

Let me explain why.

I do think the Bills are somewhat satisfied with Terrel Bernard and Dorian Williams. And Dorian Williams just made his first career interception against the Raiders, a huge play that set up a touchdown. But I think this spot is extremely upgradeable. And the bar can’t be just an okay starter, because Buffalo already has that kind of depth, with Shaq Thompson waiting on the practice squad.

What this team desperately needs is a true field general. A high-end communicator who gets everyone lined up, makes plays, and brings a physical presence.

That’s Zack Baun.

He’s 29, turns 30 in December, and his contract is easy to take on. Since he arrived in Philadelphia in 2024, he’s been one of the three best off-ball linebackers in the league.

And here’s the connection that makes this so perfect: Baun played under Jim Leonhard at Wisconsin.

Think about that, Bills Mafia. Leonhard is trying to fix communication. And here’s a player who already knows how he thinks, how he teaches, and how he calls a defense. On top of that, Baun has been playing in Vic Fangio’s system in Philadelphia, which is complex and full of zone-match coverages. After that, he could step into Buffalo’s defense easily.

He can play in space. He can cover. He can play downhill against the run. He can rush the passer. I don’t have any concerns here. This is the best chance Buffalo has to land a versatile field general.

And the cost? Maybe a third-round pick. If that’s the price, I’m sending it. Brandon Beane, go get Zack Baun.

So there are my two stars, Bills Mafia: Zack Baun at linebacker and a starting-caliber safety like Jessie Bates or Camryn Bynum. Two players in the middle of the defense who can fix the communication, tighten up the coverage, and finally let this pass rush take over games.

Now, the other linebacker name I keep seeing is Frankie Luvu from the Washington Commanders.

It makes sense on paper. Washington looks like it’s headed nowhere this season, and there are questions about the coaching situation there. Luvu is 30, Buffalo would take on about 5.3 million dollars, and he can do a bit of everything, especially as a blitzer.

But to me, he’s a high-variance player, and I don’t think he fixes Buffalo’s biggest problems. Even if it’s just a sixth-round pick to take on his salary, I don’t feel like that’s the right way to spend minimal cap space.

And that’s the same reason cornerback doesn’t excite me as much. I’d rather invest in a linebacker or safety. I could be convinced by a clear upgrade over what Buffalo has with Tre’Davious White and Maxwell Hairston. And there’s one interesting name: Tariq Woolen from the Eagles.

Woolen is 27, Buffalo would take on about 8.4 million dollars, he’s around six-foot-four with crazy speed, and he’s a good coverage player. But a lot of teams will want him, and he signed a one-year deal, so for Buffalo he’d probably be a rental. Do you give up a third-round pick for that? If you got a safety for a fifth and then sent a third for Woolen, I wouldn’t be mad. But he’s not my top priority.

Another corner name out there is Dane Jackson from the Chargers. He’s a veteran with speed, but there are tackling issues, and he’s been passed over a lot since leaving Carolina. I’m not doing cap gymnastics for him.

Now you might be asking: why am I staying away from the pass rush?

Because I’m really happy with this defensive line, Bills Mafia.

Greg Rousseau and Deone Walker have been the shining stars. DaWuane Carter looks good. Greg Gaines looks good. T.J. Sanders has popped. T.J. Parker is getting better every week. Michael Hoecht will keep settling in. Bradley Chubb, I’m still waiting to see. And I really hope Ed Oliver comes back healthy.

The problem isn’t the rush. The problem is that the coverage isn’t complementing the rush. Passive coverages, no disguise, nobody pressing receivers, plus all those communication breakdowns.

And that matches exactly what Jim Leonhard said. He pointed out that in the first ten plays of the second half against the Raiders, Las Vegas threw seven times and the Bills hit the quarterback five times. That’s a pass rush doing its job. The ball was just getting out too fast, and the coverage wasn’t tight enough to make Kirk Cousins hold it. Leonhard also admitted that when they got more aggressive and matchy in coverage, it led to less disguise and gave the offense more rhythm.

So the math is simple. Once Buffalo gets stickier in coverage, that pass rush is going to take over. And the way you get stickier in coverage is by adding smarter, better players in the middle of the defense.

Still, there are two pass rushers worth mentioning.

Khalil Mack from the Chargers is 35, and Buffalo would take on about 5.3 million dollars. He’s not the vintage Khalil Mack anymore, but he can still help. And honestly, it would feel on brand for this team, alongside veterans like Leonard Floyd, Von Miller, Bradley Chubb, and Joey Bosa.

And then there’s DeForest Buckner from the Colts, an interior lineman. He’s 32, with about 8.7 million dollars left. He’s an awesome player, but interior pass rush is far from Buffalo’s biggest need right now.

Now let’s talk about the cap, because it’s the biggest obstacle.

Buffalo has only around four million dollars in cap space. Look at the numbers we’ve talked about. Baker is just under 10 million. James is just under 8. Bates is about 8.6. Bynum is about 7. Hooker is about 6. Molden is about 4.

That means most of these moves would require some creativity from Brandon Beane. Restructures, salary going back the other way in a trade, or the other team keeping part of the salary. That’s why every dollar matters, and why I’d rather spend those dollars in the middle of the defense than on another edge rusher.

And this is where the earlier trade conversation comes in. We’ve talked about the possibility of Buffalo moving one of its running backs, like Ty Johnson or Ray Davis, before the deadline. A move like that could free up a little room and a roster spot. It’s just one idea, but it shows how Beane might create the flexibility to go get a real difference-maker.

So let me put my plan on the table.

Step one: trade for Zack Baun. He’s my number one target. A field general who already knows Jim Leonhard, can do everything, and would cost around a third-round pick.

Step two: add a starting safety. My top choices are Jessie Bates, Camryn Bynum, or Amani Hooker, with Budda Baker as a strong option too. Bates would cost more but would let Cole Bishop play his best role. Bynum and Hooker would be longer-term solutions.

Step three: leave the pass rush alone. It’s already good. Let it take over once the coverage gets tighter.

Bills Mafia, if Brandon Beane pulls off even two of these moves, I think this defense could look completely different by December. And with Josh Allen and this offense already putting up 30 points a game, a defense that can actually finish games could be the difference between another playoff exit and a trip to the Super Bowl.

Because think about it. Joe Brady said the team’s best football is still ahead. Jim Leonhard said there are changes coming that they couldn’t install until after the bye. Dan Orlovsky said this offense is about as close to impossible to defend as it gets. The road to the number one seed is wide open with the Chiefs losing. All this team needs is a defense that can close the door.

And two stars in the middle of that defense could be exactly what makes it happen.
